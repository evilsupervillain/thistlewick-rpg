# Characters from further out

People for places *The Obligatory Quest* has not built yet: towns and cities
past the edges of the current map. Nothing here is in the game. Each character
has a bio written to the house rules (funny, trope-heavy, embarrassment and
never resentment) and a finished look.

Their art is generated, not drawn: `tools/chargen` in the workspace renders the
Character Generator's four sheets from a settings file the editor can also
load. Each character's settings and sheets (face, walking, damaged, battler)
are in `../char-generator/characters/<name>/`, and `lineup.png` there shows
them side by side. See `../CHARGEN.md`.

## Gideon Markup - proprietor, Ye Final Shoppe, Penultima

*Penultima: The Last Town Before The End. Last Chance For Potions.*

Every road to a Dark Lord needs one last town with better swords in it than any
town before, and Penultima is that town, and knows it. Gideon's window holds the
**Sword of Ultimate Ending**, priced at 9,999,999 gold - which is one gold more
than a purse can hold, a fact he did not know when he priced it and has
declined to act on for thirty-one years. He polishes it every morning anyway.
A shop is only as good as its window.

He buys anything - a boot, a slime, half a potion - for exactly one gold, and
sells it back for forty. His prices double whenever a Chosen One is sighted on
the road, which he calls "seasonal". Every customer is greeted with "Welcome!
What are you buying?" and every departing one is told "Come again!", including
a burglar in the spring of '42, who did.

His wife Dorothy keeps the ledger and keeps him honest, mostly. They met when
she tried to haggle and he gave a discount, for the first and only time in his
life, and he has never once regretted the eleven coppers.

> "Ah, the Sword of Ultimate Ending. Would sir like to hold it? Sir cannot
> afford to hold it."

**Look:** waistcoat and shirtsleeves, gold wire spectacles, a waxed moustache,
and eyes permanently crinkled shut in commercial delight.

## Princess Rosamund of Fairholme-on-Ransom

Kidnapped eleven times - twelve if you count the eagle, which the Palace does
not. She keeps a packed bag by the tower window: a book, a cardigan,
sandwiches, and a card listing her dietary requirements "for whoever it is this
time".

Her father, King Aldous, posts the traditional reward on every occasion - *her
hand, or half the kingdom* - and every rescuer so far has very sensibly taken
the half. Fairholme is therefore held in shares by nine heroes, a knight, two
farmers and a man who was only passing, and the King's council meetings now take
all day and need a larger table.

She wears a travelling cloak over the ball gown, because she has learned.

The dragon on the hill, Mrs Emberthwaite, is retired, widowed and mostly wants
someone to play cards with. Rosamund has started going round on Thursdays
without being carried, and the rescue economy of three kingdoms is in uproar.
The Heroes' Guild has written to complain.

> "Oh, are you the rescue? Lovely. Could you carry the cake tin? Mind the
> seventh stair, it's a trap. The eighth is also a trap. The ninth is fine."

**Look:** pink court gown, a gold tiara with a ruby, ringlets, and a sensible
green travelling cloak nobody gave her.

## Toby Wendle, aged ten - Official Welcomer, Gladhollow

The Gladhollow Town Council pays Toby a copper a day to stand by the gate and
say **"Welcome to Gladhollow!"** and nothing else, per his job description,
which he has read nine times. He has fourteen ways of saying it and is working
on a fifteenth, the questioning one.

Travellers walk straight into him about forty times a day, because heroes do
not look where they are going. The saucepan is his mum's, on loan "for the
insurance", and the plaster is from a paladin.

He knows everything about the town - the passage behind the mayor's bookcase,
where the well key is, which barrels have a potion in them (all of them are his
granny's barrels, and she would like her potions back) - and is not allowed to
tell you any of it, because it would be a second sentence. He is saving up for a
promotion to Farewell Boy at the east gate, who gets *two*.

> "Welcome to Gladhollow!"
> (asked a question) "...Welcome to Gladhollow."
> (after the quest, bursting) "Welcome BACK to Glad- I'm not allowed that one."

**Look:** shirt and braces, a tuft of hair that escapes the saucepan, a plaster
on one cheek, and enormous, eager eyes.

## Mirabel of Olde Evermore, aged 4,112 (eight and a quarter)

Last Oracle of Olde Evermore, the hidden elven village that every party finds
eventually, by falling off the same ledge. Mirabel has foreseen the fall of
empires and all forty-eight risings of the Dark Lord, and has to be in bed by
half past seven.

Her prophecies are in the Old Tongue, flawless and terrible, and delivered from
a stepstool. The Elders - every one of them visibly older than she looks and
four thousand years younger than she is - call her Grandmother, and are sent to
fetch things down from the top shelf of the Forbidden Archive.

She was there when the Prophecy of Thistlewick was signed, and the doodle in its
margin is hers. She knows who the villain really is and will tell you, but only
as a drawing, and the drawing is a horse.

> "When the moon devours the sun and the last bell falls silent, the one who was
> not chosen shall... is that a biscuit?"

**Look:** long silver hair with a blunt fringe, a silver circlet set with a blue
stone, pointed ears, a purple and gold robe, and the half-lidded eyes of someone
who has seen all of this before, because she has.

## Brother Quillon - Keeper of the Record, Chapel of Continuance, Hithermost

The Chapel stands at the top of the pass above Hithermost, beside the only door
into the mountain, and in it is a lectern, and on the lectern is the Book, which
glows. Travellers write their names in it "in case of misfortune". Then they go
through the door. Then, quite often, they come back in through the chapel porch,
write their names again, and give Brother Quillon a look he has spent forty
years learning not to remark on.

Sir Dunstable of the Vale has come in at a quarter past four two hundred and
twelve times. He always says "Just in case." He always goes through the door. On
the two hundred and thirteenth visit, he always explains, with some warmth, that
nothing happened. Brother Quillon now leaves a cup of tea on the lectern for him
at ten past.

The Order's vows forbid exactly one thing: saying that he remembers. He has
therefore developed the most expressive silence in the Church. His theology is
simple - every soul deserves a second chance - and he adds, only to the Abbot,
that some deserve two hundred and twelve.

> "Welcome, traveller. Will you write your name in the Book?
> ...Again? Of course. Of course you will."

**Look:** bald as an egg, a long white beard, an ochre habit, and eyes squeezed
shut in the serene smile of a man not reacting to anything.

## Mrs Hester Ashbury - landlady, The Eight Hours, Restwell

Restwell is a crossroads town of one street and nineteen inns, and the Eight
Hours is the one the songs are about. A night in its beds restores all strength
and all magic, cures poison, blindness, curses and frogs, and on one occasion
cured death, after a party carried their friend upstairs and asked for a single.

Nobody has ever been seen asleep in them. Guests go up at noon, come straight
back down, fully restored, and insist it is morning. Hester stopped arguing
about this in her first year and now serves breakfast at all hours. The town
doctor has no patients left and has taken a room himself, for the rest.

Her husband Walt plays a little tune on the fiddle at the foot of the stairs
every time somebody goes up, because he likes to, and because he is privately
convinced that it is the tune that does it. After twenty-six years, Hester is
no longer sure that it isn't, and she would not dream of asking him to stop.

> "Ten gold a night, love, breakfast included. Resurrection's extra, and I'll
> want the room back by noon."

**Look:** a white lace bonnet, brown hair in a bun, a cream and orange dress
with an apron's worth of lace, and the face of a woman who has heard every
excuse for a late checkout.

## Pellam Quickfoot - travelling merchant, slightly ahead of you

Pellam is from nowhere in particular and is always somewhere you have only
just arrived. His cart is set up in the deepest room of every dungeon, on the
ledge outside the boss's door, and at the top of the tower you have just spent
an afternoon climbing. He has never been seen arriving. Asked how he got past
the lava, he says "the stairs". There are no stairs.

His stock is exactly what you needed two rooms ago. His prices rise the deeper
you go, which he calls carriage. He will buy back the loot from the monster you
have just defeated, for rather less than the monster paid him for it, and he is
unfailingly polite to monsters, who are also customers.

He offers a loyalty card with ten paws on it. Nobody has ever filled one, because
the tenth dungeon is always the last one. He and Gideon Markup of Penultima are
not rivals, both of them say, in the way that two cats sharing one windowsill
are not rivals.

> "Ah, there you are. Took the long way? Everybody takes the long way. What are
> you buying?"

**Look:** ginger cat ears and a long ginger tail, a green traveller's coat with a
fur collar, and a lopsided smile that knows where the secret door is.

## Fizz - Tutorial Sprite, Third Class, Firstfield Meadow

Every Chosen One's journey begins in Firstfield, and every one of them has been
assigned Fizz. The Guild of Guides requires her to explain everything once:
walking, talking, that a chest is for opening, and that the thing with a handle
in the wall is a door. She has explained doors three thousand times, on several
occasions to someone already holding one open.

She is not permitted to tell you anything useful. The Dark Lord's weakness, the
location of the Sacred Key and what happens if you pull the lever are all
"lore", and the Guild does not cover lore. She appears directly in front of your
face whenever you try to read a sign, to tell you that it is a sign.

She is working towards Boss Warning Sprite, First Class, who waits outside the
final door and gets to shout "It's weak to fire!" She practises in the meadow,
at the sheep.

> "Hey! Hey! Listen! This is a tree! Trees are for looking at! Press the button
> to look at it! ...You're doing SO well."

**Look:** pink hair in a ribboned ponytail with a feather clip, pointed ears,
a teal tunic, and a pair of translucent wings that never, ever stop.
