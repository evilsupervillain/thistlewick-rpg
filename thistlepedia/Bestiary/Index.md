## Preface

This is a bestiary of the country between Nether Sopping and the Obligatory Tower, and from Upper Clanging to the bottom of the Stair under Hithermost, as it was in the year 2000, written up in the winter of 2001 and corrected in January 2002.

Everybody else names monsters after how they died. I name them after what they are. It is the same job done properly. Every entry here is filed under what the animal is, and gives after it what people call it, and the people are generally adventurers, and the names are generally the circumstances. The Crow of Foreshadowing is a crow. The Thing at the Bottom is a badger. The Slain Wyvern is a goose. I have not been rude to anybody's name. I have only moved it from the heading to the line underneath, which is where a name for how something died belongs.

Where Doctor Amias Rowbotham, FRS, has named an animal, his name is given as a synonym, and is not to be used. He named some three hundred species after himself, his subscribers, his wife and his dogs, and never had a type specimen for one of them. The eleven I have replaced are in <a href="The_Fauna_of_the_Valley">The Fauna of the Valley</a>. The other two hundred and eighty-nine are for volume two.

Nobody may say, anywhere in this Bestiary, that anything in it is *basically a dragon*. Nothing in it is basically a dragon. I have drafted a clause.

## Method

A cataloguer does not hunt animals. She documents them. A fight is a step in the methodology which I resent, because you cannot measure a wing that is actively in use, but it is sometimes the only step that will get an animal to hold still, and so where there was a fight each entry has a line, **Method**, saying what it was. I have done what I could at range. I take armour off things, because the armour is not the animal. I collect two of whatever an animal drops, because one is an anecdote.

A type specimen is measured, not killed. Every type specimen in this Bestiary was let go afterwards. Every one of them minded being measured more than it would have minded being fought.

I describe only what I have seen, or what has been described to me by a witness I would trust with a ruler. Where I have not seen a thing, I say so, and list it, and do not describe it.

<div class="marginalia">The plates are mine, not Miss Halloway's. They were drawn for me from the accounts of the adventurers who met these animals, which is to say as each one looked while somebody was hitting it, and one or two by people who had plainly never seen a crab. Miss Halloway agreed to them on two conditions: that every caption gives what the thing is called, and not what it is, that being where a name for how something died belongs; and that wherever a plate is wrong, the caption says so. Several captions say so. - H.B.</div>

## Not animals

Listed because they are fought, and people will look for them; not described as animals, because they are not. The **marsh light** (the Wisp of Mild Concern), **masonry** (the Load-Bearing Gargoyle), an **urn** (Lost Property), a **suit of armour** (Grave Goods, Animated), the Hoyle Works' **attempts and steam** (Loose Pressure; Attempt, Unnumbered; Ambulant Salvage; Attempt Eighty-Four), a chapel's **damp**, the **small hours**, a **small temptation** and a **light going away**, and the Stair's **dark** and **echo**. Each is on the page for where it is met.

**THE PROPHECY.** A document. Not fauna. It fought, at the top of the Obligatory Tower, when clause twelve enforced itself. So does a great deal of paperwork. See <a href="../History/The_Prophecy_of_Thistlewick">The Prophecy of Thistlewick</a>, in another hand.

## Persons

The following appear in adventurers' accounts as monsters, and are persons. I do not catalogue persons, because a person is described by a biographer, not a cataloguer, and because they read it. A goblin, who had a list of grievances and read from it. A bandit in the fourth week of a six-week apprenticeship, and the bandit chief who runs it, <a href="../People/Meredith_Crooke">Meredith Crooke</a>, who has a syllabus. A skeleton, interning. The previous occupant of a barrow. A warden of a half-way landing. A man contracted to lose at Firstfield. A mother at Upper Clanging, who was right. Three entrants at the Bell, and the regulars at the back table. The Forty-Fourth. Grimspite. For all of them, see the People volume, or nobody; most would prefer nobody.

## Not seen

**Something in the sand**, off the west beach at Nether Sopping. **Something in the crack**, in the north wall of the Bottomless Pit, which is asleep, and gravid, and I know what it is, and I am not saying. **Somebody coming up**, on the Stair, whom nobody could see. I do not catalogue what I could not see, and I do not guess who.

## Withdrawn

One entry, on a subject living near Fairholme, was withdrawn at the request of the subject, over a hand of whist. A cataloguer honours a withdrawal. I will say only that the subject was very gracious about it, and that I lost the hand.
