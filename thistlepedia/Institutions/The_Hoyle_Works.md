# The Hoyle Works

<p class="contributor">Contributed by O. Hoyle, of the Hoyle Works, Upper Clanging</p>

<p class="hatnote">I sent Miss Hoyle a list of questions about the works, having learned my lesson over the Long Field. She sent back the list, with every question answered in one word, and this memorandum attached, which answers them properly, and which I print whole. For the town, see <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>. For the field where the works keeps its attempts, see <a href="../Places/The_Long_Field">The Long Field</a>. THE HOYLE WORKS. MEMORANDUM.

TO: Mr H. Bellwether, Nether Sopping. FROM: O. Hoyle. RE: Your questions, "The Hoyle Works", nineteen of them. DATE: 18 March 2002.

1. Your questions are returned, answered. One word was enough for each of them and I have given you one word, and since you will want more I have written it out below. Where you asked the same thing twice I have answered it once. That is how a works runs.

2. **What the works is.** The Hoyle Works builds airships to reach the Obligatory Tower. That is what it was started for and it is what it is for. It does other jobs, for the town and the valley and anybody who pays, because the town needs the work and a shed needs a roof. But the jobs are jobs. The airships are the work.

3. **Who started it.** My great-grandmother, about 1800, to stop the Dark Lord. The parish register has the earliest entry I can find: AT THE WORKS (THINKING), 1802. Somebody has pencilled HOYLE beside it and rubbed it out, because the register does not take marginal notes. I did not do that. I would like that recorded, since you are recording things.

3.1. My grandmother kept it on because the town needed the work. My mother kept it on out of spite. I am not criticising her. Spite is a perfectly good reason. It built most of this shed.

3.2. I keep it on because I like it. You asked whether that was the reason. It is the reason. Two hundred years, and we have got to "I like it", and I think that is fine.

4. **The premises.** The shed, the drawing office, the stores, the slip, and the yard. The plate beside the door says DANGER. HOT. LOUD. EARLY. All four are true and the plate is checked monthly.

4.1. There is a line on the floor of the shed. It is not decorative. Everything past it is hot, sharp, or under pressure, and three of them are all three. Visitors stand on the NO side. So does my apprentice. She always has.

4.2. There is a tank marked COAL OIL, and under it, chalked, NO WIZARDS WITHIN TEN YARDS. The chalk is not mine and I have not removed it. Miss Vance has asked me to. The answer was no. She asked why, and I told her it was a coal oil tank. She said that was fair.

4.3. The hooter sounds at six. It sounds at six on Sunday as well. The parish has asked about this, and the parish has considered it.

5. **The records.** The works keeps four. Visitors read one of them and should read the others.

5.1. *The attempt log.* One line for each attempt: the number, where it was lost, and the cause. Everybody reads the log. The log is only where it stopped.

5.2. *The drawings.* Every attempt, drawn, before and after. Nobody reads the drawings. The drawings are where the work is.

5.3. *The drawing office day book.* What was done each day and by whom. It has one underlining in it in my hand. You know which. I am not going to discuss it.

5.4. *The stores book.* A ledger, ruled in columns, kept since 1802. Everything ever issued from the stores has a line through it when it comes back. It has been kept honestly for two hundred years and it is the only document in this works that has never once been under review.

5.4.1. You asked about ITEM 1. ITEM 1 is a line in the stores book, entered 1802, one off. The description column says ITEM 1. Four generations of this works have not improved on that description and I am not going to try. What became of it is in the stores book. The stores book is not open to the public. That is not a secret. It is a stores book.

6. **The attempts.** Two hundred. One hundred and ninety-seven came home to the Long Field, and the Long Field has its own memorandum, which you have, and which you printed with my corrections, for which thank you. <a href="../People/Eighty-Four">Eighty-Four</a> is one of the hundred and ninety-seven and is in her bay now. Two did not come home, 112 and 199, and I have accounted for both of them to you separately and will not do it twice. The two hundredth is not in the field and is not going to be.

6.1. The last four entries in the log:

<div class="stamp">197. LOST OVER THE LONG FIELD. CAUSE: ENVELOPE, SEAM.<br>
198. LOST OVER THE LONG FIELD. CAUSE: ENVELOPE, THE OTHER SEAM.<br>
199. LOST IN SIGHT OF THE TOWER. CAUSE: UNDER REVIEW.<br>
200. REACHED THE TOWER. LANDED ADJACENT. CAUSE: UNDER REVIEW.</div>

6.2. Entry 199 was made in 1989. Entry 200 was made in 2000 in a fresh hand at the bottom of a page that had already said UNDER REVIEW once. You have asked me what the review has found. It has not found anything yet. That is what UNDER REVIEW means. When the review finds something, the log will say so, and not before. I do not write down anything I have not checked.

7. **The Two Hundred.** She wanted forty bolts of oilskin and a spar that would hold. The oilskin came from Mrs Barrow, <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a>, who would not have forty and was made to find forty. The spar came from Ollerenshaw's, bottom of the hill, and it held, and it was made in an afternoon, by two smiths, which I had been saying for some years that it wanted. The tube plate for her boiler was made by four hands as well. I mention this because you asked who else worked on her, and the answer is most of the town, and two smiths in particular, and a lad from <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a> who carried things.

7.1. She was handed over with one instruction: do not set her down on anything with a roof on it. You would be astonished how often that has been the finding. She was not set down on anything with a roof on it. She landed on the grass beside the Tower, two squares from the door. The party walked the two squares.

8. **The staff.** If you read the parish register at OCCUPATION you will find that it says AT THE WORKS on every line for eighty years. That is the staff. I am not going to give you a list. You would put them in alphabetical order, and half of them would be under C.

8.1. My apprentice is the works' fortieth since 1804. She has asked me nine times whether we are going to manage it, and been told nine times that it is not a question with an answer. It is a question with a schedule. She has stopped asking since 2000. I asked her why. She said that we had managed it. I told her that we had landed adjacent. She said that was managing it. We are not agreed. It is on the schedule.

9. **The town.** You asked what the town thinks of the works. The town has turned the ornaments in its front rooms round to face the works. That is what the town thinks. You also asked what <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a> thinks, and I have been told that Great Dunnage would like it known that this is only one works. That is correct. It is one works. It has built two hundred airships. I do not know how many Great Dunnage has built, and I am not going to write and ask.

10. **The Two Hundred and First.** Her keel was laid in 2001. She is to land on the Tower. Not adjacent. On. You asked what she was for, and I have told you, and I would ask you not to put it in, and I expect you will. She is on the slip. She is behind.

10.1. Since you will ask what she is behind: schedule. Everything in this works is behind schedule. That is what a schedule is for. A works that is ahead of its schedule has written the wrong schedule.

11. **Visitors.** The works does not give tours. People come up anyway, since 2000, to look at the slip. They are shown the line on the floor, and asked to stand behind it, and they do. <a href="../People/Mr_Kell">Mr Kell</a> at the Safety Valve sells them a cup of tea after. I am told it is very good for trade.

12. That is all your questions. You asked one more, at the bottom, in pencil, which was whether the works will stop now that there is no Dark Lord to stop. I have answered it in one word on your list, and I will give you the same word here, since it will not take long. No.

O. HOYLE. CHIEF ENGINEER.

<div class="marginalia">I had not, in fact, asked about the Dark Lord in pencil. It was in ink. Miss Hoyle has corrected me on this point as well. - H.B.</div>
