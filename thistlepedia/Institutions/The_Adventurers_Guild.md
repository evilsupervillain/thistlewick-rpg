<p class="lede">The <b>Adventurers' Guild (Provisional)</b> registers adventurers in the district of <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a>, posts their bounties, and keeps their monuments. It was founded in 1960, provisionally, pending a review. The review is scheduled. It has been scheduled for forty-two years.</p>

## The Charter

On a stone tablet by the door:

<div class="stamp">THE ADVENTURERS' GUILD (PROVISIONAL). Founded forty years ago, provisionally, pending a review. The review is scheduled. The review has been scheduled for forty years.</div>

The tablet says forty, and it is 2002. Mr Pell says that the tablet will be recut when the review sits, and that it would be a waste of a mason to do it twice.

The crest over the door is a sword, a scroll and a small question mark. The question mark is the provisional part. The sign it hangs from is held on with six bolts; the last sign had one bolt, and was taken by somebody who needed a sword more than the Guild did.

## Registration

<blockquote>"You cannot be an adventurer in this district unregistered. You can DO the adventuring. You just can't be one."</blockquote>

**<a href="../People/Lionel_Pell">Mr Pell</a>**, the Registrar, keeps the Guild, and has kept it for forty years. There are three requirements, and he will recite them to you whether you have asked or not. One: a character reference from a recognised authority, which means somebody with a chain. Two: a hat. The form requires a hat. It does not say why, and it does not say which; Mrs Barrow across the way sells one for thirty crowns, and no, they are not related. Three: Form A-1.

Form A-1 was mislaid in 1989. For eleven years nobody registered, because nobody could, and Mr Pell looked for it every day, everywhere in the hall. It was under the doormat, which says WELCOME, six feet from his desk. The Forty-Eighth found it. "Do not say it. I am aware of the tower. I am aware that the key was under the mat. I do not find the pattern amusing." In forty years nobody had brought him all three at once.

The Forty-Eighth's reference was written by <a href="../People/Elder_Wispel">Elder Wispel</a> of Thistlewick, with the good pen. It confirms that Bram Thistle is the Chosen One, is of good character, and grows a decent turnip, and the last part is underlined. The Elder asked the Guild, through Bram, for a reciprocal arrangement, by which he meant that he would like them to know that Thistlewick's Committee exists. The Guild kept the reference. It is framed, on the wall, beside the Charter. I am not sure that is what the Elder meant, but he was delighted.

## The filing

Every party that ever went north is in the Guild's filing, on its shelves, filed by the surname of the party's least important member, because that was the rule when the rule was made, and nobody has been able to unmake it. Finding a party is therefore a matter of deciding who in it mattered least, which has caused, Mr Pell says, a certain amount of feeling. Where the Forty-Eighth's company is filed I was not told, and I did not ask, because everybody I asked about the Tower said it was them, and I could not face asking them which of them was least.

Cobb Lorrimer, a thief, is registered under T.

## The board

The bounty board is in the hall, behind a small rope. A card on the rope says MEMBERS ONLY. The rope is eleven inches high, and could be stepped over by a determined duck. In 2000 there were two cards on it:

* **The Crab of Unusual Size**, which held the west beach and would not be reasoned with, and had been there longer than the town. Nine hundred crowns, on production of a witness.
* **M. Crooke, Bandit Chief**, who had a camp in the western hills and ran a six-week banditry apprenticeship with a syllabus. Twelve hundred crowns and the Guild's quiet relief.

Both are discharged, in Mr Pell's hand. The fish stall added two bowls of chowder to the first, because it was extremely pleased about the beach.

## Monuments

The Guild keeps the <a href="../Places/The_Barrow_of_the_Forty-Fourth">Barrow of the Forty-Fourth</a> as a Scheduled Monument of the district, entry restricted to registered adventurers. The stone is not locked. There is simply a sign, and you are simply not registered, and that is enough. It is the Guild's only monument, and it has done more good there than anywhere: a registered party went in, and the Forty-Fourth stopped, and asked for a bench.

## The southern roster

Mr Pell also holds Form C-12(S), by which a Chosen One may strike off any companion picked up south of the wall. It is the southern half of the Prophecy Committee's Form C-12 and nothing to do with registration. "A-1 registers you. C-12(S) unregisters them. Entirely separate instruments, and I will not hear otherwise." Nobody had suggested otherwise. He had the answer ready, which means somebody once did, and that he has been waiting.

## Form Z

In a drawer of Mr Pell's desk which most people never notice there was a single sheet, yellow at the edges: **Form Z, Matters Not Anticipated**. He kept it for forty years. In case. In forty years he never once used it, because every matter had been anticipated, by somebody, on something.

After the ending the Forty-Eighth put a bounty card on his desk that had no authority, no number and a drawing of an octopus on the back, for <a href="../Places/The_Stair">Something Unscheduled</a>, at the bottom of the Stair under Hithermost, which the party had fought and beaten. There was no form for it. Mr Pell said so twice, the second time more slowly. Then he opened the drawer. His hand, I am told, was not quite steady. He stamped the card DISCHARGED, and then held out his hand for the Guild Card (Provisional), and gave back one without.

<blockquote>"A discharged bounty is a completed engagement. A member with a completed engagement is not provisional. The first in forty years. I shall mention it to the review. When it sits."</blockquote>

He did not smile, quite. It was a near thing.

## Afterwards

The review has not sat. Mr Pell has mentioned the Forty-Eighth to it, by letter, and the letter is in the filing, under the review, pending. The Guild is still provisional. One of its members is not. Mr Pell has had a second Form Z printed, at his own expense, and it is in the drawer, in case. He says that he does not expect to need it, and that he did not expect to need the first.

The board has been empty since 2000, except for a card that says NO VACANCIES, which Mr Pell put up because a board with nothing on it looked, he said, as if the Guild had stopped expecting things. Mrs Barrow still sells the hat.
