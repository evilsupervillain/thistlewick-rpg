<p class="lede">The <b>Order of the Errant</b> is the order of knights who ride out, and keep riding, and write home. It keeps a Chapter House at <a href="../Places/Coldbarrow">Coldbarrow</a>, in the far north, with a great map of the island in it and a pin in the map for every knight in the field, and a clerk who reads the map the way other people read a will. One knight, one pin. That is the system.</p>

## Admission

A knight of the Errant is made by a vigil: dusk to dawn, in a chapel, under a shield that the Order hangs there for him beforehand, with a sponsor standing by and friends keeping the watch. It is a short ceremony and an old one, and it goes wrong in exactly one way, which is that the knight has to find the chapel.

Sir Aldric Pemberton-Gore III was due to keep his at sixteen, at Weeping Bottom, under a shield showing a knight in a field, looking back over his shoulder. He kept it in the wrong chapel eleven times, two of them the Order's own Chapter House at Coldbarrow, which says CHAPTER HOUSE on the door and which he thought was being modest, and one of them a barn with a weathercock. The shield hung over the right chapel's door for thirty years, and the Sisters who own the chapel took it for a pun about vigilance. In the year 2000 he kept the vigil in the right chapel, with Sir Hamo Tench standing sponsor. Something came in under the door at midnight; his article tells the rest.

## The pins

Every knight in the field has a white-headed pin in the great map at Coldbarrow, where they were last reported. A knight reports every quarter, IN THE FIELD, and is written back to, and the pin is moved, in ink. Not pencil. Pencil, Mordaunt says, is how they got there.

The pins I know of, in March 2002:

* **Dame Winifred Cope**, at <a href="../Places/Little_Gore">Little Gore</a>, who writes every quarter NOTHING TO REPORT, and is written back to, VERY GOOD. Her pin has never once needed reviewing.
* **Sir Hamo Tench**, in a bed exactly on the line in the ward at <a href="../Places/Weeping_Bottom">Weeping Bottom</a>, where he came for the sea air sixteen years before 2000 with a cough. He writes IN THE FIELD, and it is true; it is a field of a sort.
* **<a href="../People/Sir_Dunstable">Sir Dunstable</a>**, at the Chapel of Continuance at Hithermost. Mordaunt says that it is the one pin in the map he has never had to move. I put that down here without comment, because it is evidence of something, and the Order has not said what, and his own article does not decide.
* **<a href="../People/Sir_Aldric_Pemberton-Gore_III">Sir Aldric</a>**, who has no pin in the map. See below.

## The review

Until 2000 there were nine red pins in the map for one knight, six of them correct on different dates, and three of them correct in the same week, which is not possible, and was true. The Order kept nine files on him, each certain that it was the real one. A committee was formed to review the pins. It sat on Thursdays, and it was Mordaunt, appointed four times, and quorate.

It could not fault the pins, and so had to fault the knight, and he agreed that that was fair. It offered him Knight Commander of the Chapter, with a desk at Coldbarrow, and a pin that did not move, if he would stay in one place. It was the one thing he could not do, and the one thing he had always wanted to be asked, and he declined, beautifully, and asked for that to be minuted, in ink. The committee then resolved that the pin should go with the knight, and created a rank that Thursday: **Knight Errant, At Large**. Wherever the knight is, the map is correct. Sir Aldric carries his pin. The rest of the story, and the full minute, are under <a href="../Places/Coldbarrow">Coldbarrow</a>.

## Squires

The Order permits a knight a squire, whose duties are the knight's armour, the knight's horse and, in the one case I know of, the knight's whereabouts. Sir Aldric's squire is his nephew, Pemberton-Gore the Fourth, who finds the gates. Every week since the review the Fourth has sent Mordaunt a note of where his uncle is, and the date, and the time, in ink, and Mordaunt keeps them in a tenth volume, which is the only one of the ten that agrees with itself.

## The Chapter House

The Order's motto is on a scroll in the stained glass at Coldbarrow, over a kneeling knight, and is too small to read from the floor and, one suspects, too long to fit. I have asked for it by letter. Mordaunt wrote back that the motto was in the window.

There are two suits of armour in the hall. One says CEREMONIAL. DO NOT GO ON A QUEST IN. The other says ISSUE PATTERN, KNIGHTS ERRANT, ONE, and under it, in pencil, SIZES RUN SMALL. The pencil is the only pencil in Coldbarrow, and Mordaunt has left it, because it is true.

## Afterwards

There are no red pins in the great map now. In the margin there is a small card: PEMBERTON-GORE. AT LARGE. PIN CARRIED. The Order's daybook still says PINS REVIEWED, PINS REVIEWED, PINS REVIEWED (WITH TEA), and the tea is new.

Sir Aldric found Thistlewick's north gate unaided, once, on the ninth of June 2001, at about eleven in the morning. I sent the date to Mordaunt, with the names of my three witnesses. He wrote back to say that he had it already, from the Fourth, and that it agreed.
