<p class="lede">The <b>Worshipful Company of Smiths</b> of <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a> is the body that judges the work of smiths, and has done so for longer than anybody in Great Dunnage will admit to not knowing. Every smith in the land may submit one piece to its Assize. Very few manage to submit one. It is a very good Schedule.</p>

## The Assize of Hammers

Every three years, in the spring, the Company holds the Assize of Hammers in its hall, and every smith in the land may submit one piece, to be judged by the Wardens against the Schedule. The prize is the Company's mark. The prize piece of the last Assize stands on the Company's own anvil in the hall, with a card beside it: *Clauses one to forty-one, satisfied. The Company's mark, awarded.* It is a hinge. It is a very good hinge.

The Hall also gives a prize of its own, BEST IN HALL, which is not the Assize and is not judged against the Schedule, but by judges, who are smiths, and argue. Bryd Ollerenshaw of <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a> won it in 1987, the year Hob Grumnir did not enter, having a cold.

## The Schedule

*The Schedule of What Constitutes a Piece, being the rules of the Assize of Hammers*, is on the wall of the hall. It has forty-one clauses. **Warden Crabbe** reads it out at the Assize as well, all forty-one, and it takes most of the day. People ask why they don't just pin it up. They do. He reads it out as well. It is a very good Schedule.

I give the clauses I have:

> 1. A piece shall be the entrant's own work, from the ore up.
>
> 2. A piece shall be the entrant's own property.
>
> 3. A hinge is a piece. Half a hinge is not a piece. A pair of hinges is two pieces, unless submitted as a door.
>
> 40. A pair of tongs is one piece. A tong is not a piece. Nobody has ever tried.
>
> 41. The decision of the Wardens is final, and so is the Schedule.

Clauses four to thirty-nine I have not seen in writing, and Warden Crabbe has offered to read them to me, in full, in Great Dunnage, on any day I like, and has said that it would be no trouble. I am going in the spring.

## The officers

**<a href="../People/Prime_Warden_Stannard">Prime Warden Stannard</a>** is a tall woman in black, with iron-grey hair pinned up and gold at her collar, who stands at the Company's table as if it might try something. She asks whether you are a smith. If you are not, you may look. Do not touch.

**Warden Crabbe**, the Junior Warden, keeps the Schedule, aloud, and the cards on the hall's exhibits, in writing, among them the one on a suit of armour entered for the prize *by a smith who wishes to remain anonymous*.

**<a href="../People/Miss_Fettle">Miss Fettle</a>**, Appraiser to the Company, is a young woman in round spectacles who looks at swords the way other people look at sunsets. She will ask after the tang. Everybody looks at the blade. Nobody ever asks after the tang. It is the most important part of the whole sword, and it is hidden, and she always asks. She does not charge for tangs. She also does curses, elective, on Form C-1, in triplicate, and they are mostly paperwork; the economy one lasts until you next sleep in a proper bed.

## Beatrice

In the year 2000 <a href="../People/Hob_Grumnir">Hob Grumnir</a> of <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a> came to the Assize. He had never entered. He had never owned anything he made, because clause two is about property and Hob gives everything away, and when he went round eleven people to ask for one thing back, every one of them said no, with a better reason than his. So he laid his hammer on the Company's table instead. Beatrice was disqualified under clause one, not being his own work, and passed clauses two to forty-one, and the Wardens' notes are pinned up beside the hinge:

<div class="stamp">BEATRICE. A hammer. From H. Grumnir, Thistlewick. Clause one: not satisfied. Two to forty-one: satisfied. DISQUALIFIED. Remarks: in the Wardens' opinion, right about everything.</div>

It is the only entry in the Company's history to be disqualified with a remark. Hob said that she would be unbearable. He has not entered again, because he had what he came for.

## Afterwards

In 2001 the Hall asked <a href="../People/Hob_Grumnir">Hob Grumnir</a> and <a href="../People/Bryd_Ollerenshaw">Bryd Ollerenshaw</a> to sit together as judges of BEST IN HALL, which was either a compliment or a joke, and the Prime Warden will not say which. They disagreed for nine hours. The prize was awarded. Neither of them will say to whom, and the Company has not published it, and Warden Crabbe, when I asked, read me clause forty-one.

The next Assize is in the spring of 2003. The Schedule has not been amended. It is a very good Schedule.
