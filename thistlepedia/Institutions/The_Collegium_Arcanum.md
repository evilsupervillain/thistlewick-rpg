<p class="lede">The <b>Collegium Arcanum</b> is the oldest school of magic in the land, a college of cream-coloured stone, all towers and purple spires, on open grass near the east coast. It has given six hundred years of the finest magical education there is. Nobody there has ever been rude to anybody, and nothing there has ever been refused. Everything is referred.</p>

## The arms

Over the window of the Porter's Lodge are the arms of the Collegium, carved and gilded, and on a ribbon under them is the motto: REFERATUR. *Let it be referred.* Six hundred years of the finest magical education in the land, and that is the part they put over the door. I have come to think it is the most honest motto I know. <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>'s is *Not us, unless it has to be*, which is a hope. The Collegium's is a description.

## The Founder

In the middle of the lawn stands the Founder, in stone, in a pointed hat, holding up his staff. The plinth says that he founded the Collegium in one afternoon, by himself. Underneath, in smaller letters, it says that everything since has been done by committee, and that a committee is considering the plinth.

You cannot read the plinth from the path. You have to stand on the grass, and the grass is reserved to Fellows, and the public is asked to keep to the path, which goes round. Everybody who has ever read the plinth has heard, from the gateway behind them, somebody clear his throat. The Forty-Eighth did it twice.

## The Lodge

The real power in the Collegium is the Porter's Lodge, and the Lodge knows it. **Mr Gedge**, the Head Porter, is a large man in a dark suit with a ginger beard, and he stands in the gateway. He does not own it. He has the air of somebody who could, and has decided not to bother.

A visitor needs a pass, which is issued by the Lodge, on the recommendation of a Fellow, and countersigned by the Lodge. Nobody knows any Fellows. "They don't come out." So Mr Gedge recommends you himself, and countersigns it, and issues it, three times on one card in the same hand, and the whole thing takes about four seconds. He put it to the Forty-Eighth like this: "The Master needs a meeting. The Dean needs a form. I need a pencil." Then: "Hall's on the left, Library's on the right. Keep off the grass."

By the Lodge window is a board of names, with a brass slide by each that says IN or OUT. THE MASTER: OUT. The slide has rusted there. THE DEAN: OUT, and under it in the Porter's pencil, *In Hall. Out to callers.* THE SUB-LIBRARIAN: IN. THE HEAD PORTER: IN. The two who are in are the two who run it.

## Congregation

Congregation is the governing body, and it meets to receive the minutes of the last meeting, which received the minutes of the one before. Its notices are posted in the court. I copied one:

> Item 2. That the matter of the Library roof be referred to the Committee for the Library Roof.
>
> Item 3. That the Committee for the Library Roof be referred back to Congregation, with thanks.

**Dr Hapgood**, Junior Fellow of twenty-two years, goes round the lawn quickly with a great many papers. The Bursar refers them to Estates, Estates refers them to the Junior Fellow, and the Junior Fellow takes them back to the Bursar. In the year 2000 what he was carrying was a request for somebody to carry things. It had been going round since March. "Once more round! Nearly there. Never there."

Dr Hapgood's paper *On the Kindling of Damp Materials*, which once went out under a more senior name, now has his own name on it, with a note by the Dean. That is one matter the Collegium did not refer. It was settled for it, by a former student, on terms.

## The Library

The Library is where money goes to die. Its Appeal, for the roof, raised 1,412 crowns in the year 2000, and spent 1,412 crowns on the Appeal. The Library thanks you for your interest. On the floor under the notice is a bucket.

**<a href="../People/Sub-Librarian_Prewitt">Sub-Librarian Prewitt</a>** sits at a desk with nothing on it but an hourglass and a very large ledger, in a navy jacket and a tie, and she is immovable, courteous and correct. A book may be borrowed on a reader's ticket, which is issued on a letter from a Fellow. Mr Gedge's card will not do. "Mr Gedge is not a Fellow. Mr Gedge is a great deal more than a Fellow, and the Library cannot accept his letters on that account." It is never a no. Nothing at the Collegium is ever a no. It is *not from this desk*. She can refer you to the Committee for Readers, which meets in the Library, so that its members must all hold readers' tickets.

A young man at the reading table asked for a book at Michaelmas. It has been referred to the shelf it is on, which he is told is a very long way off.

The **Register of Outstanding Fines** lies on a pile of lesser registers. It is the size of a gravestone, with a brass clasp, locked, and Mr Gedge lifts it for her on Mondays. Most of what is in it is in one account. Miss Prewitt could not say whose. It was the largest number she had ever written down, and she wrote it down again every morning with the day's interest, and it was never once wrong. I asked her, by letter, whether that number is in the Register still. She replied that the Register is confidential, and very heavy, and that Mr Gedge still lifts it on Mondays, and that she hoped I was keeping well. Readers who want more should consult <a href="../People/Zephyrine_Vance">Miss Vance's article</a>, which prints both of the documents I have, and decides between neither.

On the shelf of the Collegium's monographs, in order of publication, are *On the Habits of the Lesser Wyvern*, *The Lesser Wyvern, Revisited* and *The Lesser Wyvern: A Reply To My Critics*. For some years there was a gap about an inch wide. Wren Halloway found it, in the year 2000, and looked at it for some time, and said "There's room." It is filled now, by *<a href="../Bestiary/The_Fauna_of_the_Valley">The Fauna of the Valley</a>, by What They Are*, by W. Halloway, reviewed by three Fellows, one of them the duck. The Bestiary in this encyclopedia is hers.

## The Hall

Dinner in Hall is at seven. Grace is at six. It is a long grace, and it has been referred to a committee for shortening since before anybody now an undergraduate came up.

**<a href="../People/Dean_Fenwold">Dean Fenwold</a>**, in the Collegium's white and purple, stands under his own portrait, which was painted after the incident; the painter was asked to be accurate about the eyebrows, and then, on reflection, a little less accurate. Beside it is the First Dean's portrait, with a card tucked into the frame to say that it is hung pending the approval of the Hanging Committee. It has been pending for five hundred years. The Dean's talk, *The Man Who Was Fine*, is given in Hall, Thursday week, with light refreshments, and questions afterwards if nice. Tickets from the Lodge.

## Fellows

The Fellows do not come out, and I have not met one, with one exception.

At the end of High Table, a little apart from the others, is a chair with a cushion on it, and a card: RESERVED. HONORARY FELLOW. There is a dent in the cushion, and in the dent a feather. Nobody would dream of moving it.

The Honorary Fellow is a duck. It was present, in 1989, at the incident in a very small room, and it gave evidence at the enquiry, and the Collegium, not knowing what else to do with a bird that had given evidence at a formal enquiry, elected it. It has sat in Congregation since, and has views on the endowment. It lives on a <a href="../Places/Wayside_Sights#the-ducks-pond">pond four miles off</a>, with a Fellow's medal round its neck. In the year 2000 Congregation found itself short of a quorum by one duck, and the duck had to be walked, and the Forty-Eighth's company walked it, and it walked into everything. Its vote has since been held by proxy, permanently, by Miss Vance, who has not used it. The Collegium has been quorate ever since. It is the only body I know of that a duck has made more efficient.

## Former students

The Collegium does not keep a list of its former students, because a student who leaves is referred, and a referral is not a departure. Miss Vance left in 1989 under papers that were drafted and initialled and never filed, and was therefore on study leave, unpaid and in good standing, for eleven years. A good many people in the Collegium's history may be on study leave still. The hedge mages who meet in a lay-by on the west road are, between them, better than the Collegium at exactly four things, because one of them learned from a book with four pages missing; none of them was a student here, and they would like that known.

## Afterwards

Nothing at the Collegium has happened since 2000, which is what the Collegium is for. The Library roof is with the Committee for the Library Roof, or with Congregation, depending on the week. The bucket has been emptied. The Dean has had the Hall measured, and the committee for rooms will consider it. Grace is still an hour. Dr Hapgood's request for somebody to carry things came round to me, by post, in the autumn of 2001, as a historian with an interest; I have referred it back, with thanks.

I wrote to the Master to ask whether he would read this article before it went to press. Mr Gedge wrote back, in pencil, that the Master was out.
