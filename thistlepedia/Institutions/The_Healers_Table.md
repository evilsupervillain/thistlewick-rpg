<p class="lede">The <b>Healers' Table</b> is the annual meeting of every surviving party medic within forty miles of <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a>, at a long table in a cellar room behind a door behind the red curtain by the fire in the <a href="../Places/The_Slain_Wyvern">Slain Wyvern</a>. It has one item of business, and has had it for as long as anybody at it can remember, which is a long time, because the people at it are old, and good at keeping people alive.</p>

## Item one

The Table is sitting when its chair says so. Item one is *the worst thing you've ever had to improvise with*, round the table, in turn. There is no item two. I have not been in the room, and am not on the list, and the door says so, and somebody inside says so too. But the door was not quite shut in the year 2000, and I was at my table in the bar, and the members have since told me which of their answers I may print, and they are these.

* **Mother Grizel**, eighty-three, seven parties: a femur, set with a church candlestick, in the church, during the service. The vicar carried on. The candlestick is on her mantelpiece.
* **Dr Hobday**, seventy-two: a barbarian, stitched with a lute string. The bard wanted it back. He got it back, eventually. Do not ask the bard about the lute; he will play it.
* **Nan Gilly**, ninety: a wizard's leg, splinted with his own staff. He walks with it now. He has never said thank you, and sends a card. He is ninety-two, and still cross.
* **Mr Spence**: a tourniquet, out of a paladin's sash. The paladin minded about the sash.
* **Mrs Marrow**, in the chair: a duke's son, four hit points, nine weeks. Everybody has heard it. Everybody nods anyway, because it is a very good one.

The four of them look up at once when the door opens, the way people do who have spent their lives listening for somebody falling.

## The youngest

Until 2000 the youngest at the Table was Mr Spence. Then Mrs Marrow saw a Sister in the bar with a flask, and asked whether she was a medic, and said that if so she was on the list, and in, and not to dawdle. Merribell of Thistlewick was the youngest at the Table by thirty-eight years. Mr Spence says he was the youngest until about four minutes before that, and that it was lovely.

When it came round to her she had nothing. Water, and sitting down, and socks; it always works. "I've never lost anybody. I know what that means. It isn't a virtue. It's an average."

Nobody said that she would. Nobody said that she would not. Nobody laughed, and nobody patted her hand. The four of them looked at Mrs Marrow, which is what the chair is for.

<blockquote>"Then write it down. All of it. Every cup of water and every pair of socks. Now, tonight. While you still think it's boring. Because one day it won't be, and you'll want to know what you did. And the day it doesn't, you come down here, and you tell us. That's what the Table is for, love."</blockquote>

## The Table's Kit

Then Mother Grizel put a candle-end on the table, and Dr Hobday a coil of gut string, and Nan Gilly a splint, and Mr Spence, after some thought, a very good sash, and they were rolled up in canvas, and that is the Table's Kit, and it goes to the youngest. It always has. It had been a while. Merribell took out her book, the one with every ailment in Thistlewick in it, and turned to a clean page, and started writing down that day.

The Table's Kit is not to be confused with Mrs Marrow's own kit, forty years of field medicine in a canvas roll, which she lent the Forty-Eighth for the road north, and which came back, and about the state of which she was very rude, as promised.

## 2001

The Table sat again the next year, as it always does, and Merribell had something to tell it, as the Prophecy's own account said she would. She spoke for eleven minutes, which is the shortest address in the Table's history, and the Table minuted it as the most useful. The subject was water.

The minutes are the Table's and not mine, and I have not seen them. But Sister Merribell has given me a note, which she says is the same thing, shorter, and which she would like printed where adventurers will find it. Here it is, entire.

<p class="contributor">Contributed by Sister Merribell, of the Order of Whatever Works, Thistlewick, February 2002</p>

<div class="disputed">

**Common complaints of adventurers, and their treatment.**

1. **Dehydration.** The commonest condition on any road north, and the cause of most of the others. Signs: headache, tiredness, being cross, saying you are fine. Treatment: a glass of water. Then another one.

2. **Exhaustion.** Signs: walking into things. Treatment: sit down. Not on a mound with a curse on it. On a bench, facing the sea, or a chair.

3. **Wet feet.** Left alone, this becomes a real condition with a real name, and I am not going to tell you the name, because you will like it. Treatment: dry socks. Two pairs, and a spare.

4. **Minor wounds**, most of which adventurers call major. Clean it. Bandage it. Stop showing it to people.

5. **Revival.** A person who has fallen over and is not breathing needs a medic, at once. A person who has fallen over and is breathing, and is lying there because it is pleasant to be knelt beside, needs to be asked to tea, by the parish, on purpose, through the door.

6. **Fitness to travel.** I have certified one Chosen One fit, under clause eleven. His knee jerked. His pulse was good. He had not had water since Tuesday. He has had water since.

Then see how you feel.

</div>

<div class="marginalia">I have set Sister Merribell's note in the box that this volume keeps for disputed matters, which is not what the box is for, and she has asked me why. It is because every one of the medics at the Table, on reading it, disputed a different line, at length, and then agreed with the last one. Mrs Marrow says that item five is the best thing anybody has said at the Table in forty years, and that she will not have it repeated in front of her. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

Mrs Marrow chaired in 2001, and will chair this year, and has told me that she is not going to retire. Nan Gilly's wizard sent a card. Nobody at the Table has died since 2000, and Merribell says that is not a virtue, it is an average, and that she is keeping an eye on it.
