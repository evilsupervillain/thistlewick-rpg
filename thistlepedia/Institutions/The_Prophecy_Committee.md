<p class="lede">The <b>Prophecy Committee</b> is the body that keeps the <a href="../History/The_Prophecy_of_Thistlewick">Prophecy of Thistlewick</a>, chooses each Chosen One, sends him north, and minutes it. It has done so forty-eight times. It has worked forty-seven times. Since the year 2000 it has been the custodian of a document that is not binding on anybody, and it has not missed a meeting.</p>

## What it is for

The Committee was constituted to discharge Thistlewick's side of the Prophecy, which it reads, and has always read, as an obligation on the village to send somebody. The clauses that touch it are these. Clause two makes "a hundred years" mean *such period as the Committee shall find convenient*, and so the Committee decides when the century is up; it has lately found ten years convenient, and it prefers years that end in nought. Clause eleven requires a Chosen One to be certified fit before he leaves. Clause four applies; it says so on the back of the leaflet. And clause nineteen, which the Committee added itself, somewhere about the Ninth, concerns a cottage at the coast.

None of the Committee has ever remarked on the arithmetic of clause two, in my hearing or in the minutes, and I have read the minutes. I am a Nether Sopping man, and I counted. I put the sum in Volume Two and in <a href="../History/Two_Reckonings">Two Reckonings</a>, and the Committee has read both, and filed them, and nobody in Thistlewick has mentioned it to me since. I have stopped expecting them to. It is not that they disagree. It is that, in Thistlewick, it does not come up.

## Who sits on it

**The Elder** chairs. <a href="../People/Elder_Wispel">Elder Wispel</a> has done so for a good while; asked how long, he says *a good while*, and means it. He gives each Chosen One the Prophecy by unrolling it until it reaches the far wall, and the gold from the parish fund, and the potions from the parish fund. "The prophecy is, regrettably, free."

**The Clerk** sits at the end of the table, by the door, with Form C-12, and the weariness of a man who has answered every question before you ask it. He has no name in the minutes; he is the Clerk.

**The councillors** sit down the side. One has, for as long as anybody remembers, been asking whether the Dark Lord *shall be slain* or *shall be defeated*, because *slain* is a commitment. Another, <a href="../People/Councillor_Fenn">Councillor Fenn</a>, held for forty years that clause twelve was the problem. "That's not a warning. That's a subscription." He was right, and the minutes of the week of the ending say that he wished it minuted that he had been saying so. He carries the key to the struck material on his belt.

**The Fete Sub-Committee** reports to the Committee on everything that is to be read aloud at the fete, which is where the Prophecy Fund's income comes from, and strikes what cannot be. Its most famous minute is the one on the wall of the Hall, carried four to one; the one against was Mrs Wispel.

## How it chooses

In session, at a convenient moment. The candidate need not be present. The Forty-Eighth was not; he was asleep, two fields away, and the minutes of the meeting at which he was chosen give his name under *apologies for absence*, with one goat. I do not know why there is a goat, and I have asked, and the Clerk said that the minutes were the minutes. Nobody has ever been chosen who said no, because nobody has ever been asked.

This is also why the Forty-Eighth's certificate of fitness, under clause eleven, was blank when he set out: nobody had examined a man who was asleep. It was filled in, eventually, on the road, by <a href="../People/Merribell">Merribell</a>, in front of a witness, with a halibut.

## Its paperwork

The Committee's forms are numerous and I have not seen all of them. The ones that every Chosen One meets are:

* **The leaflet**, *So You've Been Chosen*. One page. CONGRATULATIONS! and then, smaller, CLAUSE FOUR APPLIES.
* **Form C-12**, Roster Amendments, held by the Clerk, by which a Chosen One may strike a companion off and send them home to think about it. Thistlewick personnel only. A companion struck off keeps the kit and the experience; it was tried the other way once, and there is a form about why not. Anybody picked up south of the wall is on **Form C-12(S)**, which is held in Nether Sopping by Mr Pell of the <a href="The_Adventurers_Guild">Adventurers' Guild</a>. "Not my circus."
* **Form S-7**, Application to Consult Struck Material (Verse), countersigned as to the wording, which admits the bearer through the door behind the red curtain to the box. It has been used once that I know of, by <a href="../People/Piper_Quill">Piper Quill</a>, who signed for it in metre.
* **The certificate** of fitness to travel, under clause eleven.

The rest are on the shelves of the Hall, which are nearly all minutes, and include *The Meeting To Decide When To Hold The Meeting*, which is in at least four volumes, and *What To Do If The Chosen One Is Not In*, which recommends leaving a note.

## What it keeps

The Committee keeps everything. It keeps the Prophecy, rolled. It keeps the Wall of the Forty-Seven, and since 2000 a forty-eighth frame. It keeps a box of two hundred years of struck verses, one to a slip, dated and initialled, behind a door behind a curtain, under lock, so that nobody may read them; and it keeps them in the best box it owns. It keeps a potted plant, which it has meant to water since 1987, because it is in the minutes. It keeps the accounts of the Prophecy Fund: income, the fete; expenditure, swords, forty-seven, and scroll, a great deal. It keeps its own minutes most carefully of all, because the minutes are how the Committee knows that it has met.

## The year 2000

The Committee's minute book lies open, in the Hall, at the week the Forty-Eighth came home, and anybody may read it:

> ITEM 3. Clause twelve. Struck, nem. con. In its place, a note: SEE BRAM.
>
> ITEM 4. Clause nineteen. Applies. Letter sent, cottage aired. Friday suggested.
>
> ITEM 5. Councillor Fenn wished it minuted that he had been saying so. Minuted.

I wish to be fair to the Committee here, because it is easy not to be. Item three was carried without a vote against. It was the end of the thing it existed to do, and it struck it, and wrote in its place the name of a turnip farmer, which is as near to an apology as a committee can get. Item four was the Committee being a committee. Six months later, after a meeting of eleven hours which <a href="../People/Elder_Wispel">Elder Wispel</a> chaired throughout, it struck clause nineteen as well. I have the minutes of that meeting. They are mostly about the biscuits. They are also, on the last page, about clause nineteen, and it is struck, and the cottage at the coast is Bram's to use when he comes south.

Since the ending there has been a lectern by the door, and on it a ledger, *The Obligatory Quest, as amended*, which is kept up to date, and ends *The account ends there, for the present*. It is the only document the Committee has ever kept that admits it is not finished.

## Afterwards

The Committee still meets. It has nothing to choose, nobody to send, and no clause twelve, and it meets anyway, as it always has, and consults the Prophecy at every meeting, out of habit, and minutes that it has been consulted. Twice since 2000 it has minuted that it might need to see Bram, as the note says; neither time did it, and neither time has it said what about. It offered him a seat. He declined, because it meets in planting time. The minutes record his apologies for absence, without a goat.

The forty-eighth frame on the Wall still says PORTRAIT TO FOLLOW. SITTER AT THE COAST. I have written to the Committee three times to say where the sitter is. My letters are filed under *no change*.

<div class="marginalia">I asked the Clerk, for this article, what the Committee is for now. He looked at me with the weariness of a man who had answered the question before, and said that it was for meeting. I have thought about it a good deal since, and I do not think he was being unkind, or wrong. Thistlewick has a great many things in it that are for nothing in particular now, a sword above a fireplace among them, and the village is fond of all of them. - H.B.</div>
