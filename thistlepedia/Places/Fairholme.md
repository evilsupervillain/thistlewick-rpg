<p class="lede"><b>Fairholme</b> is a small kingdom in the east whose princess has been kidnapped eleven times, twelve if you count the eagle, which the Palace does not, and whose king has posted the traditional reward every time: her hand, or half the kingdom. Every rescuer has taken the half. Very sensible. Nobody's fault. But there is only so much kingdom, and it is now governed by a council of its shareholders, round three tables pushed end to end, and its chief business is drainage.</p>

## The council

The council chamber is most of the palace. The table is three tables end to end in three different woods, a dark old one, the long pale royal one and, on the end, a plain kitchen table that still has a drawer for the spoons, and the council has outgrown each of them in turn. Through the tall window you can see the whole of Fairholme and every one of its eleven new fences.

**<a href="../People/King_Aldous">King Aldous</a>** sits at the middle, small, in a red robe and a crown, and will ask you whether you are here about the rescue. If you say you are only passing he says, oh dear, that is how Mr Pilbeam started. He has given away half the kingdom eleven times. Osric, who sits at the table, has done the arithmetic, and the King is down to a two-thousand-and-forty-eighth. His portrait on the wall shows him holding the kingdom, on a map, in both hands; the map has been cut into eleven pieces since, and nobody has told the portrait. He asks every visitor, if they do rescue her, to take the half. Everybody does. There is a form.

The shareholders are the rescuers, and a deed in the chamber reads TO THE BEARER, ONE TWO-THOUSAND-AND-FORTY-EIGHTH PART OF THE KINGDOM OF FAIRHOLME, with a map; the part is mostly a ditch. Tamsin rescued her from a sorcerer, year before last, lovely girl, took the half, obviously. Brannoc had a wyrm, and has the eastern marshes. Aurel had a sorcerer; everybody had a sorcerer; she had packed a cardigan for the tower, and it was a cold tower. Hild got a turnip field and the north bridge, and the bridge is the troll's, and they take turns. Morwen was met at the door with a bag packed and a card, and says nobody has ever been so pleased to see her, or so ready. Kit got the bit with the orchard, and says it was worth it, and that she had brought sandwiches, for both of them. Leof's half came with a duck pond, and the ducks have been very good about it. Valen is a bit lost. **Mr and Mrs Budge** were only mucking out, when she came down the hill with a tin of cake, and they walked her home, and that was all. They got the half between them, and did not know what to do with it, and put most of it back into the orchard.

There is an empty chair at the end with a place card: SIR DUNSTABLE OF THE VALE. ONE SHARE. BACK DIRECTLY. - D. And there is **<a href="../People/Mr_Pilbeam">Mr Pilbeam</a>**, at the foot of the table, who was only passing. There was an eagle. That was six years ago. The council has not decided whether the eagle counts, and he cannot go until it does, and he looks at the door the way a man looks at a door he has been looking at for six years. A potted plant by the wall has a flag in it, SHARE TWELVE, pending a decision on the eagle.

The minutes, as they stood when the Forty-Eighth called:

> MINUTES OF THE COUNCIL OF FAIRHOLME. Present: HM the King; nine heroes; Mr & Mrs Budge; Mr Pilbeam (passing). Apologies: Sir Dunstable (in a mountain).
>
> 1. The table. To be lengthened again. Carried.
>
> 2. Drainage. 3. Drainage, continued. 4. Drainage, continued.
>
> 5. The eagle. The Palace does not count the eagle. Whether Mr Pilbeam, who rescued Her Highness from the eagle, therefore holds a share. Deferred. Mr Pilbeam asks whether he may go. Deferred.
>
> 6. Thursdays. Her Highness has been walking up the hill on Thursdays, unaccompanied, and coming back. The Heroes' Guild has written to complain. Noted.
>
> 7. Drainage.

The council has two teapots, because the first is quite small, and that is why the meetings take all day; the second is for item six, which is always drainage, and always takes the afternoon. I have the minutes since from Mr Pilbeam, who writes. The eagle has been deferred eight times. Sir Dunstable's apologies have been recorded at every meeting, *in a mountain*, and the council has twice moved to consider whether a shareholder in a mountain is a shareholder, and has twice deferred it, on the grounds that he might be back directly.

Hob Grumnir made the hinges on the tower door. In the year 2000 he asked whether he might have them back. The King said, oh dear, they were the only part that had held. Every rescuer kicks the door in, and the door goes, and the hinges are fine. They were on the fourteenth door. There is a drawing of the fifteenth on the council table now, and the hinges are not drawn, because they will not be needed.

## Rosamund

The tower stairs go up from the chamber to **<a href="../People/Princess_Rosamund">Princess Rosamund</a>**'s room, which is the one she is taken from. She will ask whether you are the rescue, and say lovely, and ask you to carry the cake tin, and mind the seventh stair, it's a trap; the eighth is also a trap; the ninth is fine. If you are only passing, she says that that is a relief, honestly, because it is Thursday.

She goes up the hill on Thursdays. Mrs Emberthwaite does a lovely sponge, and cheats at whist. The first time Rosamund was carried up there, Mrs Emberthwaite made up the spare room, and said she had not had anybody to talk to since Mr Emberthwaite. So now Rosamund walks. It is quicker.

Her room is a model of preparation. A travelling bag stands by the window, packed and buckled, with the look of a bag that is never quite unpacked: a book, a cardigan, sandwiches in greaseproof paper made that morning, and a second round labelled OTHERS. The wardrobe has eleven sensible travelling cloaks labelled 1ST, 2ND, 3RD and onwards, and one at the end marked EAGLE. There are two tiaras on the dresser, one FOR BALLS and one, a good deal sturdier, FOR KIDNAPPINGS. Under the pillow there is a rope ladder, which she has never used, because she thinks it would be unfair on the rescuers. Her memoir, *Rescued Again*, is in eleven volumes, and every chapter is named after a rescuer and written about very kindly. Her portrait downstairs shows her aged six in a ball gown, holding a small packed bag. Already.

On the table beside the bag is a card, in a round, firm hand:

<div class="stamp">FOR WHOEVER IT IS THIS TIME. No eels. No mushrooms. Nothing in aspic. Tea at four, and not stewed. I will not be carried unless there are stairs. I would like to be home by Thursday. Thank you so much for having me.</div>

She asks visitors to take something, if they like. Everybody who comes up takes something. Usually it is her.

## Mrs Emberthwaite

Up the hill there is a cave, and by the way in a stone tablet carved deep, PLEASE WIPE YOUR FEET. NO RESCUES. Under it, in chalk, more recently: WHIST THURSDAYS. ALL WELCOME.

**<a href="../People/Mrs_Emberthwaite">Mrs Emberthwaite</a>** is a dragon, very large and very red, seventy-two this year by Rosamund's reckoning, with a pair of reading spectacles on a chain round her neck. She will ask you to come in, and to mind the gold, it gets everywhere, and whether you are a hero, only she is retired, she has had a letter. If you are passing, lovely. Do you play whist? She will win, by a margin that makes you check the deck. The deck is fine. It is you.

Her hoard is dusted every morning, very gently, with a feather. A smaller heap has a flag in it, RAINY DAY, and another heap is for the card games, at a copper a hand on Thursdays, and Rosamund is ruthless; the score pad shows her winning by a mile. The best china has a small scorch mark on the rim of every cup, from being drunk out of by a dragon with a cold. The cuckoo clock's cuckoo is a very small dragon which comes out on the hour and sneezes. She keeps a pot of umbrellas for the knights who come up the hill in the rain meaning to slay her, so that they at least go home dry, and a bound volume of their letters, every one of which begins FOUL BEAST and ends YOURS FAITHFULLY. Her book of card games for two has TWO crossed out and AND ON THURSDAYS, THREE written in.

There is a big wooden chair drawn up to the card table, much sat in, once, with a pipe on the seat. It was Mr Emberthwaite's. He did the roaring, mostly; she was always more of an indoor dragon. Rosamund asked whose it was, the first Thursday, and has never sat in it. She pulls up the stool.

At the end of a short passage is the spare room: a bed made up with clean sheets, a washstand, a rag rug, and curtains hung neatly over no window at all, because a room ought to have curtains. On the pillow: ROSAMUND - THURSDAYS. AIRED WEDNESDAYS. NO EELS IN THE HOUSE. TEA AT FOUR, NOT STEWED. She has never once stayed the night. She walks home after the whist. There is a hot-water bottle in the bed all the same, and it is warm.

<div class="marginalia">Miss Halloway asked me to say that Mrs Emberthwaite is not in the Bestiary, at Mrs Emberthwaite's request, which Miss Halloway thinks is very proper. A tapestry in the cave shows two dragons asleep in the sun on a hillside, hung where the light catches it in the afternoon. I asked whether it was anybody. She said it was a very good likeness, and dealt.</div>
