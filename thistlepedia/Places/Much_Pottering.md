<p class="lede"><b>Much Pottering</b> is a village where nothing has ever happened, and which has put up a stone about it. It is the kind of village a story starts in, the quiet one, the nice one, the one that is on fire by the end of the first chapter, and it has declined. It is twinned with Upper Clanging, and the children write.</p>

## The plaque

On the green is a stone, the lettering picked out in gold and polished every morning: THE PARISH OF MUCH POTTERING HAS NEVER BEEN BURNED DOWN, OVERRUN, FORETOLD, CURSED, CHOSEN, OR SET UPON BY ANYTHING. Erected by the Parish Council, which was not busy. Somebody goes at NEVER BURNED DOWN with a cloth, often.

**Mrs Agatha Throssell**, chair of the parish council, asks you to mind the plaque, because it has been done. They had a Chosen One through, once. He asked the way to somewhere else. The minutes of the last meeting before the year 2000 read *Nothing arising*, and she wrote that, and is rather proud of it. She chose Upper Clanging for the twinning off the map in 1997: as different as possible, she said, and there it was. Neither village has ever visited the other, and it is going beautifully. The children write, one each; that was her idea as well. The road sign reads MUCH POTTERING. TWINNED WITH UPPER CLANGING. PLEASE GO SLOWLY. THERE IS NO HURRY, AND THERE NEVER HAS BEEN.

## Things to walk into

Much Pottering has no monsters, and so its visitors walk into things instead. **Mr Dibble's fence**, which he paints every spring, and every summer, and which is very good, and has a card on the post, WET PAINT, a moment too late; you come away partly green; he says that is four today, and goes on painting. **The pig**, asleep across the street, which does not wake up, with PIG HAS RIGHT OF WAY chalked neatly on the cobbles beside it. **The oak**, which has a sign nailed to it at head height that you read as you walk into the branch, MIND THE BRANCH, and when you mind the branch you walk into the trunk. **The wishing well**, which is not how wishes work, with a label tied to the windlass in a child's capitals, I WISH FOR A VOLCANO. A PROPER ONE. E.G., and under it, on the same string, in different pencil, A DRAGON. S.C.

The Much Pottering Maze was planted on a Tuesday, and visitors are asked not to step over it. At its centre there is a stump to sit on and a visitors' book in a tin, and every entry in it is Mr Dibble's, and the most recent says *Found it again.* A haystack has no needle in it, only a card: NO NEEDLE. THIS IS MUCH POTTERING. Through one window a front room has had nothing happening in it all morning, very contentedly. Through another a cat sleeps beside a card, CAT, ASLEEP. NO ADVENTURES, PLEASE. A door has a poker-work card in the window, NOBODY HOME. NOTHING HAPPENING. NO QUESTS, which is the village motto, more or less, and heroes knock anyway, in case.

## Effie

**<a href="../People/Effie_Gotobed">Effie Gotobed</a>** waits at her gate for the post. She is nine, in the year 2000, and was paired off at six with a boy in Upper Clanging for the twinning, and has written to him ever since. She told him that she was nearly a princess, and that Much Pottering had a volcano, and a sea serpent in the pond. He told her that he was six foot, and drove an airship called the <a href="../History/The_Flight_of_the_Two_Hundred">Two Hundred</a>, and was the foreman at the works. Neither of them had seen the other.

In the year 2000 Spare Cotterill came to Much Pottering with the Forty-Eighth, and the girl at the gate looked at him, and he looked at her, and neither said anything for some time. *You're not six foot.* *You're not a princess.* She had never said she was a princess; she had said she was nearly a princess; it is different; it is nearly. They knew each other at once, and they were both furious about it.

She showed him the volcano. It is a ring of stones on Mr Dibble's allotment with a heap of leaves in it, which Mr Dibble lit in November and which has never gone out, and he is quite proud of that, if he is honest. It is a dormant volcano; you can tell because it is smoking. Spare looked at it for a long time and said that it was a very good bonfire. Their boiler, he said, had never gone out, not since before Mam. It had ninety tubes, and he brushed them. It was the first true thing either of them had said all morning, and they both noticed.

She showed him the sea serpent. **<a href="../People/Mr_Cobley">Mr Cobley</a>** has fished the duck pond for thirty years for **Old Tom**, the biggest carp in the county, whom nobody has ever seen, which is how you know he is big. Effie writes to her pen pal that there is a sea serpent in it, and Mr Cobley has never once contradicted her, because he does not know that there isn't. They all looked at the pond for a while, and something under the water, very large and very slow, turned over, and the duck went up in the air and came down somewhere else and said so. Mr Cobley said, *Thirty years.* Spare said that it was quite big. Effie said, *I TOLD you.*

Then she showed him the post, which is a man in a red coat, and Spare said that he knew; he does theirs. Effie said that he did not drive the Two Hundred, and he said no, <a href="../People/Ott_Hoyle">Ott</a> does, he is not allowed to touch the wheel. He said that she was not nearly a princess, and that there was no volcano. They considered this, each of them, for a while. Then she asked whether he would still write, and he said that his mam says it does not have to be true. *Make the volcano go off.* They each wrote one there and then, on their knees, and the <a href="../People/The_Courier">courier</a> walked the four steps from one to the other and handed them over, and they read them at once, in front of each other, which you are not supposed to do.

<div class="marginalia">Mrs Throssell has minuted it: <i>Something arising. A Cotterill, from Upper Clanging. Our first visitor.</i> She sent me the minute when I asked, and a note to say that the volcano had gone off since, by letter, twice, and buried Mr Dibble, who is fine, and that Old Tom has not been seen again, which Mr Cobley says proves it.</div>
