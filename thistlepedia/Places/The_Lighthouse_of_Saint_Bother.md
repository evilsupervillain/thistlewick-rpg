<p class="lede">The <b>Lighthouse of Saint Bother</b> stands on a headland on the south coast, east of Nether Sopping, at the end of a road that goes nowhere else. No ship has passed the point since 1909. It is kept lit anyway, by the fourth generation of Bothers, because it can be seen from the crossroads on the north road, and it is what the ones coming home walk home by.</p>

## The keeper

**<a href="../People/Ferrety_Bother">Ferrety Bother</a>** keeps the light. "It's a family name. The saint was my great-grandmother and she'd have hated the fuss." There are tapestries of her on the ground floor: with a lantern, glaring at a ship, which looks very sorry; telling off a storm, which is leaving; and in a deckchair with her feet up and the lamp lit, with, embroidered underneath by a great-grandson, SHE'D HAVE HATED THE FUSS. Her book is on the top floor, *Lighthouses I Have Known*, by S. Bother. It is a very short book. She only knew the one, and she did not think much of the others anyway.

He has been up the stairs eleven thousand times, and would like somebody else to do it once.

## The light

The Guild paid a stipend for the light until the shipping stopped, which was ninety-one years before the Quest. The keepers' ship-in-a-bottle shelf, one bottle for every ship that passed the point, ends in 1909. After that the bottles go on, one a year, each with a tiny lighthouse in it, lit. The ships book has, under the last ship, in the same hand, again and again down four hundred pages: *no ship. light lit.* Before that, in the older books: *ship. light lit. ship waved.* And before that, in a much older hand: *got her in.*

"It isn't for the ships. Hasn't been for four generations. It's for the ones coming back down the north road at night. You can see this light from the crossroads. It's what you walk home by."

Forty-seven times that light was lit for somebody. It was lit for nobody a great deal more often than that.

In the spring before the Quest the oil ran out, and Mrs Barrow at Wick & Barrow had oil, and also a price, and there was no stipend. For the rest of that spring and the summer after it, there was no light at the point. The Chandler's in Nether Sopping, which keeps its hours by the light, was shut the whole time.

In the year 2000 the Forty-Eighth's company bought a gallon of oil from Mrs Barrow, who charged them, and carried it out to the point, and up all the stairs, and lit the lamp. The brass went from cold to warm to something you have to look away from, and out through the glass the whole south coast got an edge on it. Ferrety Bother pressed a keeper's lamp on them at the bottom of the stairs without a word, which from him is a speech.

The Prophecy's tally of the Quest records it: *The Lighthouse of Saint Bother is lit again. You can see it from the crossroads, which is the whole of what it is for.*

## The tower

The lighthouse is six floors, with the lamp at the top. The rooms are kept the way the Bothers have always kept them. A selection:

* Plates for thirty, from when the light had a crew. Ferrety uses one plate, and washes it, and dusts the other twenty-nine on Sundays.
* Barrels marked LAMP OIL, OIL, OIL, OIL and OIL, every one hollow all the way down.
* A jar of old wicks, trimmed square, each tagged with its year. The wick in the lamp is trimmed square every week, regardless.
* Crates of spare lenses, packed in straw, for a lamp that has had the same lens since it was built and is not about to change now.
* Ship's biscuit, in a crate stencilled 1908. It is the same biscuit they sell in Nether Sopping, and very probably the same batch.
* A message in a bottle, legible through the glass: IF FOUND, PLEASE RETURN TO THE LIGHTHOUSE. It is in the lighthouse. Somebody has, technically, returned it.
* Ferrety's wardrobe, which contains a very large number of identical jumpers, all of them with a lighthouse on.
* *The Keeper's Handbook*. Rule one: keep the light lit. There is no rule two. There has never been any need for one.
* *A Book of Coast Birds*, kept by four generations of Bothers. Every entry says GULL. One says NOT A GULL (?). The next says GULL.
* A book of ghost stories for keepers on long nights, every one of which ends with the keeper lighting the lamp and the ghost going home, satisfied.
* A chart of the coast with every rock and wreck in red ink and every ship that ever got past in green. There is a great deal more green than red. Somebody was very good at this.
* Almanacs of the sky, one a year, for working out when it will be dark enough to bother with the light. This is the family joke, and they have made it every year for a hundred years.

<div class="marginalia">Ferrety Bother told the Forty-Eighth that he had walked out to the crossroads at midnight once, to check, and stood there, and looked back. I asked him, when I went out to see the light for this article, whether he had been again since it was relit. He said no. He said he did not need to. Then he said that he might, one night, if somebody was coming home. I said I did not think anybody was. He said that you never knew, and that that was the whole of the job.</div>

## Since the Quest

The lamp needed oil again in the spring of 2001, and somebody got it, now that somebody has. The light has not gone out since. The Chandler's is open.
