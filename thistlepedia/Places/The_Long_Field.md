# The Long Field

<p class="contributor">Contributed by O. Hoyle, of the Hoyle Works, Upper Clanging</p>

<p class="hatnote">I asked Miss Hoyle whether she would read over what I had written about the Long Field. She sent it back with a line through all of it and this memorandum attached, which I print whole. The field is hers. For the town above it, see <a href="Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>. For the works, see <a class="new" href="../Institutions/The_Hoyle_Works">The Hoyle Works</a>. - H.B.</p>

THE HOYLE WORKS. MEMORANDUM.

TO: Mr H. Bellwether, Nether Sopping. FROM: O. Hoyle. RE: Your draft, "The Long Field". DATE: 14 March 2002.

1. Your draft is returned. It is very kind, and it says "graveyard" four times. It is not a graveyard. It is a record. A record is a kindness to whoever stands here in a hundred years, and it has to be right, so I have written it again.

2. **The field.** The Long Field is where the works keeps its attempts. Every airship this works has built since the first has come down, and when one comes down we bring her back here, or what there is of her, and give her a plot and a plate. The plates say where she was lost and why. That is all they say. A plate is not a place for feelings. People who want those may scratch them underneath, and some have.

3. **The gatepost.** The gatepost reads THE HOYLE WORKS, and under it, on a smaller plate: ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE ATTEMPTS. ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVEN ARE HERE. The second number has been re-cut four times. I re-cut it. Somebody has to keep it right.

4. **The plates.** There are twelve that may be read without moving ivy. Visitors are asked to read all of them, and in the order given, and nobody does. In the order given:

<div class="stamp">ATTEMPT 3. LOST IN THIS FIELD. CAUSE: NEVER LEFT IT.</div>

4.1. The oldest plate. The only one that gives a distance. The distance is eleven feet.

4.2. ATTEMPT 41. LOST OVER THE WEST ROAD. CAUSE: ENVELOPE, SEAM. Somebody has scratched underneath, in a better mood than the plate: *the seam was fine. so was he. he walked home.* I have checked the seam. The seam was not fine. He did walk home.

4.3. ATTEMPT 60. LOST OVER THE GLOAMWOOD. CAUSE: BALLAST, RELEASED IN ERROR. Under it, smaller: ALL HANDS RECOVERED. ONE ANNOYED.

4.4. ATTEMPT 84. LOST OVER THE LONG FIELD. CAUSE: blank. The only blank cause line in the field. See paragraph 6.

4.5. ATTEMPT 101. LOST OVER THE SEA. CAUSE: FIRE, WHICH WAS EXPECTED. The works wrote FIRE on her flight plan before she left the shed, in the box headed ANTICIPATED DIFFICULTIES. Nobody has ever said that we do not plan.

4.6. ATTEMPT 123. LOST OVER THE STANDING STONES. CAUSE: FIRE, WHICH WAS NOT.

4.7. ATTEMPT 137. LOST OVER THE GLOAMWOOD. CAUSE: FRACTURE, MAIN SPAR.

4.8. ATTEMPT 138. LOST OVER THE GLOAMWOOD. CAUSE: FRACTURE, MAIN SPAR (DIFFERENT SPAR). Eleven months apart. I do not wish to discuss the eleven months.

4.9. ATTEMPT 155. LOST IN SIGHT OF THE TOWER. CAUSE: WIND. Somebody underlined WIND twice. Somebody else, later, put a small question mark beside it. The question mark is mine.

4.10. ATTEMPT 176. LOST IN SIGHT OF THE TOWER. CAUSE: NO FAULT FOUND. Every system was recovered, stripped and tested. Every system passed.

4.11. ATTEMPT 184. LOST IN SIGHT OF THE TOWER. CAUSE: UNDER REVIEW. The first plate in this field to say so.

4.12. ATTEMPT 199. LOST IN SIGHT OF THE TOWER. CAUSE: UNDER REVIEW. Eleven years under review in 2000. Her plot is empty. See paragraph 7.

5. **Things in the grass.** A propeller blade, half sunk, polished on the side that faces the sky; the other side is the field's. A strip of envelope cloth on a bush, faded to the colour of the grass, which lifts in the wind, the way it was made to. A twist of brass too small for a plate of its own, with a tag on it: ONE OF THEM. KEPT. A single rivet, with a ring of small white stones round it so that nobody treads on it. The stones are Mr Sowerby's. He will not be told to move them and nobody has tried.

6. **Attempt Eighty-Four.** Came down in 1860, in her own plot, and did not stay there. She made herself good out of the rows either side of her, a piece at a time, for a hundred and forty years, and went north up the field doing it, and left a furrow behind her three feet deep and as wide as her plot. Nobody taught her that. Nobody could have. At the top of the field she got a foot off the ground, and then did not, and then tried again, and was still trying in the year 2000.

6.1. She was stopped. It was the Forty-Eighth who stopped her, and I came down from the works afterwards. She got further than any of them. I have not tried very hard to work out how she was still doing it, and I am not going to.

6.2. Her governor came off with two turns of a spanner and was given to the Forty-Eighth, rather than taken. She never needed it. She did not once run away with herself in a hundred and forty years, and I have a field to prove it.

6.3. There is a bay in the shed with 84 over it. For a hundred and forty years there was nothing under it. There is now. It will take years. Nobody has costed it and nobody has asked to.

6.4. I had told a great many people that there would not be a two hundred and one. The keel of the Two Hundred and First was laid in 2001. I have told the same people.

7. **Attempt 199.** She is not in the field. She is on a crag in the north-west, at the end of a track the works keeps cut, right way up, and every system aboard her still works. Pressure in her. Water in the glass. Lubricators full, bearings cool. The clock in the wheelhouse is a good clock and was serviced that spring; somebody walks out twice a year and winds it and sets it right, and it keeps perfect time, and it is at nine forty again when you go back. It has been set right eleven times. The log is open on the chart table:

<div class="stamp">0938. TOWER IN SIGHT. ALL WELL. 0939. ALL WELL. 0940. 0947. ON THE CRAG. ALL HANDS. NO INJURIES. NOTHING TO REPORT BETWEEN 0940 AND 0947.</div>

7.1. The last line is in the other hand, the steadier one: WE ARE WALKING HOME. They did. All four.

7.2. ITEM 1 was aboard her, chocked and strapped down four ways. It is not aboard her now. ITEM 1 is not the subject of this memorandum.

8. **Attempt 112.** One hundred years dead on a sea stack in the north-western approaches, which nobody could get out to, so there was no plate, so there was a gap in the row between 101 and 123. Whoever kept this field before me left the space, and put a post in it with two brass bolts four inches apart and nothing between them, and behind the bolts a card:

<div class="stamp">ATTEMPT 112. LOST. CAUSE: LOST.</div>

8.1. Every other plate names the part that failed. Nobody ever got to her to find out which part. Somebody wrote it out correctly in five words and would not put up a wrong one instead.

8.2. In 2000 the Two Hundred flew out to the stack, and her number-plate came off in one turn of a spanner, and it is on the bolts now. It fits. Of course it fits. That is the thirteenth plate. There are twelve readable plates in this field, and the thirteenth is one you make, and it does not count, because twelve is twelve.

9. **The Two Hundred.** The two hundredth attempt reached the tower and landed adjacent. She is not in this field and is not going to be. The attempt log reads REACHED THE TOWER. LANDED ADJACENT. CAUSE: UNDER REVIEW. Mr Bellwether has asked me what is under review. It is under review.

10. **For the record.** Nobody reads the field. The field is for us. If you have read all of it, you may come up to the works and say so, and I will believe you.

O. HOYLE.

<div class="marginalia">I have been to the field, and read all of it, and went up to the works and said so. Miss Hoyle believed me. She said that nobody reads the field, and then asked me which plate I had liked best, and I said Attempt 41, and she said that the seam was not fine, and that he did walk home, and that it was a long way.</div>
