<p class="lede"><b>Thistlewick</b> is a turnip-farming village at the bottom of the north road, and the signatory of the <a href="../History/The_Prophecy_of_Thistlewick">Prophecy</a>. Every hundred years, by the Committee's reckoning of a hundred years, it chooses one of its own and sends him north to the <a href="The_Obligatory_Tower">Obligatory Tower</a> to put down the <a href="../People/Grimspite_the_Inevitable">Dark Lord</a>. It has done so forty-eight times, and it worked forty-seven times, and the forty-eighth time it worked rather better than anybody had arranged.</p>

Thistlewick believes that it is the middle of the world. It is not; it is about a third of the way up the left-hand side of it, and the world goes on in every direction for a great deal further than the village has ever been told. Until the year 2000 nobody in Thistlewick had ever mentioned that there is a town on the south coast. There is. It is forty miles away, and it is where Thistlewick's heroes go afterwards (see <a href="Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a>, and the gradient described under <a href="../History/Two_Reckonings">Two Reckonings</a>).

## The village

**The north gate** stands at the top of the square, and has stood open for as long as anybody can remember. The Gatekeeper has waved forty-seven Chosen Ones through it, and looks north for a while when he says so, and does not say how many came back. He will not let a Chosen One through alone, under section four, subsection b: a Chosen One travels with a party, minimum one other person. "It's about the optics." The gate turned out to have a key, which had been in its lock, and which <a href="../People/Nix">Nix</a> had had for eleven years. It hangs on a nail beside the gate now, where it will be very safe. Just outside it is a sign, POINT OF NO RETURN, to which somebody has added in smaller letters that you can come back whenever, they just like the sign.

Sir <a href="../People/Sir_Aldric_Pemberton-Gore_III">Aldric Pemberton-Gore III</a> spent six years looking for the north gate, which is forty paces north of where he stood and has never moved. He is the subject of a notice on the board. See his article, and the date of 9 June 2001.

**The square** has a well, which a sign says is NOT A WISHING WELL, and a water butt beside it which a second sign says is also not a wishing barrel. There is a coin at the bottom of the water butt anyway. Thistlewick is a village of optimists, and of people who do not read notices. There is a market stall (FRESH! MOSTLY!), whose fish, on ice, are CAUGHT LOCALLY, although the only water in Thistlewick is the pond. There is a crate stencilled THIS WAY UP with the arrow pointing at the ground, which was stacked by somebody who trusted the arrow.

**The fingerpost** reads: NORTH, the Obligatory Tower, 200 leagues; SOUTH, the turnip fields, 40 paces; EAST, the pond, there are no fish in it; WEST, also the turnip fields. Somebody has scratched out the 200 and written FEELS LIKE MORE.

**The notice board** has had the same notices on it for as long as I have been asking about it: a goat lost, which answers to Councillor; a Chosen One wanted, position filled; the well; and Sir A. Pemberton-Gore, missing, six years, if found point him at the north gate, he will not go, point anyway. In the year 2000 it carried, for one week, a notice of a farewell tea for B. Thistle, the Forty-Eighth, Friday, bring a plate. "There is never anything new," says the board, and is wrong only that once.

**The pond** has no fish in it. The Fisher has fished it for thirty-one years. "You know what keeps a man going, lad? Not catching anything, and coming back anyway." The Forty-Eighth has said since that it was the best advice anybody gave him that day. The Fisher has a keep net now, which Nix returned to him, and he put it into the water with enormous ceremony, and it is empty, and it is somehow less empty than the pond was.

**The turnip fields** go round the village on three sides. One row has a tiny flag in it, very old, KEPT BACK FOR THE CHOSEN ONE'S SUPPER; they plant a fresh turnip under it every spring, just in case, and it has been very good for the turnips. Another row has a small tired sign asking the public not to pull the turnips up to see if one of them is a sword in a stone. NONE OF THEM IS. WE HAVE CHECKED. ALL OF THEM. The scarecrow wears Bram's old coat, and a note pinned to it: CHOSEN ONE (RESERVE). One of the Thistle rows has a neat hole in it with a label on a stick, and the creature that lives there is in the [Bestiary](../Bestiary/) under <a href="../Bestiary/Bramus_agricola">Bramus agricola</a>.

## The people

The village has two hundred or so souls, and the Quest met a great many of them on its first morning, which is the morning they all came out to look. I give the ones I have been able to speak to.

* **The Old Man**, whose boy was the Forty-Seventh. "Everyone tells you it's an honour. Nobody tells you it's a rota. Come back, lad. That's all. Come back." He waits at the edge of the square. Since Nix brought his kitchen chair back he waits sitting down, which is better for waiting. When the Forty-Eighth came back he took his hand and held on to it for a while.
* **The Farmer**, who asked who was doing the west field, and is still of the opinion that the weevils were the more pressing problem. There is a ploughshare at the end of the west field now, which Hob made, and the weevils have kept their distance from it for the first year in ten.
* **The Gossip**, who will tell you that the Forty-Eighth was chosen on the second vote, and that the first vote was the goat. The goat abstained. Very dignified about it.
* **The Dog**, and his **Neighbour**, who interprets him. The dog has not made a sound in my presence. The Neighbour says that he says to take a coat.
* **A Child**, who has heard that there is a DUNGEON, to the NORTH, and will tell you, and will tell you again.
* **<a href="../People/Mrs_Thistle">Mrs Thistle</a>**, Bram's mother, who is alive, and was making a pie (see <a href="../People/Bram_Thistle">Bram Thistle</a>).

**The goat** is Councillor, and is the second of the name. The first is buried in the churchyard under a small stone: IN MEMORY OF COUNCILLOR (THE FIRST). A GOAT. SERVED THREE TERMS. NEVER ONCE SPOKE OUT OF TURN. The present Councillor is sometimes seen standing beside it, looking thoughtful, and eating the flowers. He wears a good iron bell, which Hob made, and declined a brass one. He abstained from it.

## The buildings

* **<a href="Prophecy_Hall">Prophecy Hall</a>**, the gold one, up the road, where the Committee sits. It is always in session. It is how they know they exist.
* **The Chapel of Whatever Works**, with a teapot over the door on a bracket, because the Order never chose a symbol: it held a meeting about one, and somebody made tea, and that was the meeting. On cold mornings it steams. Inside there is a basin, FREE, DRINK IT, YOU ARE DEHYDRATED, YES YOU, and a pew RESERVED FOR THE TIRED, and <a href="../People/Merribell">Merribell</a>. See <a href="../Institutions/The_Order_of_Whatever_Works">The Order of Whatever Works</a>.
* **<a href="The_Gilded_Turnip">The Gilded Turnip</a>**, the inn, under a tin tankard that creaks in the rhythm of its favourite song.
* **<a href="Nixs_Emporium">Nix's Emporium</a>**, EST. RECENTLY, which buys anything and asks nothing.
* **Grumnir's Smithy**, under a hammer on a bracket which is just a hammer, and is not Beatrice, because Beatrice would never be left out in the rain. It is <a href="../People/Hob_Grumnir">Hob Grumnir</a>'s, and his article is the place for it. The window is black with soot except for one clean circle about the size of a nose. In it now, framed, is a written apology from an apprentice bandit, in a very good hand, next to nothing Hob made.
* **The Thistles' house**, at the bottom of the lane, with geraniums on the sill and a turnip in a pot that is being grown as an experiment and has not done anything you would call an experiment. Over the fireplace is a clean rectangle of wall where a sword used to be.

## The churchyard

The churchyard is behind the chapel, through a gate that used to stick. You had to lift it, and shove, and it scraped. It does not stick now. It was hung by Hob Grumnir in the year 2000, in good iron, true on its hangings, and the latch drops shut under its own weight. Somebody says the latch wants a spring. It has one now.

The stones are worth an afternoon:

* BEATRICE GRUMNIR. SMITH. 1919 - 1989. The parish's own plain headstone. The grass round it is cut every week, and nobody says who.
* MABEL THISTLE, 1890 - 1979, the Forty-Eighth's great-grandmother. SHE GREW A TURNIP AS BIG AS A CHAIR, AND SAT ON IT, AND THEN IT WAS THE CHAIR. The chair is still in the family. Mrs Thistle uses it for the good visitors. I have sat on it.
* ELSPETH MOORE, 1902 - 1994, who won the turnip show forty-one years running. Underneath, in smaller and much angrier capitals: THE FORTY-SECOND WAS FIXED. Somebody leaves a rosette on her stone that says SECOND.
* OSWIN FARROW. HE SAID IT WAS PROBABLY FINE. There is a hard hat on the stone.
* WALTER PIKE. HE KNEW A SHORTCUT. There is a little arrow carved under it, pointing at the hedge. Nobody has ever been brave enough.
* Councillor (the First), as above.

## Since the Quest

The village has not changed very much, which is what it would want me to say. The fields are the same fields, and the 2001 harvest was the best in the parish. The Committee asked whether it had been prophesied. It had not. The Prophecy is kept rolled in the Hall, with clauses twelve and nineteen struck, and is consulted at every meeting, out of habit, and the consultation is minuted.

The north gate stands open. Nobody has gone through it on the Committee's business since the year 2000, and nobody needs to: clause twelve is struck, and in its place there is a note in the minute book reading SEE BRAM. Nobody has needed to yet. The Forty-Eighth lives where he always lived, two fields from the square. He walks north once a year, on the day, because there is a kettle on at the top, and he comes back.

Clause nineteen is struck as well, and so the next person to come home to Thistlewick from anywhere will come home to Thistlewick, which is how most villages manage it.

<div class="marginalia">I asked the Gatekeeper, on my visit, whether the gate would be shut now that nobody need go through it. He looked north for a while. Then he said that it had been open for forty-seven of them and it could stay open for whoever was next, and that it was not a gate for keeping people in. I wrote that down. Then I asked him how many had come back. He said, "All of them, I'm told." And then, after a while, "One of them came back here."</div>
