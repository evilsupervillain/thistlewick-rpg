<p class="lede"><b>Olde Evermore</b> is the hidden village of the elves, at the foot of a high ledge in the south-west, where it has been since the First Age. Every visitor arrives by falling off the ledge into a heap of leaves, which is raked up again after each arrival. There is a perfectly good path out. Nobody has ever come in by it. Nearly every Chosen One has found the village eventually, and every one of them found it the same way.</p>

## Arriving

The village sign reads OLDE EVERMORE. HIDDEN VILLAGE OF THE ELVES. SINCE THE FIRST AGE, and under it, smaller: VISITORS ARE ASKED TO ARRIVE BY THE LEDGE, AS IS TRADITIONAL. At the bottom of the ledge there is a second sign: ARRIVALS. The leaves under it are raked into a deep, soft heap every morning and fluffed up again after each arrival, and it is somebody's whole job. He will tell you to mind the leaves, he has just done them.

Beside the heap stands **Thalion**, Judge of Arrivals, with a little book. He has judged every arrival off the ledge since the First Age, out of six, and is the whole panel, and there is no appeal. The scores have been very consistent. He gave the Forty-Eighth five point eight: the arms were lovely; he lost it on the landing; everybody loses it on the landing. The best score on record is the Forty-Seventh's, five point nine, twelve years ago. He landed on his feet. Thalion was very moved. In a window across the green there is stained glass of an elf falling off a ledge, gracefully, and below her a second elf holding up a card that says 6.0. Nobody has ever been given a six. It is a very old window, and a very hopeful one.

<div class="marginalia">I have not been to Olde Evermore. I have been to the top of the ledge, and looked over it, and come home. I am told the leaves are very soft. I am a historian, and I do not need to be judged.</div>

## The village

**Aelwen** sings to the well in the Old Tongue. It is the *Lay of the Chosen*, with a verse for every Chosen One who has come off the ledge, and it is three days long, sung properly, and nobody has ever heard the end of it, because she keeps having to add verses. The Forty-Sixth never came at all, so his verse is a rest, four bars long, and she says it is the best bit. She said that she would write the Forty-Eighth's when she had seen how it ended. I have asked whether she has. I am told that she has, and that it is not finished, and that it does not have to be yet.

**Calder** stands at the edge of the pond, with enormous patience. There is one fish in it, and he has been after it since the Second Age, and has caught it twice, and put it back both times, because then what would he do all day? It has a name. He will not tell you it. It would be insufferable.

**Ninian** sweeps the path out. Nobody has ever come in by it, so it is always very clean. He sweeps it because somebody might go out by it one day, and you would want it nice for them. When the Forty-Eighth said that he would be going out by it, Ninian looked delighted and swept a little faster.

**Elowen**, an old elf at her front door, is knitting something very small, very fast. It is a tea cosy with two little sleeves, and a large B on the front. B for Bryony. Or Bronwen. It is not for anything else. Asked about the sleeves, she says it is for a very small teapot. They get cold. Her daughter is **Elder Lirien**, who has been married to **<a href="../People/Elder_Faelan">Elder Faelan</a>** since the Second Age. Elowen had stopped knitting.

The rest of the village is beautifully kept. There is a room in which everything has been carved, including, as far as can be told, the soap. There are mushrooms growing in a perfect spiral, which in Olde Evermore counts as untidy, and somebody has already been told. There is a carved elven door with no handle, no lock and no keyhole, which opens for those it expects. It has not expected anybody for four thousand years, and has got very good at it. There is a front door in a knitted cosy, and so is the knocker, and so is the handle, and so, very likely, is the key, which would be why nobody can find it.

## The Forbidden Archive

The notice by the Archive door reads: THE FORBIDDEN ARCHIVE. THE ORACLE RECEIVES VISITORS UNTIL A QUARTER PAST SEVEN. BEDTIME IS HALF PAST. THIS IS NOT A PROPHECY. Under it, in a very careful hand, somebody small has added: IT IS NOT HALF PAST YET.

Inside, on a stepstool, stands **Mirabel**, the Last Oracle of Olde Evermore, a very small girl with long silver hair and a silver circlet, who is four thousand one hundred and twelve, and likes the quarter mentioned. She prophesies in the Old Tongue. It is flawless, and it is terrible, and every candle in the Archive leans towards her, and Elder Lirien translates: *When the moon devours the sun, and the last bell falls silent, the one who was not chosen shall-* and then Mirabel asks whether that is a biscuit. It is always a biscuit. She has been doing that one since the Second Age, and it never gets finished, and nobody seems to mind. The shelf of discarded prophecies has it on top, AND THE ONE WHO WAS NOT CHOSEN SHALL, and then a jam stain.

**Elder Lirien** translates for Grandmother, when she prophesies, and when she asks for pudding, and the pudding is more urgent. **Elder Faelan** fetches things from the top shelf, which is where the *Chronicle of the Second Age* is kept, so that she cannot reach it, because she reads the endings first. Everybody calls her Grandmother. She was old when Faelan's grandmother was a girl, and his grandmother was a girl for a long time.

Faelan is the source for a sentence I have quoted elsewhere in this volume and will quote again: the Committee counts the way the <a href="../History/The_Prophecy_of_Thistlewick">Prophecy</a> does; the elves count the way the sun does. The Forty-Seventh came off the ledge ten years before the Forty-Eighth, he said, and not a hundred, and when the Forty-Eighth said that the Age began with the Prophecy, Faelan said that the Age began with a great many things, and that the Prophecy was the one with a clerk present. See <a href="../History/Two_Reckonings">Two Reckonings</a>.

**The horse.** Mirabel was at the signing of the Prophecy of Thistlewick. It was ever so long, and everybody had to sign it three times, so she drew a horse on it. Elder Faelan has views about that, which are that it was in the margin of a sacred and binding instrument. She says it is a good horse. The Archive has a fair copy of the Prophecy, made at the signing, under a sign that says FORBIDDEN in four languages; the copyist was told to copy everything, and he copied everything, and in the margin beside clause twelve, very small and in wax crayon, there is a horse. It is quite a good horse.

Asked who the villain really is, Mirabel said that she knows, and is not allowed to say, and would draw it. She drew for a long time with her tongue out of the corner of her mouth, and then showed the Forty-Eighth, and it was a horse. He said carefully that it was a very good horse. She said that it was not a horse, it was the villain, and that she was only very good at horses. There is a picture book on the forbidden shelf in which every page is the same horse, and on the cover, in the Old Tongue: THE VILLAIN.

**The secret.** Mirabel also told the Forty-Eighth a real secret, not a prophecy, which is that Elder Lirien is going to have a baby. Across the Archive, Elder Lirien dropped a scroll, and at the foot of the ladder Elder Faelan stopped moving altogether. Mirabel had foreseen it, and told them at breakfast, before they knew. She had known since the ninth of March. It is a girl, and she is going to be called Biscuit. Elder Faelan says that she is not going to be called Biscuit. Mirabel says she is, though. I am told that neither Elder could stop smiling, which was making the colour worse.

**The clock.** The Archive clock is tall and beautifully kept, and says a quarter past seven. The pendulum is not moving. It has been tied to the side of the case with a hair ribbon, in a very neat bow. Mirabel's bed is in the corner, turned down, with a stone hot-water bottle already in it, and on the pillow a note in the Old Tongue, flawless and terrible, which Elder Lirien translates without looking up: *Not tired.*

**The copyist** sits behind a desk, copying a book in a beautiful hand, very slowly. He was told to copy everything, so he copies everything, and at present he is copying the catalogue of everything he has copied, which is very nearly up to date. Everything means the margins too. Nobody has told him to stop. He has been listening very carefully.
