<p class="lede">The <b>Gilded Turnip</b> is the inn on the square at <a href="Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>, warm and brown and loud, kept by <b><a href="../People/Prudence_Thrupp">Prudence Thrupp</a></b>. Three of the Forty-Eighth's companions were found in it, one of them on a table. It is named after a turnip on a plinth, painted gold, which a small plaque says is not a metaphor.</p>

Over the door hangs a tin tankard on a bracket, which creaks when the wind blows in exactly the rhythm of the inn's favourite song, and has taught it to three generations of the village. The song is *Turnips in the Morning*. It is a good tune. Nobody knew that until a professional played it properly.

## The house

**The turnip.** A single turnip on a plinth, painted gold, with a plaque: THE GILDED TURNIP. It is not a metaphor. It is just a turnip somebody painted. It is also, in the words of the inn's most famous guest, "the problem", and in the year 2000 the whole of the Forty-Eighth's company sat down in front of it for an afternoon to find it a rhyme. There is none. The afternoon is described under <a href="../People/Piper_Quill">Piper Quill</a>, and lasted until Prudence stopped bringing tea and brought soup, out of pity. The last verse of the ballad was finished at this table, in nine seconds, by moving the turnip down a line:

<div class="verse">
<p>and sat down with a turnip, which<br>
was worth the journey, and the wait.</p>
<span class="attribution">P. Quill, the ballad, its last verse (in part)</span>
</div>

**The piano.** An upright, with a sign on the lid: PLEASE DO NOT PLAY THE PIANO, and under it, smaller, THIS MEANS YOU, BRAM. The Forty-Eighth knows one tune, with one finger. The company between them have played everything else on it: Sir Aldric a stirring march that turns into a different stirring march and back; Hob, hard and in time, taking two keys off and putting them back on better than they were; Zephyrine a scale, after which the piano smelt faintly of toast for the rest of the evening. Middle C is still a bit warm.

**The shelves.** Sacks and boxes of turnip, labelled by year like wine. The '87 is said to be very good. The '91 is not spoken of. Behind the bar, jars and bottles each with a regular's name painted on: REGULAR, REGULAR, REGULAR, REGULAR, and at the end, PIPER (NOT ON THE TABLE). There is a big lidded pot labelled POT, so that everybody knows, with a second label inside it that says INSIDE OF POT. There is Prudence's own cordial, elderflower and turnip, which is better than it sounds. It would more or less have to be.

**The rooms.** A big bed, made up very tightly. In stories, heroes lie down on any bed they find and wake up feeling much better. Prudence will be watching you, and it is twenty crowns. The card on the bedside table asks guests to leave the room as they would wish to find it; underneath it somebody has written ON FIRE?, initialled Z.V. "It was a question," I was told. "It was a genuine question." There is a washstand with a towel folded by somebody who folds towels properly.

**The register.** The most recent entry, for six years, was SIR A. PEMBERTON-GORE. ONE NIGHT. Every night after it he wrote DITTO. "One keeps records. One never knows where the gate will be in the morning."

**The counter.** NO CREDIT. NO SINGING ON TABLES. NO CHOSEN ONES ON CREDIT EITHER. THAT MEANS YOU, BRAM. There is a lot in this inn that means you, Bram.

## The regulars

**Prudence Thrupp** keeps the house, and has kept it for longer than she will say, and her sister **Dorcas** keeps the <a href="The_Slain_Wyvern">Slain Wyvern</a>, forty miles south, which nobody in Thistlewick had mentioned. They did not speak for thirty years. "That's not a falling-out. We just stopped one day and neither of us started again. So we send each other things instead." In the year 2000 the Forty-Eighth carried a jar south for her, warm on one side, which he was told not to shake or ask about, and carried a reply back north which was heavier, and deliberately worse, and had been under construction for some time. Prudence laughed. She has written since: four pages, two of them about the Forty-Eighth, and one of those complimentary. The sisters' locket, two portraits facing opposite ways, which had been in a drawer since they were girls, went north on the Quest. "That was HER idea, that was."

**The Regular**, whose cousin was the Thirty-Ninth. Lovely tower, apparently. Since the ending, lovely coast, apparently.

**The Gloamwood Society**, which meets at the table by the door on Thursdays: nine members, forty years, a walk once a year, and a quarterly, *The Sighting*, edited by Mr Dunmore. Mrs Lumb does the talking. Miss Pellow saw the Thing in the Woods in 1971, and counted nine, and was the only one who got it right. The Society's long work came to its conclusion in the year 2000, with the help of Miss Halloway, and is described under <a href="The_Gloamwood">The Gloamwood</a>.

**Zephyrine Vance** drank at the table by the window for eleven years, being just passing through, like somebody waiting for a letter. The letter came, on thick cream paper with a purple seal, and Prudence propped it against the glass where she would see it: the Collegium Arcanum's eleventh notice of outstanding fines. See her article. The table is still hers when she wants it. She does not sit at it like that any more. There is a scorch mark on the ceiling above it, which was a moth.

**Piper Quill** was standing on a table when the Forty-Eighth first came in, describing him, out loud, in the third person, as he approached. The tavern had not fallen silent. The tavern was ignoring her. Through the window from the square you can still hear somebody in there singing a song about the Forty-Eighth which rhymes *Bram* with *jam*. It is hers. The jam was a placeholder. It has tested very well.

<div class="marginalia">I stayed a night at the Gilded Turnip on my visit, at twenty crowns, and the breakfast was turnip. Prudence asked after her sister, and I said that she was well, and Prudence said "I didn't ask," and then asked again, more particularly, about the shelf.</div>
