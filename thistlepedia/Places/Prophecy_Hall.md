<p class="lede"><b>Prophecy Hall</b> is the seat of the <a href="../Institutions/The_Prophecy_Committee">Prophecy Committee</a> of <a href="Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>, and the place where every Chosen One since the first has been told what he is for. It has a crest, two pillars, forty-seven portraits, a great many shelves, a box of verses nobody may read, and a scroll that reaches the far wall.</p>

## Outside

Over the door is the village crest, a turnip rampant on a field of slightly different turnips, with the motto in Old Thistlewick: NON NOBIS, NISI FORTE. *Not us, unless it has to be.* It has been repainted so often that it is now mostly paint, and slightly more rampant.

Through one window you can see the Committee in session, and one of them will see you, and hold a form up to the glass, and the correct response, which every Chosen One has worked out for himself, is to duck. On the sill of the other window is a row of little clay figures made by the village school, one for each Chosen One. In the year 2000 a forty-eighth was begun. At first it was mostly turnip. Then it was given a sword, which it is holding by the wrong end, and the school has decided that it is finished.

## The Hall

The Elder stands at the far end, under a stained-glass window of the First Chosen One slaying the Dark Lord. The glazier has given the Dark Lord a very patient expression, like a man waiting for a bus he has been told is coming. I did not think anything of it until I had been to the top of the story, by letter; I think the glazier had been there in person.

**Elder <a href="../People/Elder_Wispel">Wispel</a>** gives the Prophecy to each Chosen One by unrolling it until it reaches the far wall, and then rolling it back up, which takes a while, and nobody helps. On his desk is a note in his own hand: BUY MORE SCROLL. It is underlined twice. Beside it is THE PROPHECY OF THISTLEWICK (ABRIDGED), which is exactly as long as the unabridged one, the abridging having been done by the Committee.

**The Committee's table** runs down one side. At one end sits the Clerk, with Form C-12, Roster Amendments, by which a Chosen One may strike a companion from the roster and send them home to think about it. Thistlewick personnel only. Anybody picked up south of the wall is Form C-12(S), which is held in Nether Sopping, by a man called Pell. "Not my circus." A companion struck off keeps the kit and the experience. It was tried the other way once. There is a form about why not.

At the other end sits a councillor whose whole contribution to the year 2000, so far as the minutes show, was to ask whether the Dark Lord *shall be slain* or *shall be defeated*, because *slain* is a commitment, and to countersign one form, as to the wording.

And there is **<a href="../People/Councillor_Fenn">Councillor Fenn</a>**, who has been saying for forty years that clause twelve is the problem. "That's not a warning. That's a subscription." Nobody listened to him until somebody asked him what he meant, and he said that nobody had ever asked, and sat them down, and began at the original wording, which goes back to a horse. He is still explaining it. He looks very well on it. The Committee's minute book for the week of the ending records, as item five, that Councillor Fenn wished it minuted that he had been saying so. Minuted.

## The Wall of the Forty-Seven

On the west wall: forty-seven portraits. Forty-six of them are looking at the viewer. One of them is looking at the door.

At the end of them, since the year 2000, there is a forty-eighth frame, new, and empty, with a card pinned in it:

<div class="stamp">PORTRAIT TO FOLLOW. SITTER AT THE COAST.</div>

The sitter is not at the coast. He is two fields away, and has been for two years. Nobody has changed the card. A second card, the Quills', is tucked into the corner of the frame to make room. It gives the official account of the Forty-Eighth, and who wrote it, and whether it was APPROVED or AMENDED, and on that subject I refer the reader to <a href="../People/Piper_Quill">Piper Quill</a>, and say no more.

## The shelves

The Committee's library is one of the wonders of the village, and is very nearly all minutes. A selection, from the shelves by the door:

* *The Meeting To Decide When To Hold The Meeting*, Volume IV.
* *Prophecy: A User's Guide*. Chapter One is "Have You Tried Being Chosen Again?"
* *Standing Orders of the Prophecy Committee*, Revised. It has been revised in pencil, on every page, by somebody who was outvoted on all of them.
* *The Forty-Seven: A Souvenir*. Every page has a portrait, a date and a pressed flower. The flowers are all turnip flowers.
* A dictionary, which has been opened at SLAIN and DEFEATED so often that it now falls open there by itself.
* *So You've Been Chosen: A Leaflet*. It is one page long, and says CONGRATULATIONS!, and then, in very much smaller print, CLAUSE FOUR APPLIES.
* *What To Do If The Chosen One Is Not In*. It recommends leaving a note, and choosing again if he has not replied by Thursday.
* The accounts of the Prophecy Fund. Income: the fete. Expenditure: swords, forty-seven; scroll, a great deal.
* A petition to rename the Obligatory Tower the Optional Tower, with one signature on it, Councillor Fenn's.

Under them is a pad of forms, of which the top one is **Form S-7**, Application to Consult Struck Material (Verse), to be countersigned as to the wording. And beside them, on the Committee Records, are the minutes of the meeting at which the Forty-Eighth was chosen, with his name under *apologies for absence*, and one goat.

## Struck material

The Fete Sub-Committee's minutes hang on the wall, and they are the most-read document in the Hall:

> Verses one to six: approved. Verse seven: struck. Verse eight: struck, and we would like a word. Verse nine was read aloud at the fete. In error. By Mrs Wispel. There were children present. There were GRANDPARENTS present. Motion to strike verses seven and eight: carried, four to one. The one against is Mrs Wispel.

Nobody has written down what verse seven was. Nobody is going to, and that includes me.

The Committee keeps what it strikes. Of course it does; it is a committee. Behind the red curtains in the east wall there is no window, but a door, STRUCK MATERIAL, NO ADMITTANCE WITHOUT FORM S-7, and the key to it hangs on Councillor Fenn's belt. Behind that are the struck shelves (*Struck: A History*, Volume One, which has been struck; *The Prophecy: Early Drafts*, in which the Dark Lord rises "every hundred years, or so", with OR SO crossed out and the crossing-out crossed out; *Names Considered for the Obligatory Tower*, which include the Compulsory Tower, the Mandatory Tower, the Tower You Have To, and the Tower (Sorry); and *Things Not To Read Aloud At The Fete*, inside the cover of which Mrs Wispel has written, NOW you tell me). And there is the box: the best box the Committee owns, gilded, with a red stone in the lid, full of two hundred years of struck verses, one to a slip, each dated and initialled.

Piper Quill has read every one of them, and printed none. Pinned under the Fete Sub-Committee's minutes now is a slip: *Struck material consulted, Form S-7*, initialled P.Q., in metre. In the box, one slip has been very slightly straightened.

## The minute book, and the lectern

The Committee's minute book lies open on the table at the week the Forty-Eighth came home:

> ITEM 3. Clause twelve. Struck, nem. con. In its place, a note: SEE BRAM.
>
> ITEM 4. Clause nineteen. Applies. Letter sent, cottage aired. Friday suggested.
>
> ITEM 5. Councillor Fenn wished it minuted that he had been saying so. Minuted.

Item four was itself struck six months later, after a very long meeting. I have the minutes of that meeting. It lasted eleven hours, and they are mostly about the biscuits.

By the door, since the ending, stands a lectern, and on it a ledger in the Committee's best hand, open at the first page, with a pen on a chain: **The Obligatory Quest, as amended**. It is the Prophecy's own tally of the Quest, read against the world as it now stands, and it is kept up to date. It is the only document in the Hall that admits it is not finished. It ends: *The account ends there, for the present. There is a good deal of ruled paper after it.*

<div class="marginalia">The account at the lectern says that I wrote a song about the Quest, from first-hand accounts, and that it is complete and not remotely accurate. I have written to the Committee to say that I wrote a history, and that it is in prose. The Clerk has stamped my letter and filed it. I am told it was filed under "no change".</div>
