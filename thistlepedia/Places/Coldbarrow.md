<p class="lede"><b>Coldbarrow</b> is the Chapter House of the Order of the Errant, a hall in the snow of the far north where the Order keeps a great map of the island with a pin in it for every knight in the field. It is kept by the Order's clerk, <b><a href="../People/Mordaunt">Mordaunt</a></b>, who reads the map the way other people read a will. One knight, one pin. That is the system.</p>

## The map

The great map is the island in the Order's own survey, four sheets wide. Each knight in the field is a white-headed pin, spread thin, standing where they were put. Until the year 2000 there were also nine red ones, all over it, for one knight, three of them labelled in the same hand: SAME WEEK. SEE COMMITTEE.

The knight was Sir Aldric Pemberton-Gore the Third. Six of his pins were correct, on different dates. Three of them were the same week: Frostwick, and Fenmarch, and a lay-by in the west. The Order had not lost his file. People said it had. It had nine of them, on the bottom shelf, nine fat volumes each labelled PEMBERTON-GORE, and each one certain that it was the real one. Mordaunt's ledger lay open at P, and every entry on the page had been crossed out and written in again somewhere else. He asked anybody who saw Sir Aldric to note the place, the date and the time, in ink. Not pencil. Pencil is how they got there.

## The committee

A committee was formed to review the map, the pins and the week. It sat on Thursdays, at a table with two chairs and two stools, all of them empty, because the committee was Mordaunt, appointed to it four times, and quorate. In the year 2000 Sir Aldric came in, and Mordaunt put his pins down on the desk very carefully, as if they might go off, and said that it was Thursday, and the committee would see him now, and sat in each chair and on each stool in turn and then stood behind all of them.

Its findings were three. Nine pins. All nine correct. Three of them the same week. The committee had been unable to fault the pins, and had therefore had to fault the knight, and Sir Aldric said that that was entirely fair. It recommended that he be requested to remain in one place for the duration of the review, and offered him Knight Commander of the Chapter, with a desk, there, and a pin that did not move, contingent on his remaining in one place.

It was the one thing he could not do, and the one thing he had always wanted to be asked. He said that it was the finest thing he had been offered in nine years, and thanked Mordaunt, and said no. A knight who stays put is a post. He should be at that desk by nine every morning, and he should never once find it twice. But Mordaunt had asked, and nobody asks a man to stay, and he would like that put in the minutes. In ink.

Mordaunt wrote for some time, and crossed nothing out, which the ledger on his desk would tell you was a first. Then he said that the committee had a data problem: the knight could not stay with the pin. Resolved, that the pin go with the knight. He took the nine red pins out of the map one at a time, and put eight of them in a heap on his desk, and held out the ninth. KNIGHT ERRANT, AT LARGE, a rank of the Order, created that Thursday. Wherever the knight is, the map is correct. That is the system now.

The card on the committee's table reads: REVIEW OF PINS (PEMBERTON-GORE): CONCLUDED. SIR A. INFORMED. OFFERED THE CHAPTER. DECLINED. And under it, smaller, in ink: *Beautifully.* (Minuted at his request.) On the great map there are no red pins now, and in the margin a small card in Mordaunt's hand: PEMBERTON-GORE. AT LARGE. PIN CARRIED.

<div class="marginalia">I wrote to Mordaunt to ask for the Order's records of Sir Aldric since the review. He replied that Sir Aldric is wherever he is, and that the map is correct. He enclosed a note from Sir Aldric's squire, the Fourth, saying that his uncle was here, and the date, and the time, in ink. Mordaunt says that he has had one of these every week for a year and a half, and that he keeps them in a tenth volume, and that it is the only one of the ten that agrees with itself.</div>

## The hall

There is stained glass of a knight kneeling, with the Order's motto on a scroll above him, too small to read from the floor and, one suspects, too long to fit. There is a suit of armour, very bright, with a card: CEREMONIAL. DO NOT GO ON A QUEST IN. There is another, ISSUE PATTERN, KNIGHTS ERRANT, ONE, and under it, in pencil, SIZES RUN SMALL. The Order's daybook is open on a stand: PINS REVIEWED; PINS REVIEWED; PINS REVIEWED (WITH TEA).

Of the Order's other knights in the field, one at least has a pin that has never once needed reviewing: <a href="../People/Dame_Winifred_Cope">Dame Winifred Cope</a>, at <a href="Little_Gore">Little Gore</a>, who writes every quarter NOTHING TO REPORT, and is written back to, VERY GOOD.
