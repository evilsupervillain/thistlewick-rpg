<p class="lede"><b>The Stair</b> goes down into the mountain from the Door at the top of <a href="Hithermost">Hithermost</a>, in the dark, for a great deal further than anyone in the village had been. It has a schedule of hazards nailed up inside the door, and chalk on its walls in a cheerful hand from one end nearly to the other. After the Quest the Forty-Eighth took a borrowed lamp and his companions and went down it, to the bottom, where nobody had been.</p>

## The schedule

Inside the Door, nailed up, is a board:

<div class="stamp">PARISH OF HITHERMOST. THE MOUNTAIN STAIR. SCHEDULE OF HAZARDS. BY ORDER. 1. The dark. 2. The draught (puts lamps out). 3. The echo (do not answer it). 4. Somebody coming up. 5. The warden of the half-way landing. 6. Anything else: NOT ANTICIPATED.</div>

Chalked under it, in a cheerful hand: *All present and correct. - D.* Chalked beside item six, in a different hand, in the year 2000: *Anticipated.*

Item one is the dark, and it is thorough. There is no light on the Stair but what you bring, and a little circle of it round you, and the rock, and the next flight. Item two puts lamps out, and the parish has hung hooks for spare lamps at intervals down the Stair, LAMP ON HOOK, and there is no lamp on any of them, and beside one of them is chalked *Borrowed this one too. Back directly. - D.*

## The chalk

From the top step down, the rock is chalked in the same cheerful hand, a D. and a number, each a little deeper than the last. I copy a selection, in order, from the Forty-Eighth's notes:

> D. 1. Went in. Had a look round. Grand day.
>
> D. 2. Bit further.
>
> D. 3. Bit further. Nothing happened.
>
> D. 14. Signed the Book first. Just in case.
>
> D. 21. Somebody coming up. Charming fellow. Couldn't see him. Lovely manners.
>
> D. 40. Nothing happened. Rather a lot of it.
>
> D. 61. TEA. (Beside a cup on a saucer, set down on the floor of the ledge. It is still warm. Nobody is drinking it.)
>
> D. 88. Lamp went out. Lit it again. Fine.
>
> D. 96. Asked the draught to shut the door. It did not.
>
> D. 100. HALF WAY! Tea.
>
> D. 101. Not half way.
>
> D. 102. Told the warden. He took it badly.
>
> D. 131. The echo. Said good afternoon. It said it back. Lovely manners.
>
> D. 150. Wet.
>
> D. 177. Still wet. Nothing happened.
>
> D. 211. Bit further than yesterday.
>
> D. 212. Bit further than yesterday. Back directly.

There are no marks below D. 212. The stair goes on down past it into the dark, and nobody has written on any of that.

The boulders are chalked too: *Good boulder. Sat on it*; *Second-best boulder*; an arrow pointing down, *That way, I expect*; TEA, with an arrow, and under it, *If there is any*; STILL GOING, rather bigger than usual. On a boulder just above D. 212 is a little drawing of the sun, with rays, and under it, *For later.*

Both sides of the question about Sir Dunstable have found evidence on this stair, and I have set it all out under <a href="Hithermost#sir-dunstable">Hithermost</a>. I would only add here that the chalk is very cheerful the whole way down, and that it is the same hand at the bottom as at the top.

## Half way

Over a landing some way down, cut into the rock in letters a foot high: HALF WAY. Under it, in chalk, much smaller: *NOT.*

At the head of the flight below it stood the **warden of the half-way landing**, in black armour, as still as the rock, with a hook on its belt and nothing on the hook. It said HALT. HALF WAY. NONE PASS THE HALF-WAY LANDING UNINSPECTED. The Forty-Eighth mentioned D. 101. The warden looked round, down the flight behind it, into the dark, for a long time, and said that it had been told, and had been told before, and had not taken it well. Then it said INSPECTION, and inspected them, which is a long story and is in the account. Afterwards it sat down on the top step, heavily, and then got up and moved out of the way, because it was in it.

It sits a little to one side of the landing now, and says *Not half way* to itself every so often, quietly, getting used to it. When anybody comes down it says HALT. HALF -, and then, *Oh. You again.*

## The post

Somewhere above the warden, on a stair under a mountain that had been shut for longer than anybody could remember, at the edge of the lamplight, there was a man in a red coat and a flat cap, with a satchel. Nobody said anything. Nobody could think of anything that would help. He said *Post?*, and Spare Cotterill said that he had one for Upper Clanging, and wrote it on his knee, and it did not take long:

> Dear Mam. Is it spring yet? Spare.

It was the first letter Spare had sent that was entirely true. The courier touched his cap to Spare and went on down the Stair, into the dark, without a lamp. Somebody asked how he had got in, and somebody else asked how he was going to see, and nobody had an answer to either. It was spring in Upper Clanging the next morning. See <a href="../People/Spare_Cotterill">Spare Cotterill</a>.

## Misfortune

A fight lost on the Stair does not end where fights end. The party comes back in through the porch of the Chapel of Continuance, whole, with the lamp, and signs the Book again, and Brother Quillon does not remember them, because the Order forbids it. The Forty-Eighth's party came back in through the porch at least once. Sir Dunstable met them in the chapel and asked whether they had come in by the porch, same as him, and said that nothing had happened, he expected.

## The bottom

Below D. 212 the stair goes on, for one more flight, and then it ends, and the dark stops all at once, like a door shutting behind you. The bottom of the Stair is a chamber with lamps on every ledge, dozens of them, and every one is lit.

There is somebody at the bottom, and it was very pleased to see them. It is not on the schedule, except as item six, and it is not in the Prophecy, and it asked. Nobody is obliged to fight it, and so nobody ever had, and it asked whether they would, only if they had the afternoon. The Forty-Eighth's party had the afternoon. It said afterwards that it was the best afternoon it had ever had, because nobody made them. It had written a bounty card for itself, a long time before, because nobody would put it on anything:

<div class="stamp">BOUNTY: SOMETHING UNSCHEDULED. Lives at the bottom of the Stair. Would love to meet you. Reward: on production of a witness.</div>

It was not official. It wanted a stamp, and it did not know where you would get one. You get one at the Adventurers' Guild in Nether Sopping, from Mr Pell, who had kept a Form Z, *Matters Not Anticipated*, in a drawer for forty years, in case, and had never once used it, because in forty years every matter had been anticipated, by somebody, on something. He filled it in. His hand was not quite steady. The bounty was discharged on Form Z, and there is a copy on the Guild's board, stamped DISCHARGED, and under it, *Witnessed. Form Z.* Then he took the Forty-Eighth's Guild Card, which said Provisional, and gave him one that does not.

After the fight it saw them up the Stair, all of it. It took no time at all, and afterwards none of them could say how. They were standing under the Door, and from somewhere a very long way down, something waved, and then said, oh, oh no, was that too much, it was sorry, it did not get the practice. Miss Halloway's entry on it is in the <a href="../Bestiary/">Bestiary</a>, and she has asked me not to call it a Thing, so I have not.

<div class="marginalia">The Forty-Eighth has the lamp still. Sir Dunstable has not asked for it back. I asked the Forty-Eighth whether he would go down again, and he said that he had been down three times since, on free afternoons, because it is a long way down for somebody to get no visitors, and that it always asks if they have the afternoon, and that it always says nobody made them.</div>
