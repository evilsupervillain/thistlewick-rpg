<p class="lede">The <b>Sable Hart</b> is an inn in the west where doomed rivals go to brood, and has been for longer than anybody there can say. There are four corners and five of them. You can see the difficulty. They have solved it with a rota.</p>

## The regulars

<p class="hatnote">See also <a href="../People/The_Rivals_of_the_Sable_Hart">The Rivals of the Sable Hart</a>, with their dates.</p>

They are, all of them, extremely nice. They tip beautifully and they put the chairs back. **Mrs Garrow**, who keeps the house, says they are no trouble, love: four in the corners and the young one by the fire, every night of the year, and the middle of the room empty as a church. A tinker sat in the middle once, in the rain. They were ever so nice to him. He left before his pie came. She buys the bottles with the gloomy labels because her regulars prefer them, and what is inside is elderflower cordial. The tankards on the shelf each have a small knitted hood over them, to keep the dust off.

**Hollin Crake**, Secretary, stands up when you come in, which nobody brooding in a corner has ever done, and asks whether you are anybody's rival at all. He has the Secretary's corner, by the window, because somebody has to be able to read the rota. His tankard sits on a coaster he brought from home, so as not to mark the table.

**Silas Veyle** has the darkest corner, until Monday, with a scar and a pint. The shadows are his. He is sorry; that came out very possessive. They are his until Monday, and then they are Rook's, and Rook has earned them. Don't ask about the scar. Nobody asks about the scar, and he looks, very briefly, disappointed. The curtain behind him is drawn, and there is no window behind it, and he says it makes a real difference. His pint is untouched; it is for atmosphere, and he orders a fresh one at nine, and leaves that too.

**Thaddeus Rook** keeps the table: points for a vow, a scar, a dramatic exit, and standing about in the rain. Standing in the rain is two points; standing in the rain on purpose is one; they are quite strict. A dramatic exit through a door that turns out to be a cupboard is minus two. He was fourth, in 2000, because he never gives himself the benefit of the doubt, which is the whole point of keeping the table. He is rival to a very great archer on the east coast who is ninety-three this year, and he is giving him every chance.

**Lucan Morrow** edits the newsletter, *Overshadowed*, which is free and always has been. He is rival to a Mr Pennock, a very great wizard, who does not know. Morrow writes to him at Christmas, and every year gets a lovely card back. Mr Pennock thinks he is his cousin.

**Kit Blackwood** is new, and first on the waiting list for a corner, any corner, and has been since March of 2000. Until he gets one he broods on a stool by the fire, in the warmest and brightest seat in the inn, as hard as he possibly can. It is quite hard to brood by a fire. He is getting better. Mr Crake says he has a gift for a long silence. He is nobody's rival yet. There is a list for that, too.

## The table

The league table is ruled in red and pinned up beside the rota. When the Forty-Eighth called it read: 1. S. VEYLE, 41 (the scar). 2. L. MORROW, 37 (a vow, in the rain). 3. H. CRAKE, 36. 4. T. ROOK, 35 (under review, by himself). The rest was under the rota, which was pinned over the bottom half with four pins and a fifth, to be sure. Under it were Blackwood, 12 (keen); eleven members by post, in order of their last letter; and, seventeenth of seventeen, C. ASH, 0 (no meetings attended).

Mr Ash had been a member for some years. He had sent apologies to a hundred and twelve meetings. Beautifully. His chair was kept. It always had been. It was a stool at the empty table in the middle of the room, in the light, where nobody broods, and somebody had dusted it, and it did not look as if it had been easy.

In the year 2000 Corvin Ash took the fifth pin out and read down to the bottom, and sat down at the nearest table, which was the one in the middle. Crake rang the little bell, and the four corners and the fire came into the middle of the room, which none of them had stood in for years, and held a meeting, quorum two, and reviewed his points. A vow, paid for, two crowns to a fortune-teller: binding, two points. A scar: no. Standing in the rain: he had sat in one corner of the <a href="The_Slain_Wyvern">Slain Wyvern</a> for eleven years. In ONE corner? Out of turn? There was not a rota. Nobody had ever sat in one corner for eleven years. Mr Rook was asked whether there was a category, and said there was now.

He did the sum twice, in the margin, and ruled a new line in red: C. ASH, thirty-nine, one corner, eleven years. Second, behind the scar. Rook moved himself down to fifth, by his own request, and was visibly happier there. Veyle offered Corvin the dark corner, Monday being only four days off, and Crake said NOBODY out of turn, however rainy; besides, his chair was kept. Mrs Garrow brought him a pie, and he stayed for it.

Mr Ash attends now. Not every week. He sends apologies when he does not. They find it very reassuring.

<div class="marginalia">The newsletter is free. I have issue one hundred and thirteen, which has a stop press: PRESENT: C. ASH. A NEW CATEGORY (ONE CORNER, ELEVEN YEARS). ONE STOOL DUSTED. Issue one hundred and twenty, which I had this winter, reports that Mr Blackwood made his first dramatic exit, the door having been planed at last, and that it was a cupboard, minus two, and that he took it extremely well, plus one, a new category.</div>
