<p class="lede"><b>Nix's Emporium</b> is the general shop on the square at <a href="Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>. Everything in it is previously owned, and the shopkeeper at the counter does not ask by whom; that is the arrangement. In the back room, for eleven years before the Quest, <a href="../People/Nix">Nix</a> did an inventory of things that were not on the inventory. The sign is a painted sack, painted a little open so that you cannot see what is in it, which is, the Forty-Eighth suspected on his first visit, the whole business model.</p>

## The windows

In one window: a stuffed owl, a lute with no strings, a single boot, and a card that says EVERYTHING MUST GO. Somebody has added, in a small neat hand: (BACK). The card is not Nix's. The boot is.

In the other: WE BUY ANYTHING. Under it, smaller: WE ASK NOTHING. Under that, smaller still, in a different pen: PLEASE ASK SOMETHING. - YOUR NEIGHBOURS.

## The shop

The stock changes, and I can only describe what was on the shelves when I called.

* **Loaves**, each labelled with where it was ACQUIRED. Most say A BAKERY. One says THE BAKERY, and has been corrected to A BAKERY.
* **Three potions.** A purple one, labelled NOT POISON, with small print: THIS LABEL IS PROVIDED FOR REASSURANCE ONLY. A blue one, labelled MANA, because blue is always mana, and everybody knows that, and nobody knows how; small print, OR IT IS BLUE WATER. ONE OF THOSE. A green one, labelled ANTIDOTE; small print, TO WHAT IS NOT STATED. WE ARE NOT IN A POSITION TO SAY.
* **Apples**, ONE PER CUSTOMER; under that, in a different hand, PER VISIT; under that, PER HAND. Somebody has been arguing with the card.
* **Two basins.** One so that customers can wash their hands in case they have touched anything they are not going to buy, and one for customers who have touched something they are not going to buy and feel bad about it.
* **Crates.** One stencilled PROPERTY OF THE ROYAL MAIL, painted over PROPERTY OF THE EMPORIUM, painted over again, and now just PROPERTY, which, it has been pointed out to me, is honest. You can't say it isn't honest. Another with holes in the lid, labelled LIVE: houseplants, which are, technically, alive.
* **Odds and ends**: one glove, a teapot lid, a key to something, and a jar marked MISC (DO NOT ASK).
* **A shelf of returns**, each tagged RETURNED TO OWNER, and one, very small, RETURNED TO OWNER, WHO WAS VERY UNDERSTANDING ABOUT IT.

## The ledger

The ledger lives in the back room. For eleven years every entry in it read ACQUIRED, and none of them said from where, and one entry in a different hand read PLEASE STOP WRITING ACQUIRED. It was ruled with a RETURNED column, very neatly, down eleven years of pages, and there was nothing in it.

There is now. In the year 2000 Nix took back to their owners the eleven oldest things in the book, the ones on its first page, which had never been sold: a pie dish, a kitchen chair, a keep net, a gate key, a tide table, a pair of spectacles, some stamps, a pair of good boots, a snap tin, a small oil can and a cradle. None of the owners knew the things had gone, and every one of them was pleased to see them back. Two of them tried to pay. The last page of the first volume is ruled off with a double line, the way it is done, and blotted, and I am told that nobody had ever seen that book finished before, Nix included.

The first line of the book had no provenance at all. It has one now, in Nix's hand: one coat, COLLECTED, on behalf of the party of the second part. How it came to be written is her business, and is told, so far as she has let me tell it, under <a href="../People/Nix">her article</a>.

The second volume began on the page after the coat. It is filling up.

<div class="marginalia">I asked the shopkeeper at the counter where the stuffed owl had come from. He said that everything there was previously owned, and that by whom, they don't ask. I asked whether anybody had ever asked. He said that one person had, once, and pointed at the card in the other window, and said that she was a neighbour, and that she had been very understanding about it.</div>
