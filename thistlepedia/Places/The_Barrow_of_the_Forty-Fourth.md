<p class="lede">The <b>Barrow of the Forty-Fourth</b> is a long grass mound with a stone mouth in its side, in the eastern hills above the north road, where Thistlewick buried <a href="../People/Ambrose_Fitch">Ambrose Fitch</a>, Chosen One number forty-four, at some expense, within a fortnight of his death, after ignoring him for forty years. He did not ask for a mound. He asked for a bench. There is one on it now.</p>

## The sign

Outside the stone mouth there is a sign:

<div class="stamp">THE BARROW OF THE FORTY-FOURTH. A Scheduled Monument of the district. Entry restricted to registered adventurers. By order: the Adventurers' Guild (Provisional)</div>

Underneath, in chalk, in a much older hand: THERE IS A CURSE ON IT AS WELL.

The stone is not locked. There is simply a sign, and you are simply not registered, and the Forty-Eighth found, to his considerable annoyance, that this was enough. He went back to Nether Sopping and registered, with a character reference, a hat and the mat, and came back and showed the sign his Guild Card. The sign did not react. He went in anyway, which was the whole of what the card was for.

## The dedication

Cut into the stone of the porch, deeply, and recently, and at some expense:

<div class="stamp">AMBROSE FITCH. CHOSEN ONE #44. THISTLEWICK REMEMBERS.</div>

Underneath, small, in chalk, in a woman's hand: *thistlewick did not write once.*

I have not asked whose hand it is. I have a good idea, and I have left it there, which is where she left it.

## Inside

The Barrow has a west chamber and an east, each with grave goods laid out on a cloth, which nobody had touched because nobody had been in: the things a hero is supposed to be buried with. In a stone alcove off the passage there is a dish, and a collar, and a name: BUDGE. Something is still using the alcove. On a mossy pillar near it, low down at about the height of a big dog's shoulder, the moss has been worn smooth by something leaning there for a very long time, every day, very contentedly.

In the burial chamber, in the year 2000, a man was standing in the middle of the floor, in armour, with a sword, in the exact centre of a circle somebody drew for him.

"The dead hero in the mound fights the living one. It's what's done. It's on the sign outside. It's in the chalk. It's in the shape of the room. I didn't ask for any of it. I asked for a bench."

They did what was done. It took four minutes. Then he sat down on the floor of his own burial chamber, which was not something the shape of the room allowed for, and said that he had been waiting forty years to have that fight. "That's the whole trouble with the lot of us. It's what's done, so we do it." He gave them the sword and the ring, given rather than taken, which he was extremely particular about. Then he told them what he wanted.

"Top of the mound. Facing the sea. You can see the north road from up there. People walk that road tired and there's nowhere for them to sit down for four miles. Forty years of standing in a circle and that's what I want. A bench. Anyone's bench. Not mine. Anyone's."

## The bench

Wick & Barrow of Nether Sopping had had a bench in eleven pieces leaning against the wall for some years, under a card: BENCH. 400. NOBODY HAS EVER BOUGHT THIS. The Forty-Eighth bought it, and carried it out to the mound in eleven pieces under his arm, and put it together on the top. The diagram was wrong. He worked it out. It took an hour and a half and one piece was left over, which is traditional.

There is a bench on the top of the Barrow of the Forty-Fourth now, facing the sea. Anybody may sit on it. That was the point; he was very clear that that was the point. From it you can see the sea one way, and the north road and the Tower the other.

The burial chamber is empty. The circle on the floor has gone unswept for the first time in forty years, and there is nobody standing in it. If you want him, he is on the top of the mound, on a bench, facing the sea.

When I went out to see it, the grass in front of the bench was flat, and there was a cup on the end of it. The cup was not mine.

## Ysolde

Somebody had to tell Ysolde Marrow, who kept the Forty-Fourth alive for nine years, on and off, and who has sat at the table by the bar in the Slain Wyvern ever since with a kit she does not need open in front of her out of habit. The Forty-Eighth told her. Nobody in the room looked over. Everybody in the room was listening.

"Forty years I've had that argument with a village I've never been to. They wanted a hero in a mound. He wanted somewhere to put his legs up."

He had also asked the Forty-Eighth to tell her that he said she was right about the four hit points. I do not know what the argument was. I know that she was right.

## Near by

**The Middle Distance.** South-east of the mound there is a hill with grass, a view of the sea, and nothing else of any description whatever, and on it there is a bench. It is older than the one on the mound. It has no plaque. Nobody remembers who put it up. If you sit on it, nothing happens, and the sea does what the sea does. The two benches face the same water.

**<a href="../People/Edwin_Tarrant">The Hermit</a>.** Not far off, a hut, a rock, and a man looking at the middle distance with great authority, who has been up there nineteen years, and has wisdom for passing travellers: bread, candles, a wedge of the hard cheese, not the soft, nails, two inch, and something for a cough. It is a shopping list. Nobody had ever once come up and asked him if he needed anything. The Forty-Eighth went and got his shopping. He would like it known that he did not need the company. He needed the cheese. He is nearly out of candles again, but he shan't mention it.

<div class="marginalia">I asked at the Wyvern who put up the bench on the Middle Distance, which is older than anybody's memory, and has no plaque, and faces the sea. Nobody knew. Merrow Halgate said that it was probably somebody who had walked that road tired, and had nowhere to sit, and had done something about it, and had not wanted a plaque. Then he said that it was a pity Ambrose had never been told about it, because it is not far. Then he said, after a while, that perhaps Ambrose had known, and wanted one nearer the road.</div>
