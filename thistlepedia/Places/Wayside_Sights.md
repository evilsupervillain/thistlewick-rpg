# Wayside Sights

<p class="hatnote">This article collects the places by the road that are not towns, have no inn, and are worth stopping for anyway. For the larger places, see the <a href="../Places/">Places</a> index.</p>

<p class="lede">The world has a great many things beside its roads, most of which are a field. These are the ones that are not, in no particular order, because the Forty-Eighth found them in no particular order, and so will you.</p>

## The Sky Anvil

In the snow beside a road in the far north there is a little stone shrine with a round grey stone set in the top of it like an egg in an egg cup. The pilgrims call it the Sky Anvil, and built the shrine round it in 1750, and come up every summer to look at it. It came down out of the sky in 1749, into the top field, on a Tuesday, and **Elias Meech**'s great-great-grandfather set it up for a gatepost, because it was there. It was the best gatepost in the county, and outlasted eleven gates. Mr Meech did not mind the pilgrims. They kept the frost off.

In the year 2000 <a href="../People/Elias_Meech">Mr Meech</a> was between gates, the last having gone at Michaelmas. He had never thrown a gate out; all eleven were in the hedge-bottom along the top field, where each was put when it went, rusting quietly in order. Hob Grumnir put his hand flat on the stone, the way you would put it on a horse, and said that it was star-iron, come down out of the sky. Mr Meech said, on a Tuesday. Hob had the eleven gates out one after another, and sorted them on the snow into what would do and what would not, and worked the afternoon through, cold, and made a gate out of eleven gates, true on its hangings, and fitted the latch with a spring he had been carrying in his pocket since a churchyard in Thistlewick. Mr Meech hung it on the shrine himself, and said that that was a very good gate, and told Hob to take the stone, and to mind his back. It was very heavy. Hob did not mention this.

The shrine has a gate on it now and nothing in the top. The pilgrims come up and open it and shut it all afternoon. Nobody had ever given them anything to open before. What Hob made of the star-iron is in his article, and is one sword, and he has not said who it is for.

## The Lay-By

At the end of a lay-by on the west road, on the grass, there is an igloo. It is not melting. There are voices inside it, disagreeing politely. It is the annual general meeting of the association of hedge mages, which is not its name, because it has not got a name; that is item one, and has been for nine years, now eleven. The igloo is neutral ground. None of them would host, so **Mrs Quickset** made it the first winter, and it is nobody's house, so nobody has to do the biscuits. It has not melted in all that time, and it is the one thing the association has ever agreed on. **<a href="../People/Dunnock">Dunnock</a>** says that they did not agree on it; she just did it.

**Miss Privet** takes the minutes, and a member of the public who looks in will be asked to propose a name, strictly for the minutes, and the name will be carried forward under nine years of other things carried forward. Dunnock is self-taught, from a book with four pages missing, which he worked out for himself, and did better, as it turned out; he is better than the whole Collegium at exactly four things. Somebody inside says *Collegium* in the voice people use for weather.

In the year 2000 Zephyrine Vance was admitted, over one objection, which was withdrawn after Dunnock showed her page twenty-three, *Kindle*, which you do not aim, but sort of mean, at everything, and it sorts itself out. She said that was not how fire works. A gorse bush, a milestone and the lay-by's litter bin caught fire all at once, neatly, and went out, with nobody anywhere near them. She said that it should not have worked. Dunnock said the page was missing. There is a chair at the folding table now with nobody's coat on it. She proposed a name. It was a good name. It was carried forward.

## The Cinder Fen

In the far south a path goes out into the Cinder Fen and gives up. Under the cracked mud on top, nine hundred acres of water were drowning a road a foot at a time, and the <a href="../People/The_Drainage_Commissioner">Drainage Commissioner</a> stood where the path gave up, in her wellingtons, with a clipboard, waiting for a fire person. The Fen wanted burning; it was the traditional remedy, and nobody in the parish had been able to do it since Mr Tinder retired, thirty years before. The form was in triplicate, one for the parish, one for the county, and one to come back to the Commissioner signed by the fire person and countersigned by the Fen. There was a box for it. She had never had one back.

Zephyrine Vance said that she was a fire person, and had been expelled for it, for an unsanctioned quantity of fire, which was in her file. The Commissioner said that unsanctioned was only the form; they could sanction anything; that is what it is FOR. Under QUANTITY OF FIRE she wrote *nine hundred acres*, pressing hard enough for all three copies, in the hand of a person who had been told for eleven years that hers was the wrong amount. It was stamped SANCTIONED, three times. She asked the Commissioner to say that again.

It burned all afternoon and all night, and nobody in the parish went to bed, and somebody brought out chairs. In the morning the Fen was black and dry and smoking gently, and out of the mud, running north, came a stone causeway older than anybody, with a milestone on it that says GT DUNNAGE, a foot under since Mr Tinder retired. The third copy, held out over the fire at arm's length all night, came back with a scorch in one corner, exactly in the box. Countersigned by the Fen. The Certificate of Burning that had been waiting thirty years for a name has hers on it, and she says it is the first piece of paper about her and fire that she has liked. The Commissioner asked whether she would do it again in thirty years. She said to write to her, in triplicate.

## The Wrong Dragon

In the far south-east, in a cave in the rocks above the water, something very large breathes slowly, the way people breathe when they are reading. The map says it is a dragon. It is a dragon, it will tell you, in the sense that the map says so, and not in the sense anybody means. Its cave is dark and warm and full of books, in the order it read them, which is nobody else's, and it sits in an armchair at the back with a blanket over what may be its knees. People come about the dragon, and are relieved, and then a little disappointed, and then they stay for tea.

Eight years before the Quest a young knight came, very correct, with a commission with *dragon* on it and a great deal of armour, and they talked about Horace until it got dark. It hoped he had found his dragon. He had not; his dragon was done in by somebody else, a year or two later, who drinks in the Slain Wyvern and is, I can confirm, insufferable about it. In the year 2000 Sir Aldric came back, and it emerged that Horace is a poet, Roman, the Odes, and that Sir Aldric had believed for four hours that they were talking about his horse, and that it had wondered why the Odes had so much in them about oats. It asked him to read to it. Its eyes are not what they were; that is why it talks so much. He lit a lamp, and read the Odes until dark, and they are not about oats.

On the back of his commission, before he left, he wrote his first report in eight years: DRAGON: FOUND. NOT SLAIN. READ TO. P-G. He goes back. He usually cannot find the way, and it keeps the lamp lit, and when he can, he takes the book down off the shelf. Nobody describes the rest of the cave, which you see by lamplight, and I am not going to start.

## The Fair

In the sand of the far south, a long way from anywhere, the way fairs always are, there is a big tent with a fair in it: swingboats, a coconut shy with no coconuts, and at the back a little striped booth, <a href="../People/Mother_Selvidge">MOTHER SELVIDGE</a>. NEVER WRONG. TWO CROWNS. She will tell you that you are going to go a long way and meet a great many people, and that at the end of it somebody is going to be very surprised to see you, and that you will eat a turnip, and that one is free. She has never once been wrong. Ask anybody she has read.

She told Corvin Ash, at nineteen, that he was the rival of a great hero. In the year 2000, when he came back and asked her again, she told him: a long life, a useful trade, something with his hands, no destiny whatsoever. And then, when he asked again, she put the cards down and told him that she says the rival of a great hero to about four young men a year, lads at nineteen, sat in a heap, and that it is the reading that makes them stand up straight, and it has never once been wrong. They all went and did something. He said that he had sat in a corner for eleven years. She said, and then you got up.

## The Duck's Pond

Four miles from the Collegium Arcanum there is a small pond with a duck on it, wearing round its neck, on a ribbon, the little silver medal the Collegium gives its Fellows. It is an Honorary Fellow. It sets off across the pond to get a better look at visitors, and walks into the bank, and then, with great dignity, into the bank again. If you bow, it inclines its head, as one Fellow to a member of the public.

It was at the enquiry, in 1989, with Zephyrine Vance and the Dean and an unsanctioned quantity of fire, and in the year 2000 it was walked four miles to Congregation, because it was the quorum, and gave its proxy. It does not intend to be walked again. It is also one of the three reviewers of Miss Halloway's monograph, and read page four hundred, apparently, and sat down on it, which is acceptance, and it is the warmest review she has ever had. When it is away there is a stick at the edge of the pond with GONE TO CONGREGATION on it in pencil.

## Smaller sights

**The Standing Stones of Uncertain Purpose**, beside the west road. Six theories are current: a calendar; a temple; a boundary; a tomb; an early form of the Prophecy; and that they were put up by Wat Pardle of that parish to mark where his gate was, and that Wat Pardle is standing over there and will say so. Theory six is footnoted, *Not favoured. The other five are more interesting and Mr Pardle has been asked to stop coming to the site.* Beside the plaque is a second board, newer, smaller and screwed down much harder: THEORY SEVEN HAS BEEN REMOVED AT THE REQUEST OF THE PARISH AND THE FAMILY. I have nothing to add to the board.

**Ashcombe Lake**, where on a still day you can hear the old church bell ringing under the water. You cannot. See <a href="Ashcombe">Ashcombe</a>.

**The Middle Distance**, a hill with a bench on it, and **the Hermit**, who needed cheese. See <a href="The_Barrow_of_the_Forty-Fourth#near-by">the Barrow of the Forty-Fourth</a>.

**Miss Halloway's type specimens**, eleven of them, each where she found it, from the Common Hedge Egg near Thistlewick to the Pond Kappa by Ashcombe Lake. They are hers, and are in her <a href="../Bestiary/">Bestiary</a>, and she would rather you did not touch them.
