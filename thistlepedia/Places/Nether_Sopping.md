<p class="lede"><b>Nether Sopping</b> is a fishing town on the south coast: roofs, rigging, and a great deal of weather arriving off the sea. It is where Thistlewick's heroes went afterwards. For most of a century, clause nineteen of the Prophecy sent every Chosen One who came home, and a good many of the people who went with him, to a cottage "at such distance from the village as is mutually agreeable", and the distance was always here. Nobody in Thistlewick mentions that it exists. It is my home, and I have been mentioning it for forty years, to anybody who would sit still.</p>

The town sign says NETHER SOPPING, THE SEA IS RIGHT THERE, and under it, in a different hand, IT IS. WE CHECKED. I have checked as well. It goes out, and then it comes back.

## The memorial

On the harbour front there is a standing stone with names cut into it. Not forty-seven names. A hundred and eighty-one.

The Chosen Ones are on it, but so is everybody who went with them: healers, hired swords, cousins, a cook, somebody listed only as *the lad who carried the rope*. Thistlewick's Wall of the Forty-Seven has forty-seven portraits on it. Nobody in Thistlewick has ever counted the difference. There is room underneath for more. There is always room underneath for more.

<div class="marginalia">I am asked, by visitors, whether the stone is a memorial to the dead. It is not. Most of the hundred and eighty-one were alive when their names were cut, and a good many of them drink in the Wyvern, and some of them will show you where they are on it. It is a memorial to having gone.</div>

## The town

**The harbour.** A pier, two bollards worn smooth by a hundred years of rope and a hundred years of people sitting on them to look at the sea (one has a little brass plate: THIS BOLLARD IS FOR BOATS, and under that, worn almost away, AND FOR GRANDAD), and the Harbour Rules:

> 1. No adventuring on the pier.
> 2. The crab on the west beach is nobody's fault and is not to be provoked.
> 3. Rule 2 is not a challenge.

There is a crab in the well as well. It looks up. Rule two applies.

**The Ferryman** will take you anywhere at all: islands, the far shore, that bit on the map with the writing you can't read. The tide was wrong. It had been wrong since he bought the boat. Nix returned to him, in the year 2000, a printed tide table with his boat drawn in the margin, which he had owned for one week and she had owned since, and he read it, and looked at the water, and read it again, and said that the tide was right today. He has it open on his knee, and is giving it a few more days.

**The market.** SEA POTATOES, which are potatoes; SEA CARROTS, which are, as far as anybody can tell, carrots. In Nether Sopping everything is sea something. We have a sea cat. The fish stall sells fish under a card that says FISH, which is the only thing on the market that is exactly what it says it is, and the stallholder is very proud of it; and Sopping biscuit, which is older than the fish and will outlive the both of us. The stall's stamped scales go up to twenty stone and have never once lied, and were lent, in the year 2000, for a medical examination, of which more in <a href="../People/Merribell">Sister Merribell's article</a>. There is a tub of eels sold by the yard, and a tub with an octopus in it, which is very relaxed, and has arranged itself so as to be holding the fishmonger's pencil. A big crate on the market is stencilled RETURN TO SENDER, and under that, SENDER UNKNOWN, and under that, RETURN. It has been waiting to be returned for some years.

**The boards.** NETHER SOPPING IN BLOOM - HIGHLY COMMENDED, DAMP CATEGORY. SPEED LIMIT - A BRISK WALK; ADVENTURERS ARE NOT TO RUN THROUGH THE TOWN, HOWEVER URGENT THE PROPHECY. YES, YOU. WE CAN SEE YOU. TODAY'S WEATHER - RAIN, which is not chalked, but painted on.

**The beach.** Driftwood bleached silver, with K + M, WED 1972, and a small heart carved into it, and under it, in newer, deeper letters, STILL. A gull on a log that has opinions about you. The net loft, with a card on the door: IF YOU ARE HERE ABOUT THE ROPE, THE ROPE IS SPOKEN FOR. It is spoken for by whoever asks properly. Nobody ever had; they read the card and went off. Nix asked properly, and the rope came out of the window, and the voice said, "Properly."

**The boat shed.** Locked. Through the window, eleven boats, all upside down, all belonging to somebody who went north. Nobody has rowed them since. One of them gave an oar, in the year 2000, which was asked for and offered, and is a sword now; "she'd like being a sword". The receipt is in the Ferryman's hat.

**The Chandler's** keeps the hours on the card in its window, which are *when the light is on at the point*. For one spring and the summer after, the light at the point was out, and the Chandler's was shut. The card is turned round now, OPEN, because the light is lit; see <a href="The_Lighthouse_of_Saint_Bother">The Lighthouse of Saint Bother</a>. Inside, everything was dusted, twice.

## The people

* **Sops**, the potboy at the Wyvern, and his friend **Tibb**, who play Chosen One on the front. The rules are that one of them walks north and the other has to be the Dark Lord and lose, and neither of them wanted to be the Dark Lord, and they settled it by both being the Chosen One and agreeing that the Dark Lord had stayed at home today. Which, as solutions go, was well ahead of anything the Prophecy Committee had managed. In the afternoons now the Dark Lord comes down the street, in a teal hood, under protest, and loses.
* **Cobb Lorrimer**, a thief, registered with the Guild under T, who challenged Nix to a contest of fetching and lost it on paperwork, and signed for the post of Dark Lord (afternoons), and read what he had signed a moment too late. He has come back every day since, and is getting very good at losing. He would like a receipt for his tools.
* **A man having a day off**, asleep against a stack of crates with a very good sword across his knees. He has done this for thirty-one years, and today, every day, he is having a day off. There is a lad in the Wyvern who will go with anyone. Ask him. Let him sleep. The sword is one of Hob Grumnir's. Neither of them is working today.
* **The Courier**, a man in a red coat and a flat cap, with a satchel on a strap, who is always already here when you arrive, and whose boots are dry. Nobody asks him how any more. He carried Spare Cotterill's letters home from eight towns, this one among them. He is on his rounds. He does not say whose.

## Wick & Barrow, Outfitters

Across from the Guild: EVERYTHING FOR THE ROAD NORTH, and chalked under it, ALSO THE ROAD BACK (ASK). A painted breastplate, ARMOUR REPAIRED, DRAGONS QUOTED FOR. A painted teapot: A HERO RUNS ON TEA AND BOOTS. MOSTLY BOOTS. In the window, boots WALKED NORTH AND BACK, ONE CAREFUL OWNER, which look as though they would rather not talk about it, and a display of rucksack, rope, lantern and a tin of biscuits with a card, YOU WILL NEED A BIGGER RUCKSACK.

**Mrs Barrow** has been in the trade thirty-nine years. "Everything an adventurer needs, and four things nobody does." She sells the hat that the Guild's Form A-1 requires, for thirty, and is not related to the Registrar. She sells lamp oil, and a bottle of DRAGON REPELLENT, whose small print says IT IS LAMP OIL. THROW IT HARD. She sells chalk for marking the walls of dungeons, one crown, FINDING YOUR WAY OUT - NOT INCLUDED. She sells ten-foot poles, which every adventurer needs, and nobody has ever said what for, and everybody buys one. A crate of returned compasses all point at the Gilded Turnip in Thistlewick; she says it is a known fault. In the year 2000 she supplied forty bolts of oilskin to the Hoyle Works at Upper Clanging, and wrote *on account* for the first time in thirty-nine years, three times, and propped the card against the till where it could be seen from the door. "Well. Something's going the right way up that road for once."

**Wick's peg** is at the end of the row of pegs on the wall, with nothing on it, dusted. Wick went north, the same as they all do, forty-one years ago this spring, by her count; forty-three, now, by mine. "The sign says Wick and Barrow. That's two names, and one of them's still walking about, and the other one went north, and both belong on a sign."

**The bench.** For many years Wick & Barrow had a bench in eleven pieces leaning against the wall under a card: BENCH. 400. NOBODY HAS EVER BOUGHT THIS. Somebody bought it. It is on the top of a mound in the eastern hills now, facing the sea. See <a href="The_Barrow_of_the_Forty-Fourth">The Barrow of the Forty-Fourth</a>.

## The Guild

The <a class="new" href="../Institutions/The_Adventurers_Guild">Adventurers' Guild (Provisional)</a> has its hall on the front, under a sign held on with six bolts (the last sign had one bolt, and was taken by somebody who needed a sword more than the Guild did), and a crest of a sword, a scroll and a small question mark. The question mark is the provisional part. It was founded in 1960, provisionally, pending a review, and the review has been scheduled ever since. Registrar Pell keeps it. It has its own article.

## Number Forty-Five's, and the cottage next door

At the north end of the town are two cottages side by side, which clause nineteen gave to two Chosen Ones who came home.

**Number Forty-Five's** is <a class="new" href="../People/Halbert_Quy">Halbert Quy</a>'s, the Forty-Fifth, who went north in 1970 and was offered a cup of tea at the top and said no, and has thought about it ever since. He grows roses. There is not one turnip in the house, and it is a policy. The Committee's framed commendation is face down on the shelf, dusted round rather than moved. The sword over the fire is exactly like the one over every Chosen One's fire, and has dust on the upper edge, which takes a long time and a great deal of not looking.

**The cottage next door** was Ambrose Fitch's, the Forty-Fourth, who lived in it for forty years, from 1960, and watered a pot with a dead thing in it every morning, and nothing grew. He is dead; see <a class="new" href="../People/Ambrose_Fitch">his article</a>, and the Barrow. Mrs Pimm, who has the other key, airs it on Tuesdays, and does the windows inside and out, and has left a note on the table: AIRED TUESDAYS. KETTLE IN THE CUPBOARD. PLEASE DON'T MOVE HIS CHAIR. In the cupboard there is a kettle, a caddy of tea and one cup. From the window you can see the sea, and the end of the north road where it comes over the hill.

The Committee sent the key to that cottage to the Forty-Eighth after the Quest, with his pension and its congratulations, and trusted Friday would suit. Clause nineteen was struck six months later, and he has never lived in it. He uses it when he comes south, which is about once a month, to correct me. He waters the pot. Nothing has grown. I have checked. He has never moved the chair.

## The coast road

The road from Thistlewick's turning runs east along the coast. At the turning is a signpost, EAST, Nether Sopping, WEST, Thistlewick, on which somebody from one end of the road has scratched out the other end's name, and somebody from the other end has scratched out theirs. Both are still legible. It has been like this for a hundred years.

The signpost gives Nether Sopping as four leagues. Mr Quy, who was sent here by the Committee and has had thirty years to think about it, makes it forty miles. I have walked it, once, to see Thistlewick for this volume, and I side with Mr Quy, and I think the signpost was put up by somebody from the Thistlewick end.

Off the west beach, from the road, you can see the **Isle of Uncertain Ownership**, a rock with a plaque bolted to it, THIS ISLAND IS THE PROPERTY OF, and then a very great deal of scratching out, and a flagpole with two flags on it, one above the other. The order has been reversed nine times, by parties who could not get ashore and were shouting from boats. There is a sheep on it. It has never been sheared, because shearing it would be an act of ownership, and it is, by now, very nearly a sphere. It looks at you with the calm of an animal that has never once been asked to do anything.

The west beach itself was held, for longer than the town, by the **Crab of Unusual Size**, the size of a garden shed, which was on the Guild's board, and which everybody in Nether Sopping was very careful to call *unusual*. It does not hold the beach any more. Four smaller crabs hold it between them, badly. Underneath it, in the shingle, were eleven years of other people's bad afternoons. Miss Halloway has a note on the crab in the Bestiary.
