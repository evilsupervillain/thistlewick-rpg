<p class="lede"><b>Carn Drey</b> is a hill east of <a href="Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a> with a field on top and a cave in its side, and under it, at the foot of a long stair, a stone with a sword in it, and at the bottom, coiled three times round the room, the Worm. It was <a href="../People/Roland_Fairweather">Roland Fairweather</a>'s prophecy, and had been since he was twelve.</p>

## The gate

Across the mouth of the cave is an iron gate with a plate: THE WORM. NOT READY. (FELLOW BLUNT, QUARTERLY.) The prophecy's keeper, <a href="../People/Fellow_Blunt">Fellow Blunt</a>, came out quarterly for twenty-two years and checked the stone from the gate, and never went down. The gate is not locked. Nobody ever tried it.

Under NOT READY, in pencil, somebody has written READY. DONE. (R.F.)

## The stone

At the foot of the stairs is a stone with a sword in it to the hilt, and twenty-two years of dust on the hilt, and none at all on the stone. Roland stood in front of it for a long time, in the year 2000, and asked whether it was ready. He was told that it looked ready. Nobody had ever said that to him. He put his hand on the hilt the way you might put a hand on a horse you were not sure of, and the sword came out of the stone as if it had only been waiting to be asked. He said, *Oh. It's ready.* Then he went on down, with it. The stone has a slot in it now, the shape of a sword.

On the way down, a wall is cracked across, and at about shoulder height there is a smooth place where somebody once leaned to get their breath before going on. A tall rock further down has been passed so often by something very long, going down and coming back, that one side of it is polished like a banister.

## The Worm

The Worm had been waiting since before Roland was born for a hero of nineteen. Roland was thirty-four, and said that he was sorry, and that he did mean to come sooner. The Worm, which is old and very large and had been looking forward to it, uncoiled. It was over in about the time it takes to say so.

The Worm of Carn Drey was ended, as the prophecy said, and the land, which is a field with sheep in it, is presumably glad. Then Roland asked whether everybody would mind being surprised, only a little, and everybody was, not at the Worm but at Roland Fairweather, who had finished something, standing in the dark at the bottom of a hill with his mouth open. He laughed until he had to sit down on the floor and wipe his eyes, and then he thanked each of the company by name, which took a while, because he meant it.

The Worm, which had waited twenty-two years, is in no hurry, and went back to sleep. Neither is Roland. He has been down again since, after tea. I am told that it is very good about it.

<div class="marginalia">I asked Roland what had become of the sword out of the stone. He said that he wears it. He kept it as far as the tower door, the first time, and at the door it was handed back, and after the ending he went up again and stood at the foot of the tower, out of habit, and waited to be called away, and nothing happened, and went on not happening. He says that the bit after is very quiet.</div>
