<p class="lede"><b>Elder Lirien</b> is one of the Elders of <a href="../Places/Olde_Evermore">Olde Evermore</a>, the wife of <a href="Elder_Faelan">Elder Faelan</a>, the daughter of Elowen, and the translator to <a href="Mirabel">Mirabel</a>, the Last Oracle, whom everybody calls Grandmother. Nearly everything the Last Oracle has said in this encyclopedia, she said in the Old Tongue, and it reached me in Elder Lirien's words. Very little of what follows is in Elder Lirien's own, because she does not use them much, and I have tried to put down every one.</p>

## The translator

She introduces herself by her work, and is quite exact about the scope of it:

> "Elder Lirien. I translate for Grandmother, when she prophesies, and when she asks for pudding."

Asked how you tell the difference, she says that the pudding is more urgent. I do not think that is a joke. I think it is a professional observation, from somebody who has translated both for a very long time.

When Grandmother prophesies, every candle in the Archive leans towards her, and she speaks in the Old Tongue, and it is flawless, and it is terrible, and Elder Lirien puts it into ours a line behind her, steadily, the way you would read a notice aloud: *When the moon devours the sun, and the last bell falls silent, the one who was not chosen shall-* and then Grandmother asks whether that is a biscuit, and Elder Lirien stops, because there is nothing left to translate. "It never does," she said to the Forty-Eighth. "She's been doing that one since the Second Age. It's always a biscuit."

She also translates the small print. When the Forty-Eighth was told that Grandmother was four thousand one hundred and twelve, it was Elder Lirien who added, "And a quarter. She likes the quarter mentioned," and it was Grandmother who then said "Eight and a QUARTER," without any help at all. And on Grandmother's pillow, in the corner of the Archive, there is a note in the Old Tongue, flawless and terrible, which Elder Lirien translates without looking up: *Not tired.*

## Half past seven

The bedtime is hers. "She has to be in bed by half past seven. We tried eight, once." Grandmother foresaw the fall of an empire in the middle of supper, out of sheer tiredness. "It was only a small empire. Still." She says it the way any parent says it about any child who was allowed up late once and is not going to be again, and it is perhaps the only time I have heard the fall of an empire described as a reason for an early night.

The Archive clock says a quarter past seven, and has for some time, because its pendulum is tied to the side of the case with a hair ribbon, in a very neat bow. I asked Elder Lirien whether she knew. She said: "Half past seven. Not a minute after." I took that as yes.

## The scroll

In the year 2000 Grandmother told the Forty-Eighth a secret, a real one and not a prophecy: that Elder Lirien was going to have a baby. Grandmother had foreseen it, and told them at breakfast, before they knew, and had known since the ninth of March.

Across the Archive, Elder Lirien dropped a scroll. It is, as far as anybody in Olde Evermore can remember, the only thing she has ever dropped. She picked it up again, and it took her a long time, and Elder Faelan at the foot of the ladder went the colour of the carpet, and neither of them could stop smiling, which was making the colour worse. Grandmother said it was a girl, and that she was going to be called Biscuit. Elder Faelan said that she was not going to be called Biscuit. Elder Lirien, I notice, did not translate for either of them, and did not need to.

Her mother, Elowen, had stopped knitting, and started again that morning, at her front door, very small and very fast. It is a tea cosy with two little sleeves and a large B on the front, for Bryony, or Bronwen. It is not for anything else.

<div class="marginalia">I wrote to Elder Lirien to ask whether she would let me print an article about her, rather than about the things she has translated. Her reply came in the Old Tongue, which I understand she had never before had occasion to write for herself, and she had translated it underneath, and the translation was shorter. It said: <i>Yes. Spell Bryony with a y.</i> Under that, in wax crayon, in a large round hand, somebody had written something else in the Old Tongue, and that was not translated, and I have not asked. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

She is still in the Archive, translating. Grandmother still prophesies, and still asks for pudding, and the pudding is still more urgent; Grandmother is still in bed by half past seven, and says that she is not tired, and Elder Lirien still translates that without looking up.

On the slate in the Elders' kitchen, where the household writes down what is to be decided at breakfast, there are two names, BRYONY and BRONWEN, in Elder Lirien's hand, and under them a third, in wax crayon, which I have seen and will not print. Elder Faelan has drawn a line through it several times. It is still there. Asked when breakfast will decide, Elder Lirien said that elves are not in a hurry about anything, and that this was not a thing to be in a hurry about, and went back to translating. I have put nothing in this volume about the matter that she did not put in it herself.
