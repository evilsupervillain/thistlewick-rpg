<p class="lede"><b>Everard Pilbeam</b> was only passing. There was an eagle. That was in 1996. He rescued Princess Rosamund of Fairholme from it, and the council of Fairholme has not decided whether the eagle counts, and he cannot go until it does. He sits at the foot of the council table, and looks at the door the way a man looks at a door he has been looking at for six years.</p>

## Buttons

He was born in 1970 at Coddington, which is a long way from Fairholme in every direction, and became a traveller in buttons at twenty, for a firm of button-makers, with a case of samples on cards, horn and bone and brass and mother-of-pearl, which he carried from town to town on foot. He was, he says, a very good traveller in buttons: he could tell a draper's taste at a glance, and he never once oversold, and he was liked. He has the case still, under his chair at the council table. He has not opened it since 1996.

## The eagle

In the spring of 1996 he was walking through Fairholme on the road east, only passing, when an eagle came down out of the sky on the palace and went up again with the Princess in its talons, by the shoulders of her cloak, which was, he learned afterwards, the one she now keeps labelled EAGLE. It went up the hill, struggling, because she had the bag. He ran after it. He says he does not know why. He says he had never run after anything in his life except a coach.

The eagle came down on the ridge to rest, being out of breath, and he came up behind it and hit it with the case of samples, which is very heavy, and it let go and went off over the hill in a temper, and the Princess sat up, and thanked him, and asked him to carry the bag, and he walked her home.

> "I was only passing. There was an eagle. That was four years ago. They haven't decided if the eagle counts. I can't go till they decide if the eagle counts."

## The question

The Palace does not count the eagle. An eagle is not a kidnapper; it is weather, or an animal, or an act of God, the council's lawyers are divided. If it does not count, he is not a rescuer, and has no share, and may go. If it counts, he holds share twelve, a two-thousand-and-forty-eighth of the kingdom, and must stay to be minuted. Until the council decides, he is neither, and may not go, because a man who might be a shareholder cannot leave the kingdom without notice, under the articles, and he cannot give notice of a share he may not hold.

The minutes have deferred it eight times. Item five, at every meeting: *The eagle. Whether Mr Pilbeam, who rescued Her Highness from the eagle, therefore holds a share. Deferred. Mr Pilbeam asks whether he may go. Deferred.* The share is being held for him in the meantime by a potted plant by the wall, with a flag in it, SHARE TWELVE.

<div class="marginalia">I have had the minutes of every meeting since 2000 from Mr Pilbeam, who writes, in a very neat hand, and encloses a button with each letter, as a matter of habit, which he apologises for. I have nineteen buttons. They are very good buttons. The King has asked me to say that Mr Pilbeam is a great comfort to have about the place, and Princess Rosamund has asked me to say that the eagle counts, and that she has a cloak to prove it. Mr Pilbeam has asked me to say nothing either way, in case it prejudices item five. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

He is thirty-one. He still sits at the foot of the table. He has, since 2000, been made clerk to the council, unpaid, because he was there, and because he writes such a neat hand, and he takes the minutes now, and has had to minute his own deferral four times, and says that it does not get easier. On the last occasion he wrote *Mr Pilbeam asks whether he may go*, and then sat for a moment, and then, very small, in the margin, *He does not, on reflection, particularly mind.* The council has not noticed. I have.
