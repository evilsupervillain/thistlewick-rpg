<p class="lede"><b>Hester Ashbury</b> keeps the Eight Hours at <a href="../Places/Restwell">Restwell</a>, where a room is ten crowns, breakfast included, and breakfast is whenever you come down. She argued about that for a year. Now she just cooks. Every guest in her house sleeps the night through to the eight bars her husband plays them up with, and comes down better, and very late, and is given a chop, because in Restwell breakfast is generally after lunch.</p>

## The baker's daughter

She was born Hester Quine in 1949 at the baker's in Restwell, two doors down from the Eight Hours, and was up at four every morning of her girlhood to help with the bread, which she says gave her strong views about mornings that she has never been able to put into practice since. She married Walt Ashbury, the innkeeper's son, in 1973, a year before he sat down on the step, and took the house over with him when his father retired, in 1977.

## Breakfast

The Eight Hours served breakfast from seven until nine, when she came to it, because every inn does. Nobody came down for it. The guests, who had been played up the night before, slept until eleven, or one, or three, and came down better than they had been in years, and asked for breakfast, and were told that it was over.

She argued about it for a year: with Walt, with the guests, with the clock, and principally with herself, because she had been brought up at a baker's, and breakfast was at seven, and that was what breakfast was. In 1978, on a Tuesday, a guest came down at twenty past two and asked for breakfast, and she opened her mouth to tell him that it was over, and found that she was too tired of saying it to say it, and gave him a chop. She has given every guest since a chop.

> "Whenever you come down. I argued about that for a year. Now I just cook."

## Resurrection

> "Resurrection's extra. And I'll want the room back by noon."

That is what she says when guests ask her about the eight bars, and how they work, and whether it is true that a man came in with one foot in the grave and went out dancing. It is a joke, and it is meant as one, and she has never in her life charged anybody for anything but the room, and the room is ten crowns. I asked her whether anybody had ever come in very ill. She said that people came in all sorts of ways, and that they all went up the stairs the same way, and came down the same way, and that she did not ask, and that it was not a hospital, and that Dr Pike would tell me otherwise and that I was to take no notice.

<div class="marginalia">She wants the room back by noon. It is generally twenty past two when anybody comes down. She has never once, in twenty-five years, asked anybody to hurry. I asked her why she says noon at all. She said that you have to say something, and that noon was what her father-in-law said, and that it was a comfort to the guests to be told a time they could then miss. - H.B.</div>

## Walt

She has listened to the eight bars from the kitchen twelve hundred times a year for twenty-eight years, and has never once asked him to stop. "He is very good at it." I asked her whether she minded hearing the same eight bars every night. She said that she did not hear them any more, as such; that they were like the sea, if you lived by the sea. Then she said that she heard them every time, and that she always stopped what she was doing until they were over, and that it took eleven seconds, and that she had worked out once that it came to about sixty hours of her life spent standing still in the kitchen listening, and that it was the best sixty hours she had.

## Afterwards

She is fifty-three. The Eight Hours is busier than it has ever been, since 2000, with people going up to see Grimspite's chair, and she has taken on a girl from the baker's to help with the chops. The register at the foot of the stairs has a column for remarks, and the remarks have all said much the same thing for twenty-eight years; she has had a second column added, at the guests' request, headed HOW LONG, so that they can write down how long they slept, and nobody has ever written down a number. They write *eight hours*.
