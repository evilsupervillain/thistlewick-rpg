<p class="lede"><b>Dean Fenwold</b> of the <a href="../Institutions/The_Collegium_Arcanum">Collegium Arcanum</a> was in the room in 1989 when <a href="Zephyrine_Vance">Zephyrine Vance</a> produced what the enquiry called, twice, an unsanctioned quantity of fire. He is fine. He will tell you so before you have asked. He has made a second career of it.</p>

## The man who was fine

> "Ah! Visitors. Fenwold. Dean. I'm fine, thank you."

You had not asked. People do; they have been asking for eleven years. So he does a talk about it now, *The Man Who Was Fine*, after dinner, and by the year 2000 he had given it forty-one times, and they laughed in all the right places. Thursday week, in Hall, if you are about.

His account of the incident is a model of its kind: "A very gifted young woman, a very small room, and rather more fire than the room had been told about." The eyebrows grew back. Magnificently, he is told. He smooths one when he says so. Then he adds the thing that is not in the talk, or was not, until 2000: "She said the room was wrong, you know. At the time. I remember thinking she was very probably right."

There is a short pause after that. It has been in the talk forty-one times.

## The demonstration

In the year 2000 he asked Miss Vance to come and do it again, for the talk: small, controlled, in Hall, with buckets. She refused, and then agreed, on terms, and the terms were that a paper of Dr Hapgood's, *On the Kindling of Damp Materials*, should go out under Dr Hapgood's name, with a note, which the Dean would write. He said that he would write the note. He said, too, that he had thought she was very probably right, at the time. It is not much of an apology. It is the best he has, and he means every word of it, and he put it in the talk.

The demonstration went wrong in exactly the way it had gone wrong the first time, for exactly the same reason, which is that the room was wrong. He did not move. He had not moved in 1989 either. When the smoke cleared nothing was damaged, and the Dean said that the room was wrong, and that they had all heard her say so.

## Afterwards

The talk has never been better. It ends now with the room being wrong, and he gives it at a good many dinners a year, and he has told me that he is fine, which I had not asked. Dr Hapgood's paper has Dr Hapgood's name on it, with a note by the Dean.

He has also, I am told, had the Hall measured. He has not said what he means to do about it, and the Collegium has a committee for rooms, which will consider it, and he is fine with that.

His portrait hangs in the Hall, with the First Dean's, and the First Dean's has been hanging pending the Hanging Committee for five hundred years, and his has not been there quite as long. There is a duck four miles off with a Fellow's medal round its neck, which was at the enquiry as well. It is also fine. It does not give talks.
