<p class="lede"><b>Hector Kell</b> keeps the Safety Valve, the inn at <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>, named for the one part of a boiler whose entire job is to give up before anything else does. They are very proud of it. A plate under the sign reads IN THE EVENT OF PRESSURE, GO TO BED, and a plate on the foot of every bed reads IN CASE OF EMERGENCY, LIE DOWN. Beds are thirty crowns. You will be dry, and up here that is worth thirty.</p>

## The Valve

He was born over the bar in 1968, and took the house over from his mother, Mrs Ada Kell, in 1993, when she retired to the chair by the fire, which she gave up in 1997 to Winnie Marsden, after a discussion. The inn has been the Kells' since 1841, when his great-great-grandfather, a boilerman, was blown clean through the foundry roof by an engine without one, and landed in the street unhurt, and went straight into the business of putting people up for the night, on the grounds that he had had enough excitement.

> "The Safety Valve. Named for the one part of a boiler whose entire job is to give up before anything else does. We are very proud of it."

He is very proud of it. He says it in a voice of such quiet satisfaction that you come to see, by the second half, that it is a whole philosophy, and that in a town of engines the one thing nobody has ever taught a man is when to stop, and that the Kells have been teaching it for a hundred and sixty years, at thirty crowns a night.

## The list

On the wall is a framed list in a very good hand, *Things That Are Not To Happen Again*, numbered to sixty-three. It was begun by his great-great-grandfather and every Kell since has added to it. I give the items Mr Kell will discuss: 7. THE GEESE. 19. NOT LIKE THAT. 31. HOT. 44. MR PADGETT'S HAT (AND MR PADGETT). 58. ANY OF IT. 61. OLLERENSHAW.

He would not discuss sixty-one, beyond saying that it was before his time, that it was entered by his mother, and that it concerned a night in 1987 when Mr Ollerenshaw came in from the Smiths' Hall at Great Dunnage with a prize. He would not discuss sixty-three at all, and nobody in the room will say it aloud. There is a commentary on the list, much longer, which is not allowed out of the inn.

<div class="marginalia">There is a pencil mark on the wall under sixty-three, where sixty-four would go. I asked about it. Mr Kell said that it had been put there in the year 2000, the night the Forty-Eighth's company stayed, by his own hand, at about two in the morning, and that by breakfast he had decided that it was not, after all, a thing that must not happen again, and had not inked it, and had left the pencil, because he had not decided whether it should happen again either. He would not tell me what it was. I believe it concerned Mr Ollerenshaw and Mr Grumnir. - H.B.</div>

## The hooter

The works hooter goes at six every morning, and nobody in Upper Clanging has ever slept through it, because it is the works hooter, and they are a works town, and it gets them up the way the sun gets up other towns. In the year 2000, the morning after they arrived, the Forty-Eighth came down at a quarter to nine.

> "You slept through the six o'clock hooter. Nobody does that."

He said it, I am told, with something like awe. He has said since that he has kept the inn for nine years and had a great many people sleep in it, and that every one of them got up at six, cursing, and that the Forty-Eighth is the only guest he has ever had who slept as if the bed were for sleeping in, and that he has been thinking about it since, and that he has been thinking that perhaps it is.

## Afterwards

He is thirty-three. On the dresser is the best china, under a card, FOR WHEN SHE FLIES, which was dusted every week for as long as anybody can remember, and never used. It has now been used once, on the night the Two Hundred flew, for the whole of the Safety Valve, and Miss Hoyle, who came down, and drank one cup of tea out of it, standing up, and said that it would do, and went back up the hill. The china has been dusted since and put back, with a new card in front of the old one: FOR WHEN SHE FLIES AGAIN.
