<p class="lede"><b>Mr Cobley</b> has fished the duck pond at <a href="../Places/Much_Pottering">Much Pottering</a> since 1970 for <b>Old Tom</b>, the biggest carp in the county, whom nobody has ever seen, which is how you know he is big. He is a grey-haired man in an angler's hat, and he sits on the bank every morning there is, with a rod and a loaf, and he would like you not to splash.</p>

## Old Tom

He says good morning to anybody who comes down to the pond, and then he warns them:

> "Morning. Don't splash. Old Tom's about."

The duck pond is about the size of a large front room, and has one duck on it, and is not, on the face of it, a place for the biggest carp in the county. Mr Cobley is aware of this. He was born in the village in 1934 and has known the pond all his life, and in the spring of 1970, one evening, he saw a ripple on it, in the middle, where the duck was not, of a size and slowness that he could not account for. He went home and came back in the morning with a rod, and he has been there since. He named it Old Tom, because it was old, he said, he could tell, and because it looked like a Tom.

I asked how he knew it was a carp, since he had never seen it. He said that nothing else would be that patient.

> "Thirty years I've fished for him. Biggest carp in the county. Nobody's ever seen him. That's how you know he's big."

He fishes with bread, which the duck takes, mostly. He says the duck is in Old Tom's pay.

## The sea serpent

<a href="Effie_Gotobed">Effie Gotobed</a>, who lives up the road, has written to her pen pal at <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a> since 1997 that there is a sea serpent in the pond. Mr Cobley knows she has. She asked him once, when she was six, what was in the pond, and he said Old Tom, and she said *sea serpent*, and he said *carp*, and they have had the same conversation every week since.

> "Young Effie writes to her pen pal there's a sea serpent in here. I've never once contradicted her."
>
> "I don't know he isn't."

In the year 2000 the pen pal came, <a href="Spare_Cotterill">Spare Cotterill</a>, with the Forty-Eighth, and Effie brought him down to the pond to see the serpent, and Mr Cobley said *carp*, and Effie said *serpent*, and Spare asked how big, and Mr Cobley said that nobody knew. They all looked at the pond for a while. Then something under the water, very large and very slow, turned over, and the duck went up in the air and came down somewhere else and said so.

> "...Thirty years."

Spare said it was quite big. Effie said she had told him. Mr Cobley did not say anything else for the rest of the morning, I am told, and fished on until dark, and caught nothing, and went home, Mrs Throssell says, looking about ten years younger.

<div class="marginalia">I sat with Mr Cobley on the bank for an afternoon, for this article, and did not splash. Nothing happened. The duck had most of the loaf. At the end of it he said that it had been a very good afternoon, and that Old Tom had seen me. I asked whether he wanted to catch him. He thought about it for a long time, and then said that if he caught him he would know how big he was, and then he would only be that big. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

Old Tom has not been seen again, which Mr Cobley says proves it. <a href="Humphrey_Gotobed">Mr Gotobed</a>, who is a retired adventurer and has made himself responsible for the village, walks round the pond with him most evenings, in case Old Tom should turn over again, and Old Tom has not.

The letters between Much Pottering and Upper Clanging have gone on, and do not have to be true, and in one of them the dragon learned to swim, and is now, by letter, in the duck pond. Mr Cobley is furious. A dragon, he says, would put Old Tom off. He has asked Effie to write and ask it to leave, and she has, and it has written back that it will think about it.
