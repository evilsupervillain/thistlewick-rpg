<p class="lede"><b>Mrs Ash</b> of <a href="../Places/Ashcombe">Ashcombe</a> has a kitchen so new the paint still smells of it, with a big window over the lake under which the old village is, and she knits something long and does not stop. She is the mother of Corvin Ash, who sat in one corner of the Slain Wyvern for eleven years as the rival of a great hero, and to whom she wrote twice a week for all eleven of them. He is very deep, her boy. She thought he would like it if the Ruins wrote back. He did.</p>

## The old village

She was born in 1947 in the old Ashcombe, in the valley, in a house on the high street with a kitchen so dark you could not see your potatoes, and married there, and had her boy there, in 1970, and measured him on the kitchen wall, in pencil, every birthday that was a round number or felt like one. In 1989, when he was nineteen, he went off to sit in the corner of the Slain Wyvern at Nether Sopping, and Ashcombe flooded, in his absence, and was a tremendous success: everybody out by dinner, everybody dry by tea, and not so much as a hen lost. They have the Roll.

The village was moved up the hill. The new houses have yellow roofs. She has a new kitchen.

> "Mind the step. It's new. It's all new. Isn't it lovely? The old kitchen was DARK; you couldn't see your potatoes. I can see every potato I own."

## The name

Her boy wrote to say that he had taken the name Ash, after the village, in its memory. She thought, well, that's nice, and took it as well, to save the postman, and now they are all Ashes, and the post comes lovely. I asked her what the old name had been. She said that it was under the lake with the old kitchen, and that she had not been very fond of either, and that she was not going to dredge it up, and laughed, and went on knitting.

## The letters

She wrote to him at the Slain Wyvern every Wednesday and Saturday for eleven years, about the village, the drains, the Flood Dinner, and his room, and he wrote back once, to the Ruins of Ashcombe, to the old address, under the water. The Ruins wrote back. She has never said who wrote it, and she has never needed to.

His room upstairs is bigger now, and has the light, and is a study at the minute, where the papers of the Flood Dinner Committee are kept. Up the wall by the window are pencil marks with ages: C., 6; C., 9; C., 12; C., 16; C., 19. The old ones went under with the old wall. These were put back from memory, and every one of them is a little taller than a boy would have been, and the last is exactly the top of his head. She drew where he would be.

<div class="marginalia">I have stood in that room. The marks are on the wall beside the window, and I measured the last one against my own notes on him, and it is exactly right, to the inch, and she had not seen him for eleven years when she drew it. I asked her how she had known. She said that she had been knitting for him the whole time, and you get to know the length. - H.B.</div>

## The scarf

In the year 2000 her boy came home, and spoke at the Flood Dinner, and brought the name back, and the toast was to the water, which was very good about it. In the front row Mrs Ash cast off the last stitch, and stood, and held up a scarf that went most of the way down the aisle, bright yellow, the yellow of the new roofs, which is the wrong colour for everything Corvin owns. If she had done it in black, nobody would ever see him in it. He wears it everywhere.

## Afterwards

She is fifty-four, and Hon. Sec. of the Flood Dinner Committee, and has already started the next one, which is not a scarf, and which she will not describe, except to say that it is long, and that it is for somebody who is coming up the road to see the chair, and that she does not know who yet, and that she is knitting it to the length anyway, because you get to know.
