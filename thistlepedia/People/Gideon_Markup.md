<p class="lede"><b>Gideon Markup</b> keeps Ye Final Shoppe at <a href="../Places/Penultima">Penultima</a>, the last town before the <a href="../Places/The_Obligatory_Tower">Obligatory Tower</a>, where everything is priced and everything has always been priced. In the window, behind a velvet rope, is the Sword of Ultimate Ending, at nine million, nine hundred and ninety-nine thousand, nine hundred and ninety-nine crowns, which he priced in 1969, and which he asks every customer whether they would like to hold, and then tells them, warmly, that they cannot afford to. He brings his wife her tea every morning, and then he does the window.</p>

## The last shop before the end

He was born in 1944 above a saddler's in Penultima, the son of the saddler, and grew up watching heroes go up the road to the Tower and noticing that every one of them stopped at the last shop on the way, which was his father's, and bought a bridle they did not need, because it was the last chance. In 1969, when his father retired, he turned the saddler's into Ye Final Shoppe, and painted the sign himself, and on the first morning he put the sword in the window, and wrote the card.

The sword, he says, came with the shop. He will not say more than that, and Mrs Markup, asked, says that he is in the window again. My own enquiries, at the saddlers' guild, establish that his father never sold a sword in his life, and that nobody in Penultima remembers one in the shop before 1969, and that I should leave it at that, and I have.

## Seasonal

> "Welcome! What are you buying?"

> "Of course, of course. Take your time. Everything is priced. Everything has always been priced."

Asked about the prices, which are high, he says *seasonal*. "Thirty-one years in this trade and it has been seasonal every one of them. It is a very reliable season." The shop buys at a crown and sells at forty, and Mrs Markup writes down both, and the difference is, he says, the season. He believes it. He has a crate of things bought at a crown each, among them half a potion, and he is waiting for somebody who needs exactly half a potion, and he is sure they are on their way. He sells forty potions each labelled LAST POTION, and two jars each labelled UMBRELLAS - THE LAST ONE, and he does not see a difficulty, because each one is the last one, for somebody.

## The sword

> "Ah. The Sword of Ultimate Ending. Would sir like to hold it?"

> "Sir cannot afford to hold it."

He has said it to every customer for thirty-three years, including the Forty-Eighth, and including me, who did not ask. He set the price at one crown more than a purse will hold, so that it should be out of the reach of anybody who ever walked up the road. A banker in <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a> has told me that a good purse holds a great deal more than that. I have not told Mr Markup. Neither, I understand, has <a href="Nix">Nix</a>, who looked at the sword once, in the year 2000, for some time, and said afterwards that the price was wrong, and that some things are better in a window.

## The discount

He has given one discount in thirty-three years. It was on the first day the shop was open, in 1969: eleven coppers, off a lamp, to a girl who would not stop haggling. It is the first entry in the first volume of the Markup accounts, in his hand: DISCOUNT - ELEVEN COPPERS - WORTH IT.

<div class="marginalia">Mrs Markup, who keeps the ledger, has ruled round that entry twice, and has still got the coppers. She came to Penultima in the spring of 1969, at twenty-three, and was married to him the spring after. I have not asked her whether she was the girl. I have noticed that there is a lamp on her side of the bed which is the oldest thing in the house after the sword, and that it has never been for sale. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

Penultima has done a trade since 2000 in people walking up the road to see Grimspite's chair, and most of them stop at the Final Shoppe, and look at the sword, and are asked whether they would like to hold it, and are told. Mr Markup says it has never been better for business, and that he has had to have the rope re-velveted. The sign over the town has had a fourth line added, LAST CHANCE FOR A SOUVENIR, and he has added a shelf of souvenirs, priced seasonally. He is fifty-seven. He has never once been asked to bring the tea, and he has never once been asked to do the window, and he does both, every morning, in that order.
