<p class="lede"><b>Barnabas Tibbs</b> is the wharfinger of the Bonded Warehouse on the quay at <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a>, in half-rimmed spectacles, and nothing comes out without the ticket, and the duty, and him. For eleven years he kept a locked wardrobe as Bond No. 1, ONE COAT, and the rent came by post every quarter, never late, no name, and he never asked whose it was, because it was not his place. It was in bond. It was safe. That is all a thing wants.</p>

## The quay

He was born in 1934 in a house on the quay, the son of a lighterman, and has lived within sight of the warehouse doors his whole life. He went into the warehouse as a boy, in 1948, and became wharfinger in 1962, and says that in forty years he has never once let a thing out without the ticket, and the duty, and himself, and that this has made him unpopular with merchants, the Corporation, the Iron Company, and on one occasion a duke, and that he sleeps very well.

The bonds are numbered, and are a history of the town in themselves, and he knows every one by heart. No. 2, one statue of the First Mayor, actual size, duty on the hat. No. 7, ten thousand buttons, all different, declared as one button, repeated. No. 11, fog, one barrel, from Nether Sopping, duty paid in advance by Nether Sopping, to be rid of it. No. 31, rain, one barrel, returned by Upper Clanging as second-hand. No. 40, one cabbage, very large, *for the show*. The show was in 1802. Nobody has been brave enough to check on the cabbage, and he includes himself.

## Bond No. 1

> "A coat. Bond number one. Eleven years. Rent comes by post every quarter, never late. No name. Just the money, and 'Bond No. 1'. Never asked, sir. Not my place. It's in bond. It's safe. That's all a thing wants."

In 1989 a girl of fifteen came into the warehouse with a coat that was too big for her, and stood on a crate, and made a speech, and paid the first quarter's rent, and went out, and did not come back. Mr Tibbs wrote in the margin of the Bond Book what she had said: *I shall return for it when I am somebody.* He says that it was a good speech, and that he has had a great many speeches made to him in the warehouse, mostly by merchants about duty, and that it was the only one he ever wrote down.

In the year 2000 she came back, by the long way round, with the Forty-Eighth's company. He knew her at once.

> "Ah. The party of the second part. Eleven years. You stood on that crate, there, and made a speech. 'I shall return for it when I am somebody.' I wrote it in the margin. It was a good speech. And then the rent came by post every quarter for eleven years, never late, no name."

He opened the wardrobe. It fitted. "No duty owing. Rent paid to date. Always was."

<div class="marginalia">He asked her, then, whose it was. It is the only time in forty years he has asked anybody that, and he says that it was not his place, and that he could not help it, and that he has never regretted it. What she told him is in the Bond Book's first entry, which has a name now, and in her article, as much as she will allow, and not here. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

He is sixty-eight. The rent has stopped coming. He says that he misses it, a little, every quarter day, and that he goes down to the post on quarter days anyway, out of habit, and comes back without anything, and that it is the only thing in forty years he has ever missed that was not in bond. Since 2000 he has also held Bond No. 1788, for the Mayor, *money, a little, provenance none whatever*, and the Corporation pays the rent on it, by post, every quarter, and he says that it is not the same.
