<p class="lede"><b>Eudora Pargeter</b> is the retained practitioner who keeps the winter at <a href="../Places/Frostwick">Frostwick</a>, under a contract of nine years, renewed on time. She is not an ice witch, and would like that established before you ask. She is also the only person in the world, so far as I have been able to discover, who has read the Prophecy of Thistlewick all the way through, and the only one who found clause two, and read it out, and did not do the arithmetic for anybody.</p>

## The practice

"Refrigeration. Larders, dairies, the fishmonger's. The winter is one client. A good one." She keeps it the way you keep a larder, only bigger, and with the door open. She does January in an afternoon and is home by four. She works in pale blue, with sensible spectacles, and keeps her gloves on indoors as a matter of course, which is the only thing about her that anybody in Frostwick has ever called theatrical, and she says it is a matter of hygiene.

Her own larder, in the Cold Room, is a shelf of frosted jars in neat rows, labelled JANUARY, and which week. Her box files read CONTRACT; CONTRACT (AMENDMENTS); CONTRACT (AMENDMENTS TO THE AMENDMENTS); and a slim one, COMPLIMENTS.

The contract is on her desk, and Appendix C is the Schedule of Snowfalls, which I have read, with her permission, and which I think is the best-drafted document in this encyclopedia. Ordinary snow: four inches, overnight, not on market days. Blizzard: at the committee's request, in writing, on one week's notice, and not during carols. Festival Day: a light dusting, commencing at the ribbon, to settle on Mr Stagg's hat. Tolerance: plus or minus one flake.

Asked whether she minds keeping a town under a curse, she says that it isn't a curse, it's a contract. "I'd sign it again tomorrow."

## Clause two

In the year 2000 the Forty-Eighth showed her the Prophecy, because he had been told she read contracts, and she read it all the way through, at her desk, which nobody had ever done; not the Committee, not the Quills, not the forty-seven, and not me, until afterwards, and then only because she had. She had three remarks.

> "No break clause. No notice period. And clause twelve renews itself."

> "I'd never sign a thing like that. Somebody's underlined it, I see. Somebody's worried."

And then she turned back to the beginning, to clause two, which every reader since the signing had read straight past, and read it aloud:

> "'A hundred years' is a defined term. 'Such period as the Committee shall find convenient.'"

That is the whole of what she said about it. Then she mentioned that there would be a light dusting on Tuesday, for the look of the thing; it was in the schedule. She did not explain how forty-seven Chosen Ones could have gone "a hundred years" apart and the Forty-Fifth be alive in Nether Sopping and potting things. She did not do the arithmetic for him. She has never done anybody's arithmetic for them, and she sees no reason to start with a document she would not have signed.

<div class="marginalia">I have since done the arithmetic, at some length; it is <a href="../History/Two_Reckonings">Two Reckonings</a>. I sent her a copy. She returned it with a note to say that the arithmetic was correct, and that she had not asked for it.</div>

## Afterwards

She is still in the Cold Room, and the contract is still renewed on time, and the winter is still kept to the flake. Frostwick has forty-two ribbons now. She says that the winter is in very good order, and that it is a client she would recommend.

She has written to me, kindly, to point out a clause. Not one of the Prophecy's: one of mine. In Volume Two, she says, I wrote "a hundred years" eleven times without saying, once, in whose years, and she would draw my attention to the definitions, which in any document come first for a reason. She was right. Volume Three says, every time. She has not said whether she has read Volume Three, and I have not asked, because I would like to go on thinking that she might have, and that she found nothing to point out, and I would rather not know.
