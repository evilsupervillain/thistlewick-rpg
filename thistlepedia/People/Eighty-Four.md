# Eighty-Four

<p class="contributor">Contributed by O. Hoyle, Works Manager</p>

<p class="hatnote">She is not a person, and I have put her in the People volume anyway, because Miss Hoyle asked me to, and when I said that an airship was not a person, sent me, by return, her service record, and a note: <i>She went north up a field on her own for a hundred and forty years. File her where you like.</i> I have filed her here. For the field, see <a href="../Places/The_Long_Field">The Long Field</a>. - H.B.</p>

## Service record

1. **Type.** Steam airship, rigid, single envelope, one boiler of forty-eight tubes. The eighty-fourth attempt of the Hoyle Works to answer the question in the Prophecy, which is how to get up that road without walking. Designed by my grandmother, in her first year in charge, who also drew 82, 83 and 85, and who said of her on the slip that she was *the first of mine I would trust with my dinner.*

2. **Building.** Laid down in bay 84 on 14 April 1857. Three years on the slip. Envelope by the works; frames by the Ollerenshaw forge, three generations back; boiler tubes by the works, brushed by the apprentice of the day, who was the eleventh.

3. **Lifting.** 3 May 1860, nine in the morning, a light westerly. Rose to sixty feet and made north up the line of the field. Came down at nine fifty-one in her own plot, which had been marked out for her the week before, as is the practice, in case. Her plate reads LOST OVER THE LONG FIELD, and the cause line is blank: the only blank cause line in the field. My grandmother cut that plate herself, and left it blank, and I have read her notes, and she did not know, and she would not write down a guess. Neither will I.

4. **Afterwards.** She did not stay in her plot. Over a hundred and forty years she made herself good out of the rows either side of her, a piece at a time, and went north up the field doing it, and left a furrow behind her three feet deep and as wide as her plot. Nobody taught her that. Nobody could have. At the top of the field she got a foot off the ground, and then did not, and then tried again, and was still trying in the year 2000. I have not tried very hard to work out how she was doing it, and I am not going to. The works has a word for an attempt that is still working on the problem. The word is *attempt*.

5. **Stopping.** In the year 2000 she was stopped, at the top of the field. It was the Forty-Eighth who stopped her. I came down from the works afterwards. She had got further than any of them.

6. **Governor.** Her governor, a brass ball governor, which keeps an engine from running away with itself, came off with two turns of a spanner and was given to the Forty-Eighth rather than taken. She never needed it. She did not once run away with herself in a hundred and forty years, and I have a field to prove it.

7. **Bay.** There is a bay in the shed with 84 over it. For a hundred and forty years there was nothing under it. There is now. She was brought in, a piece at a time, on the cart, by Tappet Cotterill, over the summer of 2000, on Tuesdays. It will take years. Nobody has costed it and nobody has asked to.

8. **Name.** She has no name but her number, like all of them. The apprentice asked me in 2001 whether she should have one, since she had earned it. I said that she had a number, and that a number is a name that cannot be argued with. The apprentice has painted a small 84 on the door of her bay, in white, very neatly. I had not asked for that. I have left it.

O. HOYLE, Works Manager. 16 March 2002.

<div class="marginalia">Miss Hoyle says that Eighty-Four is not a person. She has written two pages about her, which is two more pages than she has written about anybody else, including herself, and I have printed them in the People volume, and Miss Hoyle has seen the proof and not objected, which is the first time she has not objected to anything I have sent her. I take that as a correction of the record, and have filed it. - H.B.</div>
