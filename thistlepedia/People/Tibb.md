<p class="lede"><b>Tibb</b> is a boy of <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a> who plays Chosen One on the front with Sops in the afternoons, and who settled, with Sops, the question of who should have to be the Dark Lord, which the Prophecy had not settled in two thousand years, by deciding that nobody should, and that the Dark Lord had stayed at home.</p>

## The settlement

"We're both the Chosen One now. Nobody's the Dark Lord. He's at home." I asked Tibb whose idea that had been. He said it had been both of theirs, at once, on a Tuesday, after a long argument about whose turn it was, and that once you thought of it, it was obvious, and that it was strange nobody had thought of it before. I agreed that it was strange. I have since put the question to the Prophecy Committee, who minuted it as *Noted.*

The difficulty with a game in which nobody is the Dark Lord is that nothing happens in it. He and Sops would walk north along the front together, to the end of the harbour wall, both Chosen, and there would be nobody there, and they would walk back. "Still no Dark Lord," Tibb told the Forty-Eighth. "Still working." It was said without complaint. It was said, I think, the way an engineer says that a thing is under review.

## The list

When Nix and Cobb Lorrimer had their contest on the front in the year 2000, it was Sops and Tibb who wrote the list. Lorrimer came back with a helmet out of the outfitters' window and a sword off the knees of a man having a day off, who came up the street after it in his socks. "I STOLE them," said Lorrimer. "I'm a thief. It's the whole point of me."

> "Mum says we're not to have stolen things. She says it twice."

Nix came back with a rope, a pot and an oar, each lent until sundown and signed for. Then Tibb said, "There's one more thing on it." It was ONE DARK LORD (WHO LOSES). Neither child would be it. Nobody in the town would be it. Lorrimer was it, in a teal hood, under protest, and lost, and has lost every afternoon since, and the game has had, for the first time, somebody at the end of the harbour wall.

<div class="marginalia">I called on his mother, Mrs Ada Tibbett, on Rope Walk, to ask whether the name was short for anything. She said that it was short for Tibbett, which is what we all are, and that I was not to put that in, and that she had said it twice. I have put it in once. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

Tibb is eleven, and he still plays on the front, and is still the Chosen One, and Sops still is as well, and they still do not take turns. Lorrimer comes down the street in the afternoons. Tibb says that Lorrimer loses better than he used to, and has started losing in a different place each day, to keep it interesting, and that yesterday he lost on the harbour steps, and fell in, on purpose, and that it was the best one yet.
