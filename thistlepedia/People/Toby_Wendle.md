<p class="lede"><b>Toby Wendle</b> was the Welcomer at the west gate of <a href="../Places/Gladhollow">Gladhollow</a> in the year 2000, aged ten, with a saucepan on his head. He was allowed one sentence, *Welcome to Gladhollow!*, and no other, and so he worked on it, and had fifteen ways of saying it, written out on his kitchen table under the heading FIFTEEN WAYS. He told the Forty-Eighth everything about the town without saying a single other word. It was all in the duties.</p>

## The saucepan

He was born in 1990 in the house behind his granny's barrels, the younger of two boys, and became the Welcomer at seven, in 1997, when the post fell vacant because the previous Welcomer, his brother Ned, was promoted to Farewell Boy. The post came with a saucepan. The saucepan is the Welcomer's, and has been since anybody can remember; it is the town's, and is kept on a nail by the gate, and is worn on the head, because heroes coming into a town do not look where they are going, and walk into the Welcomer, and it is better for everybody if it rings.

It rings like a bell. He does not fall over. He straightens the saucepan, takes a deep breath, beams, and says *Welcome to Gladhollow!*, and there is no rest of it, and he is delighted.

## Fifteen ways

He is not allowed a second sentence. He has worked on the first. The list on the kitchen table runs: the Standard; the Formal; the Whisper; the Song; the Salute; the Surprised; the Slow One; the Dramatic Pause; the Rainy Day; the Very Loud; the Sorry; the Tour; the Evening; the Classic; and the fifteenth, which in the year 2000 was not finished.

The Forty-Eighth heard all fifteen, in order, over several visits, and at the fifteenth Toby tried it anyway, going up at the end: *Welcome to Gladhollow?* And then he looked anxiously to see whether a question counted as a second sentence. It was decided that it did not. He wrote something on the back of his hand. Once, overexcited, he got as far as "Welcome BACK to Glad-", and stopped dead, and said, very quietly, "...I'm not allowed that one," and then, with enormous restraint, "...Welcome to Gladhollow."

<div class="marginalia">The Slow One, one word at a time, is for anybody from abroad. The job description does not mention anybody from abroad. He checked. He did it anyway, and when I asked him why, he said *Welcome to Gladhollow*, very slowly, one word at a time, and looked at me until I understood that I was from abroad, which, from Nether Sopping, I suppose I am. - H.B.</div>

## The duties

Asked about the town, he says *Welcome to Gladhollow*, with his eyes on the mayor's house, and both hands flat beside him, pushing, like somebody sliding a very heavy bookcase aside. His eyes go to the well, and the pot beside the well, and the well, and the pot, and then, very innocently, to the sky. Then he points at his granny's barrels, and at his granny, and puts his hand on his heart. He has said nothing. It is all in the duties.

What a hero does with this is the hero's business, and the Forty-Eighth found the bookcase, and the well, and the key under the pot, and gave Granny Wendle a potion back out of her barrels, which she said was Tuesday's knee sorted. Toby said *Welcome to Gladhollow!* about it, the Very Loud, which is for people on horses, and nobody was on a horse.

## Granny Wendle

His granny's barrels stand outside her door, and every hero who comes through has the potions out of them. She does not mind. They are very good potions. She would like them back, though. They are for her knees. Each is rolled in a stocking in the straw with a label sewn in, PLEASE RETURN TO MRS WENDLE, GLADHOLLOW. Toby has, since he was seven, quietly gone round after every hero and asked for the stocking back, and has got, by his count, thirty-one stockings and four potions. He says the stockings are the important part. They are hers.

## Afterwards

He is twelve. In the spring of 2001 Ned went to be apprenticed to the wheelwright, and Toby was made Farewell Boy, at the east gate, in the cap, which fits. He has two sentences now, *Farewell from Gladhollow! Mind how you go!*, and he uses both of them with enormous care, and I am told that *Mind how you go* is said fifteen different ways. The new Welcomer is a girl of eight, who has been given the saucepan, and has asked him how he managed. He has not told her, because it is not in his duties. He has given her the list.
