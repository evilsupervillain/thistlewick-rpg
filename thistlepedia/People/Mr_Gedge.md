<p class="lede"><b>Mr Gedge</b> is the Head Porter of <a href="../Institutions/The_Collegium_Arcanum">the Collegium Arcanum</a>, and stands in its gateway, a large man in a dark suit with a full ginger beard. He does not own the gateway. He has the air of somebody who could, and has decided not to bother. The Master is a committee, the Dean is in Hall and out to callers, and the Fellows do not come out, and so for most of the people who have ever come to the Collegium, Mr Gedge is the Collegium, and it is in very good hands.</p>

## The pass

He introduces himself in two words and a title, which is everything the Lodge thinks you need:

> "Afternoon. Gedge. Head Porter."

A visitor who wants to look round will need a visitor's pass, which is issued by the Lodge, on the recommendation of a Fellow, and countersigned by the Lodge. I said, as everybody says, that I did not know any Fellows. "Nobody does, sir. They don't come out." So he recommended me himself, and countersigned the recommendation, and issued it, writing on one card three times in the same hand, and it took about four seconds, and then he explained the Collegium to me in one sentence, which I have since found to be complete:

> "The Master needs a meeting. The Dean needs a form. I need a pencil."

Then: "Hall's on the left, Library's on the right. Keep off the grass." And afterwards, every time I passed him, "Keep to the path, sir. It goes round. Everything here does."

He was born in 1940, and came to the Lodge in 1956, at sixteen, as the most junior of three porters, and the other two retired and were not replaced, because their replacement was referred. He has been Head Porter since 1970, of a Lodge of one. I asked him whether the post had been advertised. He said the Lodge had advertised it, and the Lodge had applied, and the Lodge had been successful, and that the whole thing had taken about four seconds.

The card will get you into the Hall, and up to the Library desk, and no further, because the Sub-Librarian will not accept a letter from anybody who is not a Fellow, and Mr Gedge is not a Fellow. She says that he is a great deal more than one. He does not say anything about that at all, and I think that is because he agrees with her on both points.

## The grass

The Great Court has a lawn, and on the lawn is the Founder, a wizard in stone with his staff held up, whose plinth says that he founded the Collegium in one afternoon, by himself. The plinth cannot be read from the path. Everybody who has ever read it has read it standing on the grass, and every one of them has heard, behind them, in the gateway, somebody clear his throat.

That is all he does. He does not come out on to the lawn, and he has never, so far as anybody can tell me, said a word about it. He clears his throat, once, and you come off the grass, and you go round, because everything here does.

## The board

By the Lodge window is the IN and OUT board, with a brass slide beside each name. THE MASTER: OUT. The slide has rusted there. THE DEAN: OUT, and under it, in the Porter's pencil, *In Hall. Out to callers.* THE SUB-LIBRARIAN: IN. THE HEAD PORTER: IN. The pencil is Mr Gedge's. Everything in pencil at the Collegium is Mr Gedge's, and everything in pencil at the Collegium is correct.

## Miss Vance

In the year 2000 <a href="Zephyrine_Vance">Zephyrine Vance</a> came back to the Collegium for the first time since 1989, with a notice of fines in her hat and the intention of appealing against something she had not yet identified. Mr Gedge was in the gateway. He did not ask for a Fellow's recommendation.

> "Miss Vance. Afternoon. Your pigeonhole's full. Eleven years of it. Library first, I should think."

She had believed for eleven years that she had been expelled. She had not. The ten notices before the one in her hat had gone to her pigeonhole, which was her last address, and he had kept them there, in order, the whole time. "You KNEW," she said. "You knew I'd never been expelled."

> "The Lodge knows everything, miss. The Lodge is never asked."

What was decided at the Library desk that afternoon is in her article, and I am not going to add to it from here. He was sent for in the middle of it, to lift the Register, although it was not a Monday, which had never happened before. He lifted it, and went back to the gate. He greets her there by name now, every time, and what he says after her name depends on the afternoon, and both of the things he says are kind.

## The Honorary Fellow

The Collegium's duck, which is an Honorary Fellow and walks into things, has walked into Mr Gedge on more than one occasion, generally after a milestone and the gatepost, and in that order, and with great dignity. He touches his hat to it. When it was carried to Congregation in 2000, and did what it did there, it was Mr Gedge who took it home to its pond afterwards, in the Lodge's handcart, which it walked into first.

<div class="marginalia">Every letter I have written to the Collegium Arcanum in the course of this history has been answered by Mr Gedge, in pencil, by return. This includes the letters I addressed to the Master, to the Dean, to Congregation and to the Committee for Readers. For this article I wrote to Mr Gedge himself, at the Lodge, and that letter was answered by the Committee for Readers, which regretted that it could not correspond with a person without a reader's ticket, and had referred it to the Lodge. He answered it the next day, in pencil. He said that I might print what I liked, and that I should keep to the path. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

He is in the gateway. The Master's slide is still at OUT. He oils it every spring, so that it would move if anybody asked, and nobody has, and he puts it back where it was. I asked him how many pencils he had been through since 1970. He said one at a time, sir, and that was the end of the interview, and it had taken about four seconds.
