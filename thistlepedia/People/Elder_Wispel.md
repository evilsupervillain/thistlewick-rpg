<p class="lede"><b>Elder Wispel</b> chairs the <a href="../Institutions/The_Prophecy_Committee">Prophecy Committee</a> of <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>, and gives each Chosen One the charge in <a href="../Places/Prophecy_Hall">Prophecy Hall</a>. He is a kind man, and a punctual one, and he has never once in his life noticed anything about the calendar, which in Thistlewick is not a failing but a qualification.</p>

## The charge

The charge is the same every time, and he gives it beautifully. He says the Chosen One's name and number. He unrolls the Prophecy, which keeps unrolling until it reaches the far wall. He says that every hundred years the Dark Lord rises in the <a href="../Places/The_Obligatory_Tower">Obligatory Tower</a>, and every hundred years Thistlewick sends someone, and that it has worked forty-seven times. The Forty-Eighth asked him what had happened the other times. He said that it had worked forty-seven times.

Then he rolls the scroll back up. It takes a while. Nobody helps. The Committee has considered helping, and minuted it, and decided that the rolling is part of the dignity of the office, and also that nobody else knows which way it goes.

Then: take companions, up to three ("that is the clause, not my preference"); go north; kill the Dark Lord; come home. "In that order, ideally." The gold and the potions are from the parish fund. The Prophecy is, regrettably, free.

He is fond of every Chosen One he has charged, and he is not able to say how many that is, because to Elder Wispel every one of them went a hundred years after the last and he has charged them all. I asked him how long he had been Elder. He said, "Oh, a good while," and offered me a biscuit. A man from <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a> notices this sort of thing. A man from Thistlewick does not, and is happier, and I am not going to be the one to tell him. See <a href="../History/Two_Reckonings">Two Reckonings</a>, which he has not read.

## The reference

When the Adventurers' Guild at Nether Sopping asked the Forty-Eighth for a character reference, he walked forty miles back to the Elder for it, as the nearest thing to a recognised authority for eleven leagues. The Elder was extremely pleased to be asked. He was already looking for the good pen before Bram had finished the sentence. "For a GUILD? Do they know who you are?"

He wrote for some time. The reference confirms that Bram Thistle is the Chosen One, is of good character, and grows a decent turnip, and the last part is underlined. He asked that the Guild be told the Committee would welcome a reciprocal arrangement, "by which I mean tell them we exist, because I do not think they know," and that they not be allowed to keep the reference, because it was Committee stationery. They kept it. It is framed in Registrar Pell's office, beside the Charter, and I have seen the underlining.

## Mrs Wispel

It would be incomplete not to mention Mrs Wispel, and inaccurate to say much. She read verse nine aloud at the fete, in error, and there were children present, and there were grandparents present. When the Committee moved to strike verses seven and eight the motion was carried four to one, and the one was Mrs Wispel. The Committee's struck shelves hold a volume called *Things Not To Read Aloud At The Fete*, and inside the cover, in her hand, it says: NOW you tell me. The Elder speaks of her with great respect and changes the subject.

## Afterwards

When the Forty-Eighth came home, the Elder shook his hand with both of his, and asked to see the Receipt, and held it up to the window the way you would a banknote. "Renewable," he said. "What a lovely hand." He reminded Bram about clause nineteen, and the sea air, which is the thing, and which Bram had earned, and the long way, which he had earned as well. Six months later, after a very long meeting, the Committee struck clause nineteen, and the Elder took the chair for all eleven hours of it.

He still chairs the Committee, which still meets, and still consults the Prophecy at every meeting out of habit, and minutes the consultation. Clause twelve has a note in its place, SEE BRAM, and the Committee has twice minuted that it might, and has not needed to. The Elder offered Bram a seat on the Committee, and Bram declined, because it meets in planting time, and the Elder said that he quite understood, and minuted that he did.

I wrote to him, for this article, to ask how many times the Prophecy has worked now. He wrote back, in the good pen: *Forty-seven times.* Under it, in a different ink, as if he had come back to the desk later: *And once otherwise.*
