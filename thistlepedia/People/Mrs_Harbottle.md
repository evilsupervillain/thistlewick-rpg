<p class="lede"><b>Mrs Harbottle</b>, in gold lace, chairs the panel for the Contest at the Sign of the Bell in <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a>, and the panel practises on everything between contests, because the Contest is always tonight and has simply not had the entries. The fire was a six. The door was a four, because it creaks in the wrong key. Your entrance was a five. Your second entrance, a five.</p>

## The panel

She was born at the Bell in 1971, during a Contest, which her mother, who chaired the panel before her, marked. It got an eight. She was brought up at the judges' table, and was scoring the soup by six, and was co-opted to the panel at eighteen, and has chaired it since her mother retired in 1996. She married Mr Harbottle, a brass-founder, in 1994, and the panel gave the service a seven, and the cake a nine, and Mr Harbottle a six, which she says he took extremely well, and which she has since revised upwards, by private ballot, several times.

> "Harbottle. I chair the panel for the Contest at the Sign of the Bell. We are practising. Everything. The fire was a six. The door was a four; it creaks in the wrong key."

> "Mr Ambler gives everything a nine. Mr Ambler cannot hear. We have never had the heart. It averages out. Miss Quayle gives everything a two."

## The Contest

The Contest at the Sign of the Bell is always tonight. In the year 2000 it had four entries, for the first time in some years: <a href="Septimus_Dray">Mr Septimus Dray</a>, on the serpent, for eleven minutes; <a href="Lettice_Moon">Miss Lettice Moon</a>, Ballad (Sad), in which everybody dies, including the narrator, who comes back for the chorus; Mr Tweddle, Birdsong (Open); and Piper Quill, Ballad (Not Sad), which nobody had ever entered.

Mr Tweddle's thrush that had lost its keys drew a three from Miss Quayle, which had never happened, and the room gasped. Miss Quill came second, by one point, to the thrush. Mrs Harbottle announced the result standing, in gold lace, and says that it was the best Contest in the history of the Bell, and that she gave it, privately, a nine, which she has never given anything, and that she has told nobody but me.

<div class="marginalia">She gave this article, in draft, a six. She said that it was well constructed, and accurate, and that its second paragraph creaked in the wrong key. I have not been able to find where. She says that I would not, and that that is why she is on the panel and I am not. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

She is thirty. The Bell has had more entries since 2000 than in the twenty years before it, because people walking up to see <a href="Grimspite_the_Inevitable">Grimspite</a>'s chair stop on the way, and because Piper Quill wrote about the Contest. Mr Ambler still gives everything a nine. Miss Quayle has given two threes since, and the room gasped both times. The fire is still a six. The door has been oiled, and is now a five, and Mrs Harbottle says that she preferred it before, because a four was at least something.
