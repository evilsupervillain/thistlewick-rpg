<p class="lede"><b>Ida Stagg</b> is the wife of Perce Stagg, the man who broke the winter at <a href="../Places/Frostwick">Frostwick</a>, and she loves the Festival, and she will tell you so, and then she will tell you that he did one great thing forty years ago, and a great many things since, and that none of those ever got a ribbon. Then she will ask you not to tell him she said so, because he'll only go pink again.</p>

## Frostwick

She was born in 1937 in Frostwick, under the old winter, and grew up in it, and says that until she was twenty-two she had never seen a gutter run. She was in the square on the evening in the spring of 1960 when a young man in a red coat came down the hill at dusk and the snow began to drip, and she says that she was not the first to cheer, but she was the loudest, and that he looked at her, because of the noise, and went pink, and that she had decided by the time he got to the bottom of the hill.

They were married the next spring, the day after the first Festival, at which he had cut the ribbon, and on which, she says, he had been so pink all day that she was worried he would go pink at the altar and not stop, and he did, and it has never really stopped since, and she has never minded.

## The great many things since

> "Mind it? I love it. Forty years of it. He cuts the ribbon, the whole town cheers, and he goes pink."

> "He did one great thing forty years ago. And a great many things since. None of THOSE ever got a ribbon."

I asked her for the great many things, for this article, and she gave me a list, on condition that it did not go any further than the encyclopedia. It is long. It includes the lanterns, every year, two hundred and twelve; the roof of the Working Group's room, twice; <a href="Wilhelmina_Blagg">Mrs Blagg</a>'s mother's garden wall; the year the well froze and he carried water up the hill for six weeks; the dog; and *every morning, the fire, before I am up, for forty years, without once being asked*. I have not printed the rest. She has asked me to say that it goes on.

<div class="marginalia">Clem Blagg, at seven, told the Forty-Eighth that Nan says Mrs Stagg has never once been cold, not in forty winters, and that it must be Mr Stagg's lanterns, because he does ever such good lanterns. The committee found the ceiling very interesting. I put Clem's observation to Mrs Stagg. She said that it was the fire, every morning, before she was up, and that the boy was quite right, and that I was to say so, and that Mrs Blagg was to stop looking at the ceiling. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

She is sixty-four. She has seen forty-two Festivals, and loved them all, and has started a ribbon of her own, which she ties to the crown every year, under his, a little lower, plain blue, with nothing written on it. I asked her what it was for. She said it was for the great many things since, and that nobody had to know, and that he did not, and that I was not to tell him, because he'll only go pink again.
