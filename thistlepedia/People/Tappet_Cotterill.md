<p class="lede"><b>Tappet Cotterill</b> is the fourth of the ten Cotterills of <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>. She brings the castings up from the foundry to the Hoyle Works on Tuesdays, six crates, and puts them in the same place as always, and for four years she watched the <a href="../History/The_Flight_of_the_Two_Hundred">Two Hundred</a> not fly from the bottom of the gantry while she did it. She would still have got in it. When it flew she stood under the empty gantry and said, "It flew," and that was all she said, grinning, and that is, I think, the best review any airship has ever had.</p>

## Tuesdays

She has carted for the foundry since she was fifteen, in 1997, with the foundry's pony, whose name is Gearbox, and who was named by her, and who is the only creature in Upper Clanging named by a Cotterill other than <a href="Nora_Cotterill">Mrs Cotterill</a>, <a href="Ernest_Cotterill">Mr Cotterill</a> or <a href="Spare_Cotterill">Spare</a>. On Tuesdays she brings the castings up the hill to the works, six crates of them, and backs the cart in under the gantry, and asks, every week, "Castings. Six crates. Where do you want them?" and is told, every week, the same place as always, and puts them there.

Nobody ever asks her anything else. "Nobody does," she says. "That is the trouble with this place."

## The thing

For four years the Two Hundred sat in its gantry, and did not fly. Tappet backed her cart in underneath it every Tuesday and looked up at it. "I bring the castings up on Tuesdays. I have watched that thing not fly for four years." And then, without a pause, because she had obviously been thinking it for most of the four: "I would still get in it."

<div class="marginalia">I asked Miss Hoyle whether Tappet had ever been up in one. She said that Tappet had never asked. I told Tappet. Tappet said that nobody asks her anything, and that she had not known you were allowed to go the other way. - H.B.</div>

In the year 2000 it flew. She was under the gantry on a Tuesday, with six crates, when she saw that it was empty, and she stood there with the reins in her hand, and said: "It flew." Then she went on grinning, for some time, and Gearbox ate a crate lid. She does not say more about it than that, when asked. She does not need to. "Tuesdays," she says. "And it is still here." She means the gantry, and she means herself, and she is perfectly happy about both.

## The dinner hour

She is a third of the shift at the Hoyle Works at the dinner hour, which is Miss Hoyle, and Nib, and Tappet, and at the Hoyle Works that is all of it. They sit along the gantry with their tins, and when Mrs Cotterill came up to read out Spare's letters from the road, Tappet always asked first which one it was. "Is it the dragon one?"

The works had a sweep on the dragon, all month. Nib had Thursday. Tappet had *there is no dragon*. There was no dragon. Nib paid her, and was not happy about it. It is the only bet Tappet has ever made. She says that she is retiring on it, which is a joke, because the sweep was for a farthing a head and there were three heads.

## Afterwards

She still brings the castings up on Tuesdays. The keel of the Two Hundred and First was laid in 2001, in the old gantry, and Tappet backs the cart in under it every week, and looks up. She has asked Miss Hoyle, this time, whether there will be room aboard. Miss Hoyle told her that it was not a question with an answer; it was a question with a schedule. Tappet has asked to see the schedule. She has been shown it. She is on it, in pencil, under CREW, with a question mark, which she says is the first time anybody has ever written her down for anything but a delivery, and she would like to keep the question mark, thank you, because a question mark means somebody is thinking about it.
