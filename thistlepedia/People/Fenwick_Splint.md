<p class="lede"><b>Fenwick Splint</b> is the custodian of <a href="../Places/The_Bottomless_Pit">the Bottomless Pit</a>, a hole in a hillside in the western hills which is a wonder of the district and four feet deep. He keeps a hut, a rope and a turnstile, and takes two crowns at it, and tells every visitor to mind the step, and then that there isn't one, and that that is the joke. For forty years he told the <a href="../Institutions/The_Adventurers_Guild">Adventurers' Guild</a> that there was a badger. There was a badger.</p>

## The custodian

He introduces himself as he introduces the pit, all at once and with the worst of it put fairly:

> "Fenwick Splint, custodian. Two crowns and you may look into the Bottomless Pit, which is a wonder of the district and, I'll be straight with you, four feet deep."

Asked why it is called the Bottomless Pit, he says: "Because 'the Pit' is not a wonder of the district." This is the whole of the case, and I have not heard it improved on.

The board at the turnstile reads THE BOTTOMLESS PIT. ADMISSION 2 CROWNS. CHILDREN 1 CROWN. NO REFUNDS RE: DEPTH. He would let you in for nothing, he says, but then it would not be an attraction, would it. He is not mean about the two crowns. He is firm about them, which is a different thing, and is the thing that makes a hole in a hillside a wonder of the district instead of a hole in a hillside.

He also considers himself the parish, for the purposes of the pit. The lost property at the far end belongs to the parish by rights, he says, and the parish is him, and he says keep it. I asked Registrar Pell whether this was so, and he said that it was not, strictly, and that nobody had ever wanted to argue with Mr Splint about a sock.

## The badger

The Guild was founded in 1960, provisionally. Mr Splint's first report reached it the same year, and said that something had moved in down the far end and was eating the lost property: two hundred years of it, and it had *eaten* it. The Guild said that a badger was not a monster. He said, come and see the badger. They did not come and see the badger.

He went on reporting it, every year, for forty years. <a href="Lionel_Pell">Registrar Pell</a> tells me that the reports are all on file, under S, and that they are the only correspondence in the Guild's keeping that never once asked for anything but a visit. The Guild's position was correct throughout. A badger is not a monster. It was not, however, a small badger.

In 2000 he put it to the Forty-Eighth's company instead, lowering his voice to do it, at the turnstile, and they paid their two crowns, minded the step, and went down and saw the badger. It was the size of a hay cart, and asleep on everything Nether Sopping had ever failed to come back for, and it left, at speed, up a slope nobody knew was there, and took one boot with it. When they came back up he said:

> "You've done it. You actually did it."

And he looked into the pit for a while. Then: "Forty years I've had a badger. I told the Guild. They said a badger wasn't a monster. I said come and see the badger. They never came and saw the badger." He paid them seven hundred crowns and three Hi-Potions, which is a great deal for the custodian of a four-foot pit, and I do not know where it came from, and I have decided that it came from forty years of two crowns at a time, and I have not asked him in case it did. Admission is free for them now, for ever. "It's four feet deep and you're welcome in it whenever you like."

<div class="marginalia">I paid my two crowns and went down, for the article on the pit. Mr Splint told me to mind the step, and that there wasn't one, and that that was the joke. I said I had heard it was. He said that he did it every time, and that it had never been told to a historian before, and that he would like that noted. It is noted, in two articles now, which he says is more than the badger got. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

The Guild has not yet sent anybody to see the badger. Mr Pell says that there is no longer a badger to see, which is true, and that the matter is therefore closed, which Mr Splint says is also true, and is exactly what he would expect of them. He has put up a second line on the board under the first: SITE OF THE BADGER, 1960-2000. He says it brings people in. It is the first thing at the pit that is not four feet deep.
