<p class="lede"><b>Halloran Vance</b> was the Forty-First Chosen One of <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>, who went north in 1940 and came back from the marshes with nothing, to a whole village waiting on the quay at <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a> for a monster. So he brought them one. It took him two days, with wire, and a goose, and he never once smiled about it, and it has been a wyvern over the fireplace of the <a href="../Places/The_Slain_Wyvern">Slain Wyvern</a> for sixty years.</p>

There is no portrait of him in this volume. There is one on the Wall of the Forty-Seven in <a href="../Places/Prophecy_Hall">Prophecy Hall</a>, with a line of Quill verse under it, and I have not reproduced the verse, because it mentions a wyvern.

## The goose

What happened in the marshes he never said. That he came home with nothing, to a quay full of people who had been promised a monster, is the whole of what is known, and Dorcas Thrupp, who keeps the Wyvern and who had the story from those who were there, tells it the same way every time:

> "He was a good man, and he needed it to be a wyvern, and it's been a wyvern in my house for sixty years. So."

For sixty years the whole of Nether Sopping agreed that it was a wyvern, which in a town of people who were pensioned for noticing things is a remarkable act of kindness, kept up daily.

In the year 2000 <a href="Wren_Halloway">Wren Halloway</a> looked at it, and said what it was, *Anser anser*, greylag, adult female, with two bats' wings wired on and the whole thing varnished, and was asked to leave, not for good, while Dorcas decided. She was entirely right, and she was asked to leave, and both of those were true at once.

## The plaques

There are two plaques now, one either side of the fire. The first is the wyvern's, as it has been for sixty years. The second, which Miss Halloway paid for at a crown a letter, a hundred and twenty-eight crowns, reads:

<div class="stamp">A GREYLAG GOOSE, ANSER ANSER, OF A FARM ON THIS COAST. MADE INTO A WYVERN IN 1940, IN TWO DAYS, WITH WIRE, BY H. VANCE, CHOSEN ONE #41, BECAUSE THE VILLAGE WAS WAITING ON THE QUAY.</div>

It does not correct the first plaque, and does not repeat it, and it is, I think, the only memorial anywhere to the Forty-First that has his reason on it. It is the kindest thing in the room. Both plaques are true. Nobody in the room looks at either of them while you are looking at them, which is its own kind of answer.

## Afterwards

Both plaques are still up, and neither mentions the other. Miss Halloway is welcome in the Wyvern now, and sits under the goose, by choice, and Mrs Thrupp dusts both plaques on the same morning, in the same order, and has never said which she does first. On the anniversary of the quay, which nobody marks and everybody knows, somebody puts a half on the mantel under the bird, and it is gone by closing.
