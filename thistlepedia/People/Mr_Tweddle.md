<p class="lede"><b>Wilfred Tweddle</b> of <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a> does birdcalls: a nightingale, a curlew, and a thrush that has lost its keys, which goes down very well. In the year 2000 he entered the Contest at the Sign of the Bell, Birdsong (Open), and the thrush went looking for its keys all round the room and found them, and drew a three from Miss Quayle, at which the room gasped, and he won, by one point, over Piper Quill.</p>

## The Wire Works

He was born in 1939 at the Wire Works, where his father drew wire, and drew wire there himself for forty-six years, from fourteen. The Wire Works is the loudest of the nine, and he says that he started doing birds to have something in his head that was not the drawing-bench, and that by the time he was twenty he could do any bird in the valley, and that the only one he could not do was a thrush, because a thrush never sings the same thing twice, and he could not settle on one.

He retired in 1999 and started practising for the Contest, which he had meant to enter every year for forty years and never had, because there was always the bench.

> "Tweddle. Birdcalls. I've been practising since Easter. I do a nightingale, a curlew, and a thrush that's lost its keys. The thrush goes down very well."

## The thrush

The thrush that has lost its keys was, he says, an accident. He was practising the thrush in his kitchen at Easter, and could not settle on what it was singing, and it went up and down, looking for something, and his wife said, from the scullery, *It sounds as if it's lost its keys,* and he said that it had, and went on, and the thrush looked under things, and in pockets, and behind the clock, and found them, and he has done it that way since.

At the Contest he did the nightingale, which got sevens, and the curlew, which got sevens, and then the thrush, which went all round the room looking for its keys, and under the judges' table, and in Mr Ambler's hat, and found them, at the end, in the last place you would look, and Miss Quayle, who gives everything a two, gave it a three. The room gasped.

<div class="marginalia">I asked Miss Quill whether she minded coming second to a thrush. She said that it was a very good thrush, and that she had been beaten by better songs and worse birds, and that she had written him a verse, which is in her notebooks, and that it rhymes *keys* with *please*, which is not her best, but was what the thrush would have wanted. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

He is sixty-two. The cup is on his mantelpiece, beside his wife's clock. He has been asked to judge, and declined, and asked to enter again, and declined, on the grounds that the thrush has found its keys now, and that he would have to lose them again, and that it would not be the same. He still does the thrush in the kitchen, for his wife, on Sundays, and it still loses its keys every time, and finds them, and she still says, from the scullery, *They're behind the clock,* and they always are.
