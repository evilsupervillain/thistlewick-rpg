<p class="lede"><b>Lavinia Barrow</b> keeps Wick &amp; Barrow, Outfitters, on the front at <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a>: everything an adventurer needs, and four things nobody does. She sells the hat that the Guild's Form A-1 requires, and is not related to the Registrar. At the end of the row of pegs on her wall is one with nothing on it, which she dusts, which is Wick's. Wick went north, the same as they all do, forty-one years ago this spring.</p>

## Wick and Barrow

She was born in 1936 in Nether Sopping, above a chandler's, and went into the outfitting trade in 1958, at twenty-two, in partnership with Wick, who had the shop and the stock and the sign, and who needed, he said, somebody who could add. The sign went up that spring: WICK &amp; BARROW, OUTFITTERS. Two names. In the spring of 1959 Wick went north. Everybody went north, that year and the next; it was a year ahead of the Chosen One, and not everybody went together.

Wick has not come back, and nobody has heard where he got to. She counts her years in the trade from 1961, not 1958, and I asked her once why. She said that 1961 was when she stopped expecting Wick back on the Monday and started doing the books properly, and that before that she had only been minding the shop. She would not say what Wick was to her, beyond the sign, and I did not press it, and I have been told by three people in Nether Sopping three different things, and I am not printing any of them.

> "The sign says Wick and Barrow. That's two names, and one of them's still walking about, and the other one went north, and both belong on a sign."

<div class="marginalia">Wick's peg is the last on the row. Nothing has hung on it in forty-three years. It is dusted every morning, before the shop opens, after the counter and before the window, which is to say second. - H.B.</div>

## The trade

"Everything an adventurer needs, and four things nobody does." Which four, she will not say; she says that you would only buy them. She sells the hat for thirty, because the Guild's form requires a hat and does not say which, and she is not related to the Registrar, whatever anybody says, and nobody has ever said it to her face, because she sells the hat. She sells lamp oil, and DRAGON REPELLENT, which is lamp oil, and says so in the small print. She sells chalk for marking the walls of dungeons, finding your way out not included. She sells ten-foot poles. Everybody buys one. Nobody has ever said what for. She says that she has not asked, because it is not her business what a customer does with ten feet, and that it is the best-selling line in the shop.

She also engraves. In the year 2000 <a href="Wren_Halloway">Wren Halloway</a> came in to have a brass plaque cut for the goose at the <a href="../Places/The_Slain_Wyvern">Slain Wyvern</a>, and asked the price. "Letters, love, not words. Let's see." It came to a hundred and twenty-eight letters, and a great deal of money, and was paid, and was cut, and Mrs Barrow said, "That's yours, done. Mind the edges, and mind whose wall it goes on," which is advice she gives with every plaque, and which on this occasion was very much to the point.

## On account

In the year 2000 the <a href="../Institutions/The_Hoyle_Works">Hoyle Works</a> at <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>, by the Forty-Eighth's hand, ordered forty bolts of oilskin from her for an airship. She counted them out loud, twice, and made somebody count them again after her. "Forty." And then, when the party said forty again: "I heard you the first time. I was enjoying it." They were bought on account.

> "Thirty-nine years in this trade, and I have never once had cause to write 'on account'."

> "North? Well. Something's going the right way up that road for once."

The account was settled, in full, by <a href="Ott_Hoyle">Miss Hoyle</a>, by post, inside the month, with a note that said *Received with thanks. It flew.* Mrs Barrow has it framed, beside the till, under the price of the hat.

## Afterwards

She is sixty-five, and the shop is as it was, except that the bench in eleven pieces that leaned against the wall for years under its card, NOBODY HAS EVER BOUGHT THIS, has gone, to the top of a barrow in the hills, facing the sea, and she has not ordered another, because she says that it would not be the same bench, and that she does not stock things to make a point. She has taken down the card. She has kept it. It is on Wick's peg.
