<p class="lede"><b>Hester Lumb</b> is the chair of the Gloamwood Society, which has met at the <a href="../Places/The_Gilded_Turnip">Gilded Turnip</a> on Thursdays since 1960 to prove that there is nothing in <a href="../Places/The_Gloamwood">the Gloamwood</a>. She is a small woman with grey hair in a bun and a green cloak she wears indoors, in case there is news, and she does the talking. For forty years the Society walked the wood once a year with lanterns and found nothing that would stand up. In the year 2000 Miss Halloway proved it, and Mrs Lumb has been happy ever since.</p>

## Thursdays

The Society has a table by the door of the Turnip, and three of its nine are always at it: Mrs Lumb, who chairs; Mr Dunmore, the secretary, who edits *The Sighting*; and Miss Pellow, at the end, who saw it. Mrs Lumb introduces it to anybody who comes in, whether they have asked or not:

> "The Gloamwood Society. Thursdays. Nine members, forty years, and a walk once a year."

And then, because it is the Society's business and nobody else will do it:

> "Any sightings? We take anything. A shape. A smell. A feeling."

She was born in Thistlewick in 1931, and has walked the Gloamwood since she was a girl, to the mouth of it and a little way in and back, like everybody in the village, and saw nothing, like everybody in the village. In 1960, at the Turnip, on a Thursday, nine people who had all heard about the Thing in the Woods, and none of whom had seen it, agreed over a second pint that it ought to be settled one way or the other. Mrs Lumb said that somebody ought to prove it. She was made chair, she says, because whoever says that somebody ought to is.

The Society's object, in its rules, which Mr Dunmore wrote out on the back of the Turnip's bill of fare and which are framed by the door, is *to establish that there is nothing in the Gloamwood*. I asked her why not *to establish what is in it*. She said that they had all lived beside it for thirty years, and that you would know.

## The proof

The Forty-Eighth's party came in one Thursday in the year 2000 with <a href="Wren_Halloway">Miss Halloway</a> among them, and Mrs Lumb knew her at once. "You'll be Miss Halloway. The one who said the goose was a goose." Miss Halloway asked to see the sightings. Mr Dunmore brought forty years of *The Sighting* out from under the table, bound by the year, and she read all of it, and it took the rest of the afternoon, and then she said that she could prove the Thing did not exist, and never had.

> "Can you? Oh - can you really?"

The walk was that night. It is always that night, Mrs Lumb says, when there is news, and in forty years there had not been any. Nine members in good boots, with lanterns and flasks, stood round the deep clearing where it is seen most, and Mrs Lumb said, "Miss Halloway. In your own time." The proof is in the Bestiary, and I will not give it here, except to say that it had nine parts, and that Mrs Lumb heard it out to the end and then said, after a while, "Forty years. With lanterns. We've been chaperoning snakes." Miss Halloway said that they had done it very conscientiously, and that nobody had got up to anything. Then the Society cheered.

## Miss Pellow

One member did not cheer, and Mrs Lumb saw it before anybody, because she is the chair. <a href="../Places/The_Gloamwood">Miss Edith Pellow</a> had seen it, in 1971, and written it up, and stood a little apart with her lantern looking at the place where it had been. Nobody said anything to her. Mrs Lumb took her lantern, and Mr Dunmore took her arm, and on the walk home the whole Society contrived, without anybody arranging it, to be on either side of her. I asked Mrs Lumb afterwards who had arranged it. She said that nobody had. Then she said that it was what a chair was for, and that you did not minute it.

By the edge of the wood Miss Pellow was telling everybody about the nine. At the Turnip, Mrs Lumb gave Miss Halloway honorary membership, and the badge. "We've never given one away before." There were ten badges made in 1960, one for each member and one spare, in case. It was the spare.

<div class="marginalia">The Forty-Eighth came in to the Turnip a good deal that year, and sat at the next table, and Mrs Lumb asked him every Thursday whether he had any sightings, and he said that he had not. He had been in the clearing himself, before any of them, and come out of the north end of the wood with a sword that needed cleaning. I asked him why he had never told the Society. He said that they had not asked about that. He has not told me either. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

> "Forty years, and proved. Next year's walk is going to be marvellous. There's nothing there."

It was. The walk of 2001 was three hundred and twelve strong, and Miss Pellow led it, and nothing was found, and it was minuted with satisfaction. At the general meeting after it a new member asked what the Society was for now that it had done it, and Mrs Lumb said that a thing that has been proved wants keeping proved, or people start taking anything again. The motion to carry on was carried nine to nothing, with one honorary abstention by post. *The Sighting* still comes out four times a year, and every issue since the hundred and sixtieth has reported nothing, in full.
