<p class="lede"><b>Roland Fairweather</b> is the finest swordsman of his generation, magnificent, glowing and extremely good at everything, and for most of his life the protagonist of a much more expensive story than the one he was in. He joined the Forty-Eighth in the <a href="../Places/The_Slain_Wyvern">Slain Wyvern</a>, carried more than his share all the way north, liked everybody, and left at the door of the <a href="../Places/The_Obligatory_Tower">Obligatory Tower</a>, as he had warned them he would, and gave them his sword.</p>

He warned them in the first minute. "I have three things to tell you and you will only like two of them. One: I am extremely good at this. Genuinely. Embarrassingly. Two: I will come with you gladly, for nothing, and I will carry more than my share, and I will like you. Three: I cannot be there at the end."

## The doors

He has been doing this since he was eleven, when he was page to Dame Florence Ickworth against the Wyrm of Ickworth, in 1977, and could already disarm her. He left at the wyrm's door. They had sent for him. Dame Florence and the others did the wyrm themselves.

It happened at every door after that. He would turn up, help, and be called away before the last room. "It isn't cowardice. It isn't a curse. It's the shape of the thing." He tried to stay, very hard, twice. The Forty-Eighth's was the eleventh, and it happened at the tower door, in front of the whole party:

"I have to be somewhere. I don't know where. I never know where. I only ever know that it is not here, and that it is now, and that I am going. ... Take it up there. It is a very good sword and it has never been in a last room. Neither have I. One of us should manage it."

He is the only member of the company who does not tell me he was at the top of the Tower. He was not.

## The appointment

What he was called away to, every time, was a **review meeting**. Roland Fairweather has a prophecy of his own. It is a proper one, with a lineage, a sword in a stone, a sorcerer and a Worm, and in 1978 he was told it was nearly ready. A committee of four has met about it ever since, in the snug of the Bell at <a class="new" href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a>, under a Convenor, and at every meeting it has gone through readiness - the stone, the sword, the sorcerer, the lineage - and found it still not ready, and noted his continued availability, and thanked him for his patience. A meeting takes eleven minutes. When the party followed him to one, it was the hundred and forty-first. "Mr Fairweather. Good. We're quorate."

Whenever somebody else's story came to its last door, the committee sent for him, to check that he was still available, because he was the asset, and the asset must be accounted for.

<div class="marginalia">I have read a set of the minutes. They are dry and they are devastating, and I have nothing to add to them.</div>

## His own quest

The prophecy named a thing in a place: a Worm, under Carn Drey, east of Great Dunnage, with a stone. The party went, with him, and did it. The thing had been scaled for a hero of nineteen, and he was thirty-four, and it took an afternoon, and it was over before Piper Quill had got a verse out. "That's it. That was it. Twenty-two years." He asked them, carefully, and without much hope, whether they would mind being surprised. Only a little. Everybody was.

I have it from everybody who was there that it was kind, and was not sad, and should be told completely straight, and I have tried to.

## The sword

The sword he left at the tower door has a maker's mark on it, and Hob Grumnir knew it. It was made in the Thistlewick forge in 1960 by Beatrice Grumnir, Hob's aunt, and sold to a man going south, and it had travelled, and come back. "I leave it at a door," Roland says. "Every time. And every time, somehow, it finds its way back to me." Hob put a Thistlewick haft on it. It went into the last room after all, in a Thistlewick hand.

## The alumni

The parties he had left at their doors held a dinner, at the Slain Wyvern, organised by Dame Florence. Every one of them had finished its quest without him, and said so, warmly, and every one of them had thought of him as *their* Roland, and found that there were ten other tables. It was a wholly happy room. Nobody said the word. It ended in a toast: to the man who got them all to the door.

## The understudy

<a class="new" href="Maud_Penhallow">Maud Penhallow</a> had been in Fellow Blunt's yard at Great Dunnage since she was nine, learning sword, horse, lineage and how he stands, as his understudy, in case the prophecy was ready and he was unavailable. Nobody had told her about the meetings. He told her. She took it well, and thought about it, and asked a question none of the grown-ups had thought of in twenty-two years. I have it. I am not going to print it. Nobody has answered it.

When the party went up the Tower, she waited at the door. "Somebody ought to see the end of one."

## Afterwards

The prophecy is discharged. He was rehired after the ending, from his table, and went back up the Tower with the party, and was not called away, and asked what the bit after is like. "Finished? Marvellous. I've never been in the bit after. Is it nice?" Miss Penhallow came and told him how the Quest had ended, all of it, in order.

Since then he has finished two further things, which he has not told me about, but which he keeps on a list, and shows people. He is still asked to leave at doors, out of habit, by committees. He mostly does not.

<div class="marginalia">I asked him whether he minds that he was not at the top. He said that he had been at eleven doors and the bit after, and that the Forty-Eighth had been at the top, and that between them they had very nearly the whole of one, which is more than most people get. Then he asked me how my history ended. I told him I would let him know.</div>
