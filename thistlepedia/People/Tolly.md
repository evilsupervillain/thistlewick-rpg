<p class="lede"><b>Tolly</b> carried the maps for the Forty-Sixth's party in 1980, and was there when he sat down. She has written to him every month since, in the narrow hand, and she does the stamps, two to a letter, because it is a long way, and he is worth two stamps. She addresses the envelopes, and that is how I have his name.</p>

## The maps

She was the party's map-reader, at twenty, in 1980, having been for four years the junior clerk at the harbour office who kept the charts. She says that she had the whole of the road north drawn out in pencil, every turning to the Obligatory Tower, on a sheet she had copied herself, and that they used about an inch of it. She still has the sheet. The inch is worn through. The rest is as clean as the day she drew it, and she says that is how it should be, and that a map nobody uses is a map that has kept somebody safe.

## The letters

She writes once a month, and Nabb writes once a month, and the letters go out on Thursdays. Nabb thinks that she does not know they are never answered.

> "She asks about the letters, does she. Nabb thinks I don't know they're unanswered. Nabb has thought that for eleven years."

She has known all along. She has known, too, what the Forty-Sixth told the Forty-Eighth: that reading them is the answer. She has not told Nabb. She says that Nabb is happier thinking Nabb is protecting her from something, and that she is happier letting Nabb think it, and that the Forty-Sixth is happiest of all, sitting down, and that it would be a poor sort of friend who spoiled a thing like that by explaining it.

> "Two stamps. It's a long way, and he's worth two stamps."

## The sheet of stamps

In 1989 she bought a whole sheet of stamps, once, forty of them at the old rate, off the counter at the Wyvern, to save going in every month, and was very pleased with herself, and put it down on the counter while she paid for her half, and it was gone. She never found out where. She went back to buying them in twos the next month, and has bought them in twos since, and says that she found she did not mind, because going in to buy them had turned out to be part of it.

In the year 2000 Nix came into the Wyvern with the eleven things on the first page of her ledger, which she had taken in 1989, at fifteen, and had never sold, and was taking back to their owners. "Stamps. A sheet of forty, off the Wyvern's counter. The old rate."

> "We don't buy them by the sheet. We buy them in twos."

"You bought a sheet once," said Nix. "Eleven years ago. I've had it ever since." Tolly counted them. There were forty.

> "Twenty letters, then. He's worth two."

She went back to her envelope, and it had two stamps on it before she had finished the sentence.

<div class="marginalia">The rate has gone up since 1989, and a stamp at the old rate is not, strictly, enough for the Gloamwood. Tolly knows this. She has made up the difference every month in coppers, taped to the envelope, and the man who collects the post has never once said anything, and the Forty-Sixth, she says, will have noticed, and will think it is very funny, and will not say so either. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

She is forty-two. She still writes in the narrow hand, and still lets Nabb think what Nabb thinks. She is working through the sheet, two at a time, and has used, as I write, twenty-two of the forty, and says that when it is done she will go back to buying them in twos, on Thursdays, and that she is looking forward to it.
