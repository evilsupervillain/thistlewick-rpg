<p class="lede"><b>Prudence Thrupp</b> keeps the <a href="../Places/The_Gilded_Turnip">Gilded Turnip</a>, the inn on the square at Thistlewick, where three of the Forty-Eighth's companions were found, one of them on a table. She is the younger sister of Dorcas Thrupp of the Slain Wyvern at Nether Sopping, and the two of them did not speak for thirty years and sent each other things instead, which she says was not a falling-out. In the year 2000 she gave the Forty-Eighth a jar to take south. It was warm on one side.</p>

## The Turnip

She was born upstairs at the Turnip in 1951, during last orders, which her father said was the most consideration any child of his had ever shown the trade, and was behind the bar by eight, reading customers. She took the house over in 1976, when her father retired to the chair by the fire, from which he went on running it until 1991, by remark.

Her bed is twenty crowns, and includes breakfast, and breakfast is turnip. She has never been asked to change this and would not. The turnip on its plinth in the middle of the room, painted gold, with a small plaque to say that it is not a metaphor, was her father's idea, and she has kept it gilded, every spring, in the same paint, and she says that it is the only thing in Thistlewick that has never once been chosen for anything.

## The jar

When the Forty-Eighth came through the Turnip on his way out of the village, properly out, roads and everything, she gave him a jar.

> "There's a town on the south coast. Nether Sopping. Follow the road out of the gate and turn left at the sea. There's a woman there runs a tavern. Dorcas Thrupp. My sister. Give her this. Don't shake it. Don't ask what's in it."

> "Thirty years we've not spoken. That's not a falling-out. We just stopped one day and neither of us started again. So we send each other things instead."

> "Thirty years. Tell her I said nothing. She'll know what I mean by it."

He brought the reply north, and she opened it in front of him, and looked at it for some time, and said, "...The cow," and laughed, which the regulars of the Turnip say they had not heard her do, properly, from the bottom, in thirty years.

Then she went upstairs and came down with the locket, which had been in her drawer since they were girls: two portraits, one of each, facing resolutely away. "It's hers and mine and it's been in a drawer since we were girls and I'm sick of the sight of it. That was HER idea, that was. I'll write to her. Don't tell her that. I'll write to her at the weekend."

<div class="marginalia">The locket was made at the Michaelmas fair in 1962, when Dorcas was fourteen and Prudence eleven, by a man who did little portraits on ivory while you waited. Dorcas says that the facing away was Prudence's idea. Prudence says it was Dorcas's. The man who made it, if he could be found, would be able to settle it, and neither of them has looked for him, which I take to mean that both of them know. - H.B.</div>

## Thistlewick

She has never once remarked on anything that Thistlewick does not remark on, which is how she has kept a public house there for twenty-six years; she knows, for example, that the Regular's cousin was the Thirty-Ninth, and she has served the Regular every night for as long as she has kept the house, and she has never done any arithmetic about it, and would thank you not to do any in her bar. Nobody in Thistlewick mentioned to the Forty-Eighth that she had a sister. She would like it known that this is because nobody asked.

## Afterwards

She wrote to her sister at the weekend, as she had said she would: four pages, two of them about the Forty-Eighth, and one of those complimentary. She has written every month since. The letters go south by the carrier on the last Friday of the month, and Dorcas's come north on the first Thursday, so that each of them has the other's letter for a week before she has to answer it, which Prudence says is the right amount of time, and that she does not know how they managed before. They have not met. Prudence says that Dorcas will have to come to her, being the elder, and having moved. She has written to say so. She has had a reply.
