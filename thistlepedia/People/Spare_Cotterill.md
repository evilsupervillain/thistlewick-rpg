<p class="lede"><b>Spare Cotterill</b> is the ninth of the ten Cotterills of <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>, and was, at nine, the youngest member of the Forty-Eighth's company, and the last to join. He had had a bag packed for two years. He had asked every Chosen One who ever walked up his street whether he could come. All of them said no, until the Forty-Eighth, who said no as well, and then listened to why he should be asked again.</p>

"Are you the Chosen One? I have a bag. It is packed. It has been packed for two years. I am nine. I am nine in a useful way."

## Nine in a useful way

The boiler of the Hoyle Works has ninety tubes in it, and they have to be brushed, and a man does not fit. Spare fits. He has been going into the boiler since he was six, and Miss <a class="new" href="Ott_Hoyle">Hoyle</a> pays him in buns. He has, he says, never once been frightened.

Then he said the rest of it, which I print because he has given me leave to and because it is the truest thing anybody told the Forty-Eighth on the whole of the road:

"I have been frightened. Not of the dark. Of coming out and nobody being there. There is always somebody there."

He was also, that winter, about to stop being the littlest. In the spring there would be ten Cotterills. He was not sad about it. "I have had nine years of being the littlest and it is a very good job. I would just like to have done one thing first."

## Joining

It took a great deal. The party had to hear him out, all of it, and meet every Cotterill there is, on the street, in the works, at the forge, in the Safety Valve and at the Parish Rooms, because you do not take a boy out of a family you have not met. It needed the airship, because a nine-year-old does not walk to the Obligatory Tower through the Gloamwood. It needed a form, and the parish had one: Form P-9, which his sister Gudgeon, who clerks at the Parish Rooms, had never once issued and knew exactly where to find. It needed a parent's signature.

It needed, in the end, a discussion with his mother, which went the way discussions go in this part of the world, and he lost it at least once, alone. He was not upset. She conceded. Her terms were these, and he has kept every one:

> Jumper on. You do not go in first. You write. It does not have to be long, and it does not have to be true.

Shim, who is eleven and has a tin, gave him a washer. He wears it.

## On the Quest

He does not leave at the door. That is Mr Fairweather's job.

**Letters home.** He wrote from every town, four lines each, and handed them to the same courier, who was always already there and never explained. His mother read them out at the dinner hour at the works, and the whole shift knows about the dragon. There was no dragon. He kept a copy of every one, in pencil, in case. I have been shown the copies, and with his permission I print the first:

<div class="verse">
<p>Dear Mam, I am at the seaside. The sea is right there.<br>
I have checked. It goes out and then it comes back.<br>
I have my jumper on and I did not go in first. Nobody<br>
has hit me. There is no dragon. Love, Spare.</p>
<span class="attribution">S. Cotterill, from Nether Sopping</span>
</div>

His mother did more potatoes anyway.

**The Bottom Lock.** His eldest brother Cotter, at the Iron Company's gate in <a class="new" href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a>, had a job no man would fit: the bottom gate of the Dunnage cut, jammed, and a paddle to clear in a brick culvert under it. Spare went in alone, in the dark, and counted, and cleared it, and came out. Somebody was there when he came out.

**The pen pal.** Since he was six he had been writing to Effie Gotobed of <a class="new" href="../Places/Much_Pottering">Much Pottering</a>, through the same courier, and she had had every untrue thing he ever wrote, and what she wrote back was exactly as untrue. They met, at last. Her volcano is a bonfire. Her sea serpent is a carp. There was no volcano. They are still writing.

## The tenth

After the Quest, he asked the party every morning, on waking, whether it was spring yet. It was not. Then one morning it was, and Bessie Marsden came for him, and the street was empty for a day, and **Washer** Cotterill was born at six in the morning, on the hooter. His brother Clevis had been running a book on it, and it paid twenty to one.

Spare is not the littlest any more. He is a big brother. He came back to the party for a while afterwards, and was, I am told, very good at it.

## Afterwards

He is at home for Washer, and still writing to Effie. He also writes to me. Since the Quest I have had thirty-one letters from him, every one of them four lines long, and every one of them untrue, and I have filed every one of them under Fiction, where they are the best-kept part of my files. He has given me leave to print one:

<div class="verse">
<p>Dear Mr Bellwether, I have been made Mayor of Upper Clanging.<br>
It was a close vote. The duck was against.<br>
I have my jumper on and I did not go in first. Washer<br>
has said a word. It was spring. Love, Spare.</p>
<span class="attribution">S. Cotterill, from Upper Clanging (filed under Fiction)</span>
</div>

It was also Spare who confirmed for me, by letter, that the twenty-first of March 2002 is the first day of spring, which is the date on which I have signed this volume. It is the only true thing he has ever written to me. I checked.

<div class="marginalia">Washer has not said a word. I have checked that too. I am told he says a great many things, and none of them is a word yet, and that his brother is working on it.</div>
