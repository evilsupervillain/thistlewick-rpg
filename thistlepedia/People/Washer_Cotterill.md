<p class="lede"><b>Washer Cotterill</b> is the tenth and youngest of the Cotterills of <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>, born at six in the morning on the first day of spring in the year 2000, on the hooter, the only one of them, Mrs Cotterill says, who was ever on time. Mr Cotterill had said not Washer. Spare got to it first.</p>

## Before

The tenth was expected in the spring. Every Cotterill in the town knew it, and so did the Forty-Eighth, the moment he came up the street, because Mrs Cotterill told him at the door, "Nine. And one in the oven," and then, "Ten in the spring. Mr Cotterill is very pleased." The family had given all the small clothes away and Mrs Cotterill had to go round the town and ask for them back. Ferrule was in charge of her not lifting anything. Winnie Marsden had been to the seating plan of the Class of 'Nineteen and found that it had no column for it. Clevis had opened a book on the name, with Gib at evens and Washer at twenty to one, because Da had said not Washer. Shim had said that Spare could have a washer if he went away before the baby came, and Spare had gone away, with the washer, to the Obligatory Tower.

So Washer was talked about, priced, provided for, argued over and twice named before being born, and she was, at the time, the most discussed person in the north who had not yet done anything.

## Spring

The Forty-Eighth's company came down from the Tower, and after the ending Spare went down the Stair under Hithermost, and on the Stair, in the lamplight, a man in a red coat asked whether there was any post. Spare wrote his ninth letter home on his knee, and it said *Dear Mam. Is it spring yet? Spare.*, and it was the first one he had ever sent that was entirely true.

After that he asked every morning. *Is it spring yet?* He was told not yet, and took it extremely well, and was told soon, and asked how soon, and looked at the sky as if it were late with something, and said that he would ask tomorrow, and did. Then one morning the light was a different colour, and the air smelt green, and something was singing that had not been singing the day before, and he was told yes. "Then I have to go home. Mam said ten in the spring. It is the spring." He had the bag on before anybody had finished saying it. It was, as everybody who was with him has told me, the first deadline in the whole of the Quest that anybody could actually have missed, and he did not intend to.

He was home by the evening. Every Cotterill in Upper Clanging was indoors, and the street was empty, which nobody in the town could remember happening before, and every window in the house was lit. Bessie was at the Cotterills' door, not her own, and saw him, and opened it, and held it. "I have to go in now," said Spare to the company. "Come back in the morning. Not before. Mam said." He went in with the bag, and the door shut.

Mr Cotterill came off nights for it, for the first time in twenty-two years, and he and Rivet passed at the door at six the next morning, the both of them going in. At six o'clock exactly the works hooter went for the day shift, and Washer was born on it.

Bessie told it at the door to anybody who came by: "Washer. Six o'clock this morning, on the hooter. Mrs Cotterill says it is the only one of them that was ever on time." Gudgeon entered it in the register at a quarter to seven, in ink, and had to rule a new column, because the old one was full. Clevis paid out most of Upper Clanging. Cotter came home on the Saturday, for good. And Spare came out onto the step, and told the company, as if they could not possibly have heard:

> "It is a baby. It is very small. It got hold of my finger and it would not give it back. I am not the youngest any more. I am a big brother."

He said it, I am told, the way other people say they have been knighted.

## The name

Mrs Cotterill had always had Washer in reserve. Mr Cotterill had said not Washer. She has given me the whole of what happened, and it is short:

> "Ten. Washer. Spare got to it first. He said call it Washer, and I was tired."

Mr Cotterill's account is shorter. "I said not Washer." He says it now with no hope at all, and great contentment, and has added, since: "Washer. I have stopped saying not."

Asked why Washer, Spare told me, in a letter that I have not filed under Fiction, that a washer is the thing that goes under the nut, so that the nut can hold, and that you do not notice it, and that without it the whole thing works loose. He was nine. The letter is four lines long, and three of them are about something else.

<div class="marginalia">The washer that Shim gave Spare, and that went to the top of the Tower on his bootlace, was taken out of her tin the winter before Washer was born and has never been back in it. There is a second one in the tin now, in a twist of paper, labelled WASHER'S, for when she is bigger. I do not think anybody in the family has noticed that the tenth Cotterill has been given a washer twice over, once by name and once by Shim, and I have not pointed it out, because in that house, if you point a thing out, somebody opens a book on it. - H.B.</div>

## Now

Washer is two today, the twenty-first of March 2002, the first day of spring, which Spare has confirmed by letter, and which is why I have dated this volume as I have. She is walking, and has not said a word yet, and looks, when Grommet starts on the names at breakfast, at Grommet, with what Grommet calls an expression. Spare is working on the first word. The whole street has money on it. Clevis is not taking bets. Clevis says the family has been through enough.

She is a girl, which is the one thing about her that nobody in Upper Clanging had a book on, because Mrs Cotterill let it be known in the autumn of 1999 that it was none of the town's business, and in that town, when Mrs Cotterill says a thing is none of its business, the town goes and finds some other business. It makes five and five. Ferrule has noted it on the slate in the kitchen, under BALANCE, and nobody is allowed to rub it out.

The peg at the end of the line of vests in the kitchen window, which was put up in hope, has a vest on it now, the smallest. Round the rim of the rain barrel are ten tin cups on ten nails, and the tenth is Washer's, and is the only one with a handle on both sides.
