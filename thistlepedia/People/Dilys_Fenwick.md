<p class="lede"><b>Dilys Fenwick</b> keeps Station Three, Quests, at <a href="../Places/Firstfield_Meadow">Firstfield Meadow</a>, where her three sheep are lost. They are not lost. They are just there, about where you would leave a sheep. They are also not sheep. The Guild ordered sheep and sent pigs, and the form says sheep, so they are sheep, and they have been practising their baa, and would like that noted.</p>

## Station Three

She came over the hill in 1993, at twenty-two, to take the post at Station Three, which had been empty since the last keeper's sheep had died of old age and the Guild had ordered more. The Guild sent pigs. There was a form. The form said sheep. Dilys looked at the pigs, and at the form, and said that if the form said sheep they were sheep, and that she would not hear a word against them, and the pigs have been sheep since. Their names are Daisy, Primrose and Mrs Hogg. Mrs Hogg leans on her.

> "The Guild says a meadow has to have a fetch quest. I said my sheep aren't lost. They said, well, lose them a bit. So they're lost. A bit. They're very good about it. They've been practising their baa."

If you decline to find them, she says *But thou must*, and then she says sorry, the Guild gave her that line. The Forty-Eighth declined, once, to see what would happen, and was told *But thou must*, and then sorry, and then he found them. They were just there.

## The reward

> "This is for you. It's the reward. It's out of my own cupboard, so don't tell the Guild it's not a proper one."

It was a potion, from the shelf in her kitchen, and it was a proper one. She says that the Guild sends her rewards, from time to time, in a crate, and that they are generally not as good as what she has in the cupboard, and that a hero who has found three sheep that were not lost deserves the cupboard.

## Tam

She married Tam Fenwick, a carter from Firstfield, in the meadow, in 1995, on a fine day, with the whole Guild there, and the sheep. Fizz explained the vows to them first. Twice. In capitals.

> "Tam cried at the second one. He says it was the capitals. He cries at the harvest supper as well, bless him."

<div class="marginalia">I have met Tam Fenwick, at the cottage by the gate, and he is a large, quiet man with a cart and a soft voice, and he cried, slightly, while telling me about the wedding, and apologised, and said it was the capitals, and that even remembering them did it. Mrs Hogg, who had followed me in, leaned on him. He said that she does that. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

She is thirty-one, and still at Station Three. The sheep are still lost, a bit, and still very good about it. Miss Halloway's Bestiary, which I have printed in this volume, does not list them, and Dilys has written to Miss Halloway to ask that they be entered, as sheep, enclosing the form. Miss Halloway has replied that she has considered the form, and that it is a very good form, and that they are pigs, and that she will enter them in volume two, under *Sus scrofa*, with *sheep (Guild)* as a synonym, which is more than she has done for Dr Rowbotham. Dilys has written back to say that she will not hear a word against them. The correspondence is, I understand, continuing, and both of them are enjoying it very much.
