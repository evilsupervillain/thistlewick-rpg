<p class="lede"><b>Nabb</b> was the cook in the Forty-Sixth's party, in 1980, and was there when he stopped on the path in the Gloamwood and said that the woods were enough, and looked at the others, and said: fair. Nabb has written to him every month since, in the round hand, and drinks at the Slain Wyvern with Tolly, who writes in the narrow one, and gave the Forty-Eighth the shortest of the six tales in my history, which is also the only one with nothing missing from it.</p>

## The whole story

> "He stopped. Just - stopped, on the path, and said the woods were enough. And we looked at each other, and we said: fair."

> "That's the whole story. Everyone wants there to be more. There isn't more. We were tired, and he said the woods were enough, and he was right, and we went home."

I have heard Nabb tell it eleven times, to eleven people, and it has never been a word longer, and it has never been a word shorter. In a tavern full of men who have improved their stories every year for forty years, this is a kind of genius, and I do not think anybody in the Wyvern but me has noticed it.

## The cooking

Nabb was taken on by the Forty-Sixth's party as the cook, in the spring of 1980, having cooked at the harbour eating house since leaving school and wanting, Nabb says, to see whether food tasted any different further north. Nabb never found out. The party got as far as the Gloamwood, and stopped, and came home, and Nabb went back to the eating house on the Monday and has been there since. The Wyvern says that the eating house does the best fish on the coast, and that it is because Nabb has had a great deal of time to think about it.

## The letters

> "We write. Every month, the both of us. He's never once written back. He reads them, though. The man who collects the post says so."

> "Post goes out Thursdays. Tolly does the stamps. It's something to do."

That is the whole of what Nabb says about the letters, and it is meant to sound like nothing. Tolly says that Nabb has thought for eleven years that Tolly does not know the letters go unanswered, and that she has never let on. The Forty-Sixth says that Nabb doesn't know that reading them is the answer, and that Tolly does. I have the three accounts. I am not going to try to reconcile them, because they do not need reconciling; they are three people being kind to each other at forty miles' distance and twenty-two years, and the post goes out on Thursdays.

<div class="marginalia">I asked Nabb what goes into a letter every month for twenty-two years to a man who does not write back. Nabb said, the weather, and what was on at the eating house, and who had come into the Wyvern, and how the fish were. I said that seemed a great deal to tell somebody who never answered. Nabb looked at me for some time and then said that it was not a great deal at all, if you thought about it, and that I should think about it, and went to get the drinks. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

Nabb still writes every month. The month the news came south that the Forty-Eighth had done what forty-seven had not, Nabb's letter ran to four sides, which is three more than usual, and Tolly says it was mostly about the fish, and that it was the best letter Nabb had ever written.
