# The Rivals of the Sable Hart

<p class="hatnote">For the inn, the rota and the league table, see <a href="../Places/The_Sable_Hart">The Sable Hart</a>. For the member who sent apologies to a hundred and twelve meetings, beautifully, see <a href="Corvin_Ash">Corvin Ash</a>. They are, all of them, extremely nice.</p>


<p class="lede">The <b>rivals of the Sable Hart</b> are the five doomed rivals who brood at the inn of that name in the west: four in the corners, by rota, and the young one by the fire. They tip beautifully and they put the chairs back. They keep a league table, a newsletter, a waiting list and a rota, and every one of them is the rival of somebody very much greater who does not, as a rule, know. I have given each of them a short entry below, because they would not hear of separate articles, on the grounds that it would be unfair to whoever came out shortest.</p>

## Hollin Crake, Secretary

> "Good evening. Hollin Crake, Secretary. Are you anybody's rival at all?"

He is the only man in the history of brooding who stands up when you come in. He has the Secretary's corner, by the window, because somebody has to be able to read the rota, and his tankard sits on a coaster he brought from home, so as not to mark the table. He was born in 1931 and has been a member since 1958, and Secretary since 1972, and he devised the rota in 1985, when a fifth member was elected and there were, as he says, four corners and five of us. You can see the difficulty. The darkest corner changes hands on a Monday. NOBODY broods in it out of turn, however rainy.

He would not tell me whose rival he was. He said that it would be unkind to the other party, who was a very great man, and who had been dead since 1971, and had never known, and that a rivalry was a private matter between a man and his own shortcomings. He said that the other party's widow sends him a card at Christmas, and that he has never told her either.

## Silas Veyle

> "...Leave me. The shadows are mine. Until Monday. Sorry. That came out very possessive. They're mine until Monday, and then they're Rook's, and he's earned them."

He has the darkest corner, in rotation, and the scar, and the most points on the table, forty-one, mostly for the scar. Don't ask about the scar. Nobody asks about the scar, and he looks, very briefly, disappointed. The curtain behind him is drawn, and there is no window behind it, and he says it makes a real difference. His pint is untouched; it is for atmosphere. He orders a fresh one at nine and leaves that too.

<div class="marginalia">I asked about the scar. He was so delighted to be asked that he could not, for some minutes, speak, and then he said, with great feeling, "Don't ask about the scar," and bought me a drink. Mrs Garrow told me afterwards that he got it in 1988, on a garden rake, in her mother's time, and that it is the best thing that ever happened to him, and that I was not to say she said so. - H.B.</div>

## Thaddeus Rook

> "Thaddeus Rook. I keep the table. Points for a vow, a scar, a dramatic exit, and standing about in the rain."

He keeps the league table, ruled in red, beside the rota. Standing in the rain is two points. Standing in the rain on purpose is one. They are quite strict. A dramatic exit through a door that turns out to be a cupboard is minus two. He was fourth in 2000, "because I never give myself the benefit of the doubt. That's the whole point of keeping the table." He is the rival of a very great archer on the east coast, who was ninety-one in 2000, and he is giving him every chance. When Corvin Ash was reviewed for points in 2000 and was found to have sat in one corner of the Slain Wyvern for eleven years, Mr Rook was asked whether there was a category. "There is now." He then moved himself down to fifth, by his own request, and was visibly happier there.

## Lucan Morrow

> "Lucan Morrow. I edit the newsletter. There's one on the table. It's free. It's always been free."

The newsletter is *Overshadowed*, and it is free, and he has edited every issue since the first, in 1990. He is the rival of a Mr Pennock, who is a very great wizard, and who does not know. Morrow writes to him at Christmas. "Every year. A lovely card. He thinks I'm his cousin." Morrow has never corrected him, and says that it is the warmest correspondence of his life, and that if Mr Pennock ever finds out he will have to give up either the rivalry or the cousin, and that he does not know which, and would rather not be made to choose.

## Kit Blackwood

> "Could I have that category, one day? When I've got a corner?"

He is new, and first on the waiting list for a corner, any corner, and has been since March of 2000. Until he gets one he broods on a stool by the fire, in the warmest and brightest seat in the inn, as hard as he possibly can. It is quite hard to brood by a fire. He is getting better. Mr Crake says he has a gift for a long silence. He is nobody's rival yet. There is a list for that, too. In the winter of 2001 he made his first dramatic exit, the door having been planed at last, and it was a cupboard, minus two, and he took it extremely well, plus one, a new category.

## Mrs Garrow

Mrs Garrow keeps the house, and was born in 1962, and has kept it since 1985, and says they are no trouble, love: four in the corners and the young one by the fire, every night of the year, and the middle of the room empty as a church. She buys the bottles with the gloomy labels because her regulars prefer them, and what is inside is elderflower cordial. The tankards each have a small knitted hood over them, to keep the dust off. She knitted them. She says that she has never once in seventeen years been asked to, and that they are her regulars, and they ought to look the part.

## Afterwards

Corvin Ash attends now. Not every week. He sends apologies when he does not. "We find it very reassuring." He is second on the table, behind the scar, with thirty-nine points in a category of his own, and his stool at the middle table is still kept, and still dusted. Kit Blackwood is still first on the waiting list, and has been offered the Secretary's corner, temporarily, by Mr Crake, on a Wednesday, and has declined it, on the grounds that it would not be fair to the rota, which Mr Crake says is the most promising thing he has heard from a young member in forty years.
