<p class="lede"><b>Bram Thistle</b> is a turnip farmer of <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>, and was the forty-eighth and last of the <a href="../History/The_Chosen_Ones">Chosen Ones</a>. He was asleep when he was chosen, and so did not object, which the Prophecy Committee took as consent. He went north, as forty-seven had, and beat the <a href="Grimspite_the_Inevitable">Dark Lord</a>, as forty-seven had, and then did the one thing none of them had done, which was to ask the Dark Lord a question and stay for the whole of the answer.</p>

He would like it known that he did none of it on his own. I have known him for two years and that is the only correction he has ever made to anything I have written about him, and he makes it every time.

## Early life

The Thistles have farmed the same ground outside Thistlewick for as long as anybody has written anything down, which in Thistlewick is not long. His great-grandmother, Mabel Thistle, grew a turnip as big as a chair, and the family still speaks of it in the tone other families keep for a war. He lives with his mother, who keeps the house, irons his good shirt on a Tuesday, and on the morning of the Quest told him there were eleven people outside and one of them had a scroll.

I record, because one version of events in circulation says otherwise, that <a href="Mrs_Thistle">his mother</a> is alive, well, and has read that version. She asked me to put in that she makes a good pie. She does.

There was a sword on the wall above the grate. It had been there, his mother said, a hundred years, waiting for exactly this, "and frankly it has been unbearable about it." I have tried to date the sword and cannot. A hundred years is what Thistlewick says about anything that has been anywhere for a while, and in Thistlewick that is not a measurement (see <a href="../History/Two_Reckonings">Two Reckonings</a>). It is back on the wall now, and is, I am told, quieter.

## The choosing

The minutes of the meeting give his name under *apologies for absence*, with one goat. He was not consulted, because he was asleep, and he was not woken, because the Committee did not think it would make a difference, and it did not. Elder <a href="Elder_Wispel">Wispel</a> gave him the charge in <a href="../Places/Prophecy_Hall">Prophecy Hall</a>, the parish gave him five hundred crowns and three potions, and a neighbour in the lane asked him who was going to do the west field.

His own account of that morning, which I have had from him several times, is that he did not know what else to do, so he went. I have put this to forty-seven people who did the same and they all recognised it at once.

## The Quest

The whole of it is under [The Obligatory Quest](../History/The_Obligatory_Quest), and the longer version is Volume Two. What distinguishes him from the forty-seven is not what he did - they all went north and did the sword - but the things he did that were not on the way:

- **He went south.** Nobody does. He came into the <a href="../Places/The_Slain_Wyvern">Slain Wyvern</a>, sat down with a room full of people the Committee would rather he had not met, and listened to six of them to the end. I paid him for it, and he found out the wyvern is a goose.
- **He asked.** <a href="Halbert_Quy">Halbert Quy</a>, the Forty-Fifth, told him what to ask at the top, and he asked it.
- **He listened to the answer.** All of it. Grimspite had been waiting four thousand eight hundred contract years for somebody to.
- **He read the document.** Out loud, in full, in the middle of a fight, because somebody he had just beaten told him to. It is the only recorded case of a Chosen One reading the <a href="../History/The_Prophecy_of_Thistlewick">Prophecy</a>, and it was fatal to the Prophecy.

He also, on the way, helped fly a ship, light a lighthouse, build a bench, stop a wreck, examine a goose, register with a Guild, name a beetle (it is not a beetle; I have been corrected), find a rhyme that does not exist, and walk into, by the account's own count, very nearly every cliche there is. He was certified fit to travel at about the point at which he had finished travelling.

<div class="marginalia">The account in Prophecy Hall also records how many turnips he ate on the road. I have the figure. He has asked me not to print it, on the grounds that it is nobody's business, and I agree.</div>

## The question of companions

The clause allowed him three at a time and he had eleven, in one combination or another, and so whatever you have heard about who was at the top of the Tower with him, somebody else has heard something different, and both of you heard it from the person concerned. Bram's own answer, when I asked him, was "all of them", which is not possible under the clause, and which he says is true anyway. The articles on each of them are listed below, and each says what they told me.

## Character

He is not clever, and does not claim to be, and is the only person I have met who has had the whole Prophecy explained to him and understood it. He is polite to everybody, including the people who were about to kill him. He has a quantity of confidence that has never once been audited, and has never needed to be. He is, it must be said, very lucky; Miss <a href="Wren_Halloway">Halloway</a> has described his luck in print as "statistically indefensible", and he took it as a compliment, after some thought, because it was accurate.

He does not like being written about. He comes south anyway, about once a month, to correct me.

## Afterwards

The next morning there were still turnips. There was also a letter on the mat: clause nineteen, one quarter's pension, and the key to <a href="Ambrose_Fitch">Ambrose Fitch</a>'s old cottage at <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a>, with the Committee's trust that Friday would suit. Friday did not suit. He stayed at home and farmed, and six months later the Committee struck clause nineteen, after a meeting of eleven hours.

He kept the key. He uses the cottage when he comes south, which is about monthly, and every time he is in it he waters the pot on the sill, which has had something dead in it since Mr Fitch's day. Nothing has grown. I check.

The Committee struck clause twelve as well, and wrote in its place SEE BRAM. Nobody has needed to see him yet. The Committee has twice minuted that it might. It has also offered him a seat, which he declined, because it meets in planting time.

His harvest of 2001 was the best in the parish. The Committee asked whether it had been prophesied. It had not.

Once a year, on the day, he walks up to the top of the <a href="../Places/The_Obligatory_Tower">Obligatory Tower</a>. The kettle is on.

In Prophecy Hall, at the end of the Wall of the Forty-Seven, the forty-eighth frame is still empty, and the card in it still says PORTRAIT TO FOLLOW. SITTER AT THE COAST. He is not at the coast. He is two fields away. Nobody has changed the card, and he has asked me not to tell them.
