<p class="lede"><b>Merribell</b> is the healer of <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>, a Sister of the <a href="../Institutions/The_Order_of_Whatever_Works">Order of Whatever Works</a>, and the first person to join the Forty-Eighth, on the grounds that he had not had a glass of water since Tuesday. Her order holds that theology is a distraction from the actual problem, which is usually that you have not had any water today. She has never lost a patient.</p>

"Sit down. When did you last drink water?" is how she meets everybody, and it was how she met <a href="Bram_Thistle">Bram</a>. He gave an answer. "That wasn't the question," she said. "Water. When."

## The Order

The Order of Whatever Works has a founding document, which is a pamphlet, and a Rule of Life four lines long, one of them about socks. It does not have a doctrine and does not want one. Merribell trained at the mother house at <a href="../Places/Weeping_Bottom">Weeping Bottom</a>, a wet valley chosen by a founder who believed the damp was good for the chest, and left at twenty-two, in 1996, because the Sisterhouse had begun holding symposia.

She keeps the most complete medical record in the county: every ailment of every soul in Thistlewick, dated, in a good hand. <a href="../Institutions/The_Prophecy_Committee">The Prophecy Committee</a> has never once asked to see it, preferring the Prophecy.

## Manner

She is a professional, and professionals have a vocabulary. She says the anatomical words the way other people say "elbow", plainly and at the volume needed to be heard over a fight, and the party learned early to look at the ceiling. "Where does it hurt? Be specific. 'Down there' is four separate systems and I will guess wrong." When Bram said ankle, she said, "Oh, thank goodness."

She carries a basin. She brought it unasked. "Nobody has ever won an argument with me about hydration and I am not starting a losing streak on a Thursday."

## On the Quest

Of all the things done on the road, three were hers, and the Prophecy's own account records each of them.

**Fit to travel.** Clause eleven of the [Prophecy](../History/The_Prophecy_of_Thistlewick#clause-eleven-fit-to-travel) requires a Chosen One to be certified fit before departure. It had been done forty-seven times. The Forty-Eighth's form was blank, because he had been asleep. She found the form on a pad at <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a> and did it properly: stamped scales from the fish stall at <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a>, the light of the lighthouse at <a href="../Places/The_Lighthouse_of_Saint_Bother">Saint Bother</a>'s, lit, a clean surface, and a witness, from among the company. It is, the account says, the first properly documented attempt in four thousand years. I have interviewed several of the witnesses and they all looked at the ceiling.

**The Sisterhouse.** The mother house had split between the Order of Whatever Works and the Order of What Ought To Work, which wins every argument and loses patients, and the two agreed to settle it the way this world settles things, with a ward and a queue. Merribell asked the patients whether they had eaten. Whatever Works won on points, nineteen to one, in an afternoon. <a href="Sister_Bewick">Sister Bewick</a>, of the other order, has since been examined on Whatever Works' doctrine, and passed. It is four lines long.

**The curse of Sump.** The village of <a href="../Places/Sump">Sump</a> had been cursed since 1794, with headaches, poor sleep and a general sense that things used to be better. She diagnosed it in nine seconds. There were wasps in the well and nobody had had any water for two hundred years. The curse was lifted by the Water Committee, one member, with a glass of water, and the minutes record an item three.

She also called on <a href="Tobias_Frayne">Tobias Frayne</a>, who had been revived by passing adventurers eleven times, and declined to make it twelve. "There is nothing wrong with you, Mr Frayne. That's the trouble." She prescribed having the parish to tea, through the door, on purpose. He has not died since.

## The Healers' Table

Every surviving party medic within forty miles meets once a year in the back room of the <a href="../Places/The_Slain_Wyvern">Slain Wyvern</a>, under the chair of Mrs <a href="Ysolde_Marrow">Ysolde Marrow</a>, to compare the worst thing they ever had to improvise. Merribell was invited in the year of the Quest, the youngest at the Table by thirty-eight years, and asked to speak, and had nothing.

"I've never lost anybody," she told them. "I know what that means. It isn't a virtue. It's an average."

The room did not console her and did not laugh. Mrs Marrow told her to write it down, all of it, now, while she still thinks it is boring. She said, "Yes, Mrs Marrow." I was in the bar, and the door was not quite shut, and I have written down only what she has since said I may.

<div class="marginalia">Merribell has read this article and has one correction, which is that I should drink more water. I have noted it.</div>

## Afterwards

She spoke at the Healers' Table in 2001, as the account said she would. She spoke for eleven minutes, which is the shortest address in the Table's history, and the Table minuted it as the most useful. The subject was water.

She is still Thistlewick's healer, still the only member of her order for eleven miles, and still has not lost anybody. She is writing it down.
