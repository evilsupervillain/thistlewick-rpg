## Statement of service

1. **Name.** Prosper Hollowell. Caretaker. Retained.

2. **Date of birth.** I have been asked this by Mr Bellwether, and I have looked, and it is not on file. The file was opened when I was retained, and I was already here, and I have never thought it my place to go back and enter myself in a record I keep for somebody else. I am told that a great many people in this volume have had their dates filled in. I should not like to cause any trouble over mine. Please put *pending*.

3. **Previous employment.** Before the removal I was under-steward of the Premises, in the engagement's old years, in charge of the cushions on the visitors' sofa. There were never any visitors. I plumped them every morning. I was told by the steward that this was the most important post in the building, because if a visitor ever did come, the first thing they would do was sit down. I have never forgotten it.

4. **Retention.** When the engagement was moved into the Tower, with a door at the bottom, ground floor, very sensible, the steward retired, and I was retained to keep the Premises ready, in case they were re-let. I have kept them ready. Four thousand eight hundred years. Everything is in perfect working order.

5. **Duties.** The hall. The throne, polished, with its dust sheet folded on the seat, ready to go back over it. The four suits of armour, one of which faces the door, and which I polish more than the others, and I could not tell you why. The sofa. The deeds. The potted plant, which is watered by the rain, from underneath. The visitors' book, which does not leave the lectern. The lift.

6. **The lift.** Commissioned following a complaint, upheld. Courtyard to battlements. It goes up, smoothly and in perfect silence, and comes back down, smoothly and in perfect silence. It works beautifully. It always has. I have mentioned it in every report, and I should like to mention it here, as it is the best thing in the building, and I have had very little opportunity to say so to anybody who was not reading a report.

7. **Reports.** Quarterly. Nineteen thousand two hundred. Each was acknowledged with the same form letter, THANK YOU FOR YOUR REPORT, WHICH HAS BEEN FILED, and I have found that very steadying. Under ITEM TWO: VISITORS every one of them says NONE, except one.

8. **Visitors.** In the year 2000, one party, by air, from below, in an airship from Upper Clanging which I should very much like to see. They took a slip up the Tower for the current incumbent, and brought it back signed: *Never been. Heard marvellous things about the lift.* I pasted it in, square. That quarter's report says *Yes!*, underlined twice. It is my only underlining. I do not regret it.

9. **Release.** In the spring of that year I received a NOTICE OF TERMINATION: *The Engagement is concluded. The Premises are released from retention. No further action is required.* I filed it under N, and under T, and under C, in case. That took me until about half past nine.

10. **Present position.** I am staying on, pending instructions, unpaid. The Premises are in perfect working order and somebody ought to keep them so. I have written a quarterly report to say so, and had the reply, which is that it has been filed. I find I do not mind in the least.

11. **Remarks.** I should like to thank the Forty-Eighth's party for coming, and for the slip, and the courier for bringing the post, which he has done throughout, on his rounds, without being asked whose. I should also like to say to any reader who is passing by air that the tour starts in the hall, and also ends there, and that the cushions are plumped every morning, in case.

P. HOLLOWELL, Caretaker. The Former Premises, the sky. First quarter, 2002.

<div class="marginalia">I have done what he asked, and entered his date of birth as *pending*. He is, by the engagement's reckoning, a great deal older than anybody else in this volume, and he is, by the look of him in his portrait, about sixty-five, and a very kind sixty-five, and I do not think the two numbers need to be reconciled, any more than the Tower's. The courier brought his statement with a second sheet, which was not for the file: a drawing, in pencil, of what he imagines the airship at Upper Clanging to look like. It has a lift. - H.B.</div>
