<p class="lede"><b>Aveline Stope</b> is the Prioress of the Sisterhouse of the Order of Whatever Works at <a href="../Places/Weeping_Bottom">Weeping Bottom</a>, the Order's mother house. For some years before 2000 she held symposia. An order that holds one symposium, she says, is thinking; an order that holds eleven has stopped. She said it the way a physician names a symptom she has seen before, in other people. The eleventh was never held.</p>

## The chair

She was born in 1944 at Brede Saltings, two valleys east of Weeping Bottom, where it is dry, which she has never let the Sisters forget, and came into the Order at twenty, in the drizzle, and took to it. She was a good nurse, and a better administrator, and was made Prioress in 1983, at thirty-nine, the youngest the Order had had.

She found, as Prioress, that the Order had no doctrine, and that this worried the bishops, and the physicians, and some of her own Sisters, and so in 1990 she held a symposium on the question, in the chapel, with the chairs in two rows facing each other, and she chaired it very well. It was referred to the next symposium. She held ten.

> "I stayed for the symposia. Eleven of them. I chair them very well. The eleventh is at three. *Whether Anything Works.* It will be referred to the twelfth."

## The line

By the year 2000 a party of the Sisters under <a href="Sister_Bewick">Sister Bewick</a> had found a doctrine, and called themselves What Ought To Work, and dyed the trim of their habits black, and the ward was divided down the middle by a line, with the Prioress's half on the west and Sister Bewick's on the east. The patients crossed it in the night, because there was soup.

Merribell, who had left the mother house in 1996, at twenty-two, because it had begun holding symposia, came back on the day of the eleventh.

> "Sister. We'll talk. It's today, now, and I know it is. You left at twenty-two. You said we'd started holding symposia, and you'd come to look after people."

Merribell's proposal was: don't hold the symposium; open the door; twenty patients to each half, and whoever is through first is right. The Prioress laughed at it, once, which the Sisters say they had not heard her do since about 1990. Then she agreed. Whatever Works was through its twenty while What Ought To Work was on patient one. Nineteen to one, on points.

<div class="marginalia">The eleventh minute, in her hand, reads: <i>Not held. A queue instead. Whatever Works, on points. Soup.</i> I asked her whether she minded that her last symposium was never held. She said that it was the only one that had ever reached a conclusion, and that she was very proud of it, and that she had chaired it from the soup pot. - H.B.</div>

## The pamphlet

When Sister Bewick asked to be examined on the Order's doctrine, since that is how a doctrine is settled, the Prioress took Mother Ursula's own pamphlet out of a drawer, where it had been since the first symposium. Four pages, stitched, damp at the corners, with the Rule of Life on the first page. I asked her why she had put it away in 1990. She said that she had put it away because she was going to hold a symposium, and you cannot hold a symposium about a thing that is four lines long and lying on the table, and that she should have noticed that at the time, and that she had, and had held the symposium anyway, and that that was the whole of the trouble.

## Afterwards

She is fifty-eight. The Sisterhouse holds no symposia. The chairs in the chapel have been put back in rows facing the altar. Merribell has been back twice since, to see her, and they talk. The pamphlet is back in its drawer, but the drawer is left open now, so that anybody who wants the doctrine can see it, and the Prioress says that in two years nobody has asked to, and that everybody has had some soup.
