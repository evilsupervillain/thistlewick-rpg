<p class="lede"><b>Ferrule Cotterill</b> is the fifth of the ten Cotterills of <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>, and is in charge from six, when Da goes to work, and then nobody gets to sit down. Ferrule introduced the whole position to the Forty-Eighth in three sentences, in the voice of a foreman reading a notice: "I am Ferrule. I am sixteen. I am in charge from six."</p>

## From six

The arrangement began in 1996, when Ferrule was twelve, on an evening when <a href="Ernest_Cotterill">Mr Cotterill</a> had gone down to the foundry and <a href="Nora_Cotterill">Mrs Cotterill</a> had gone up to the Parish Rooms about a christening, and <a href="Cotter_Cotterill">Cotter</a> was in <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a>, and <a href="Rivet_Cotterill">Rivet</a> was asleep, and <a href="Gudgeon_Cotterill">Gudgeon</a> was at the Rooms with Mam, and <a href="Tappet_Cotterill">Tappet</a> was out with the cart, and so the oldest Cotterill awake in the house was Ferrule. When Mrs Cotterill came home at nine the floor had been swept, the younger ones were in bed in order of size, the kettle was on, and Clevis had been made to give back three farthings. Mrs Cotterill looked round the kitchen for some time and then said, "Right, then," and that was the appointment.

It has not been revoked. She has been in charge from six every evening since, for six years, and the house runs, in the evenings, the way a works runs: to time, and standing up. "Da goes to work. Then I am in charge and nobody gets to sit down." I asked what would happen if somebody did sit down. She looked at me for a moment as if I had asked what would happen if the river ran uphill, and then said that nobody did.

## Mam

The difficulty of the position is Mrs Cotterill, who is not in charge after six, and who does not accept it. In the winter of 2000, before <a href="Washer_Cotterill">Washer</a>, she was not to lift anything, by Ferrule's order, and lifted things the moment Ferrule's back was turned. "She lifts things the moment I turn round." Ferrule had taken to walking backwards round the kitchen, to keep her in view, which she said was very unsettling and a sign of a disordered household, and lifted a coal scuttle to make the point.

The family had given all the small clothes away, thinking there would be no more, and Mrs Cotterill had to go round the town and ask for them back. Ferrule went with her, carrying the basket, so that she would not.

After Washer, it was worse. "Mam is to stay sat down for a week," Ferrule announced, at six, to the whole family and the midwife. A week later Ferrule reported the outcome to the Forty-Eighth: "She has been up four times. I have counted." And then, with the air of somebody making a very great concession to circumstances: "Nobody gets to sit down. Except Mam. Mam has to."

<div class="marginalia">Mrs Cotterill disputes the figure. She says she was up eleven times, and that Ferrule only saw four, and that it is not her fault if some people turn round slowly. Ferrule says that the other seven were not counted because they were reported, by <a href="Shim_Cotterill">Shim</a>, after the fact, and that a reported rising is not an observed rising. I have printed both figures. Clevis offered me odds. - H.B.</div>

## Clevis

"Do not encourage Clevis." It is the one standing instruction Ferrule gives every visitor to the house, and the only one she gives twice. She and Clevis are three years apart, and share a landing, and Clevis runs a book on everything that happens in the family, and Ferrule is in charge of everything that happens in the family, and so the two of them are, in a sense, in the same line of work from opposite ends. Clevis has never once taken a bet against her. He says it is not a sporting proposition.

## The dragon

In the year 2000, writing to his pen pal at <a href="../Places/Much_Pottering">Much Pottering</a>, Spare told her that he had a dragon now, and that it was called Ferrule, and that it was quite big. Effie Gotobed told the Forty-Eighth this at her gate, with great satisfaction, and asked him not to tell Spare she had told. Ferrule heard about it in the summer, from Shim, who hears about everything, and was quiet for some time, and then said that it was a very sensible name for a dragon, because somebody would have to be in charge of it, and went back to sweeping.

## Afterwards

Ferrule is eighteen, and still in charge from six. With Cotter married and the eldest four at work, the evening household is now Clevis, Shim, Spare, Washer and Mrs Cotterill, and Ferrule says that is not fewer, it is younger, which is more. She has been offered a place at the Parish Rooms, by Gudgeon, and at the works, by Tappet, and at the Safety Valve, by <a href="Mr_Kell">Mr Kell</a>, who said that he had never seen anybody keep a room standing up like it. She has declined all three, on the grounds that the position at home is not vacant, and nobody else could fill it, and Washer is beginning to walk.
