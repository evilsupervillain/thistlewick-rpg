<p class="lede"><b>Shim Cotterill</b> is the eighth of the ten Cotterills of <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a>, and collects things: washers, mostly, and three bolts, a doorknob, and a bearing, and the bearing is the best one, because you can hold it and it goes round. When Spare said he was going away before the baby came, she said that he could have a washer, and he could, and he did, and he wore it on a bootlace to the top of the <a href="../Places/The_Obligatory_Tower">Obligatory Tower</a> and back.</p>

## The tin

She has kept the collection since she was five, in a toffee tin with a picture of a lighthouse on the lid, under her side of the bed she shares with <a href="Ferrule_Cotterill">Ferrule</a>. It contains, as of March 2002, forty-one washers, three bolts, a doorknob, the bearing, and a second bearing which she has been given since and which she is keeping an open mind about. She says the doorknob was the first thing in the tin, and is the oddest, and is the one she would save in a fire, after the bearing, and after the family, and possibly before <a href="Clevis_Cotterill">Clevis</a>.

She showed the Forty-Eighth the collection on the step, with great seriousness, item by item:

> "I collect things. Washers, mostly. Three bolts, a doorknob, and a bearing."

> "The bearing is the best one. You can hold it and it goes round."

And then, at the end, because she is generous: "You can hold the bearing if you like." He held it. It went round. I have held it since, by invitation. It is a very good bearing, and the thing about it, which Shim understood at five and I did not understand until I had it in my hand, is that it does nothing at all but go round, perfectly, for as long as you like, and that is enough.

## The washer

When the tenth was coming, Spare said he was going away before the baby came, and Shim said that he could have a washer. When the Forty-Eighth came to the house about Form P-9, she asked whether it was for Spare. It was. She opened the tin, and took out one washer, and held it up to the light.

> "That one. It is a good one. It has been flat the whole time."

It went into Spare's bag. It is on a bootlace round his neck, and has been since, and he calls it *A Washer, Off Shim*, and <a href="Nix">Nix</a>, who keeps the provenance of things, has entered it in her ledger as *Given, by Shim, to Spare. A good washer. Not the bearing.* Shim says the last three words were quite right, and that she had thought about the bearing, for a while, and that a person has to keep something.

<div class="marginalia">Grommet tells her every day that a shim is a packing piece, and not a fastener. She says that she knows. A packing piece is what you put in between two things to make them fit, and the whole family is two things that do not quite fit, ten times over, and somebody has to be the packing piece. I am not sure she meant to say anything so wise. I am fairly sure she did. She is thirteen. - H.B.</div>

## Washer

In the spring of 2000, when the tenth came and was called Washer, Shim was delighted, because it was a name off her tin.

> "I am going to give it a washer. When it is bigger. Not yet."

> "Mam said not yet."

> "When it is bigger."

## Afterwards

Washer is two today, and is bigger, and Shim has not yet given the washer, because Mam has said that Washer will put it in her mouth, and Shim says that Mam is right, and that a washer is not for eating, and that she can wait. She has picked it out. It is in the tin, apart from the others, in a twist of paper with WASHER'S on it in <a href="Gudgeon_Cotterill">Gudgeon</a>'s hand. Shim says it is nearly as good as the one she gave Spare. She says it has been flat the whole time as well.
