<p class="lede"><b>Mrs Tunnicliffe</b> has kept the Parish Rooms at <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a> since 1951: births, deaths, and whatever else nobody else will take on. She has the register, the census and the counter, and also the tea, the urn and the hymn board, and the rota says so, and under it, EVERYTHING ELSE - SEE ABOVE. She was born in the same year as the Class of 'Nineteen, in June, and is not invited to the dinner, and has kept the dates of every one of them for fifty years.</p>

## June

She was born Edna Hargreaves on the eleventh of June 1919, which is to say four months before the <a href="../History/The_Cold_Winter">Cold Winter</a>'s children, and she has never been allowed to forget it, and has never once complained of it.

> "Class of 'Nineteen. The Cold Winter lot. Foundry was banked down six weeks that January. No work. No heat in the whole town but what was in the houses. Two hundred and forty of them. All born the same autumn, near enough."

> "They have a dinner every year. I am not invited. I was born in June."

She says it as a matter of record, which it is. She has the record. I asked her whether she minded. She said that she had been at school with every one of them, in the same two rooms, and had sat at the end of the alphabet, being an H among the Class's A to Zs, at a desk of her own by the stove, and that she had been the only one of them warm all that first winter, and that she had always thought that was fair.

## The Rooms

She went to the Parish Rooms as the clerk's girl in 1934, at fifteen, and became the clerk in 1951. She married Walter Tunnicliffe, the verger, in 1942, and he did the hymn board until he died, in 1988, and then she did it. The three urns are hers: one for a funeral, one for a wedding and one for the fete, the three sizes of parish emergency, and every one of them is on a Saturday.

> "Parish Rooms. Births, deaths, and whatever else nobody else will take on. I have the register, the census, and the counter."

Asked what else there was, she said: "What there is, is behind me." It was. It was everything.

<div class="marginalia">The register lies open at OCCUPATION, and every line for eighty years reads AT THE WORKS, in one hand or another, and in fifty years of the hand being hers she has never once written anything else, because nobody in Upper Clanging has ever been anything else, except the verger, who was AT THE WORKS (VERGER), which she says was a stretch, and which the vicar let go. - H.B.</div>

## Ten clerks, and an eleventh

She has had, she says, eleven clerks in the Rooms since 1951. Ten of them went to the works, which is where everybody goes. The eleventh was Gudgeon Cotterill, in 1995, at fifteen, to help with the census, and Mrs Tunnicliffe says that she knew on the first afternoon, from the way the girl ruled a line, that this was the best hand she had ever had, and has said so since to anybody who would listen, including Gudgeon, who says that she has heard it, and that it does not get any less nice.

In 2001, at eighty-one, Mrs Tunnicliffe went to the parish council and asked to be made the clerk's assistant, so that Gudgeon could be the clerk, and so that she herself could do the urns, which she said was all she had ever really wanted to do, and the only part of the job nobody had ever let her do properly, because there was always the register. The council agreed. Gudgeon is the clerk. Mrs Tunnicliffe does the urns.

## Afterwards

She is eighty-two. She is at the Rooms every morning at eight, before Gudgeon, to fill the urns, and she says that she has made, since 2001, the best tea of her life, and that it was the register all along that was spoiling it. She has still not been invited to the dinner. <a href="Winnie_Marsden">Winnie Marsden</a> has, this winter, asked her whether she would like to come and help with the urns on the second Saturday. Mrs Tunnicliffe said that she would be delighted. There is no seat for her on the plan. She says that she would not want one. She will be at the urn, and at the urn, she says, she has always been invited everywhere.
