<p class="lede"><b>King Aldous</b> is the King of <a href="../Places/Fairholme">Fairholme</a>, a small kingdom in the east whose princess has been kidnapped eleven times, twelve if you count the eagle, which the Palace does not. Every time, he has posted the traditional reward, her hand or half the kingdom, and every rescuer has taken the half. Very sensible. Nobody's fault. But there is only so much kingdom, and he is down, by Osric's arithmetic, to a two-thousand-and-forty-eighth, and it is mostly a ditch.</p>

## The kingdom

He was born in 1932, in the palace, when Fairholme ran from the hills to the marshes and had two rivers, a forest, nine villages and a very good cheese. He came to the throne in 1961, on his father's death, and was by every account a careful and well-liked young king, who kept the roads mended and the cheese up to standard, and was mentioned in two neighbouring chronicles as *a sound man*, which in that part of the world is the highest thing that can be said of a monarch.

He married Isobel of the Marches in 1975, late, and very happily, and their daughter Rosamund was born in 1978. The Queen died in 1983, of a fever, and the King did not marry again. He says that one is enough, if it is the right one, and he says it about the Queen, and about Rosamund, and on one occasion, absent-mindedly, about the cheese.

## The reward

Rosamund was first kidnapped in 1986, at eight, by a sorcerer, from her tower room. The King did what his father had told him a king must do, and what the old books say, and what everybody expected: he posted the traditional reward. Her hand, or half the kingdom.

> "Every time she's taken, I post the traditional reward. Her hand, or half the kingdom. They all take the half. Every one. Very sensible. Nobody's fault. But there's only so much kingdom."

He has posted it eleven times. Every rescuer has taken the half, and he has never once been offended by it, because he says it would have been very awkward for everybody otherwise, and that Rosamund has views. The kingdom is now governed by a council of its shareholders, who are the rescuers, round three tables pushed end to end, and its chief business is drainage. The King sits in the middle. He has one vote. He has used it, in my hearing, to second a motion to lengthen the table, and he did it with great dignity.

> "If you do rescue her, take the half. Everybody does. We've a form."

<div class="marginalia">His portrait on the wall of the chamber shows him in his coronation year, holding the kingdom, on a map, in both hands. The map has been cut into eleven pieces since. Nobody has told the portrait. I asked the King whether he would like it brought up to date. He said that he would not, that it was a very good likeness of a young man who had not yet been asked to do any arithmetic, and that he was fond of him. - H.B.</div>

## Mr Pilbeam

His one sorrow is <a href="Mr_Pilbeam">Mr Pilbeam</a>, who was only passing, and rescued the Princess from an eagle in 1996, and has been sitting at the foot of the council table ever since, waiting for the council to decide whether the eagle counts. The Palace does not count the eagle. The King would like it to. He has said so, in council, eight times, and each time the matter has been deferred, and he has seconded the deferral, because it is not for the King to overrule his shareholders, and because Mr Pilbeam is, he says, such a comfort to have about the place, and he does not know what he would do on a Tuesday without him.

When the Forty-Eighth told him that he was only passing, the King looked at him with great kindness, and said, "Oh dear. That's how Mr Pilbeam started."

## The hinges

<a href="Hob_Grumnir">Hob Grumnir</a> made the hinges on the door of the Princess's tower, a long time ago, and in the year 2000 asked whether he might have them back. "Your hinges? Oh dear. They're the only part that's held. We're on the fourteenth door. Every rescuer kicks it in, and the door goes, and the hinges are fine. Take the hinges and there's only so much door." Hob agreed that there was, and left them.

## Afterwards

He is seventy. There have been no kidnappings since 1999, Rosamund having taken to walking up the hill on Thursdays of her own accord, which the Heroes' Guild has complained about, in writing. The fifteenth door is drawn on the council table, and the hinges are not on the drawing, because they will not be needed. The eagle is still deferred. He says that he has given away half of everything he had eleven times over, and that he has never once been poorer for it, because every half went to somebody who brought his daughter home, and that a man who has been given his daughter back eleven times is the richest king in the east, and is down to a ditch, and is perfectly content. Then he asks whether you are here about the drainage.
