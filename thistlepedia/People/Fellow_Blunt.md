<p class="lede"><b>Fellow Blunt</b> was the member of <a href="Roland_Fairweather">Roland Fairweather</a>'s review responsible for the stone. For twenty-two years he went out to <a href="../Places/Carn_Drey">Carn Drey</a> once a quarter, looked at the stone from the gate, came back, and reported at the next meeting, under item three, that it was not ready. He never once went down the stairs to it. The stairs were not his department.</p>

## Readiness

The review sat in the snug of the Bell at <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a>, under a Convenor, with three Fellows: Fellow Grice, who reported the sorcerer's apologies; Fellow Amory, who took the minutes; and Fellow Blunt, who reported on the stone and the sword. At the hundred and forty-first meeting, which the Forty-Eighth's party attended, item three went like this, and I am told it had always gone like this:

> **The Convenor:** Item three. Readiness. The stone?
>
> **Fellow Blunt:** Not ready.
>
> **The Convenor:** The sword?
>
> **Fellow Blunt:** In the stone.

He said nothing else at that meeting. Before it, while they waited for the asset, he said, "The stone is not ready. I check the stone." I am told by people who were at the table that he did not say it unkindly, or as a man who doubted it. He said it as somebody saying where he lives.

## The gate

There is an iron gate across the mouth of the cave in Carn Drey's south face, and on it is a plate: THE WORM. NOT READY. (FELLOW BLUNT, QUARTERLY.) That is his. He put it up in 1978, when the review began, and he came out to it every quarter, by his own account in all weathers, and stood at the gate, and looked down the stairs into the dark at a stone he could not see from there, and went home and said that it was not ready.

The gate was not locked. Nobody ever tried it. In the year 2000 the Forty-Eighth suggested to Roland Fairweather that somebody might go down the stairs and look, and there was a very long pause, and then he said, "...Today?" The stone was ready. It had, as far as anybody can tell, been ready for some time. The sword came out of it in Roland's hand, and the Worm under the hill was ended that afternoon. Under NOT READY on Fellow Blunt's plate somebody has since written, in pencil: READY. DONE. (R.F.)

<div class="marginalia">I wrote to Fellow Blunt to ask whether, in twenty-two years, he had ever been tempted to go down. He wrote back by return, two lines: <i>The stairs are not my department. It is minuted.</i> Then, underneath, in a different ink, as if added the next morning: <i>It was, I find, ready.</i> I have not been able to improve on either line. - H.B.</div>

## The yard

He also keeps a yard at Great Dunnage, where the review had its understudy taught: sword, horse, lineage, the lot. In case. <a href="Maud_Penhallow">Maud Penhallow</a> went into it in 1993, at nine, and was told that the prophecy was nearly ready, which was his report and the review's, and was true, as far as the gate went. Everything Miss Penhallow knows about a sword she learned in his yard. She says he was a very good teacher, and very patient, and that he never once told her she was ready either, about anything, which she has decided was a matter of department.

## Afterwards

He is sixty-four. The review met once more after the Worm, to minute the prophecy discharged. It took four minutes. Under item three Fellow Blunt reported, for the first time, that the stone was ready. Fellow Amory underlined it. The review has not met since, and Fellow Blunt has nothing left to check, but he still goes out to Carn Drey once a quarter, in all weathers, and stands at the gate and reads the pencil, and comes home.

Miss Penhallow is still in the yard. He has not asked her to leave. He has started asking her opinion.
