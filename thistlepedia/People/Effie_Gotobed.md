<p class="lede"><b>Effie Gotobed</b> is a girl of <a href="../Places/Much_Pottering">Much Pottering</a>, the village where nothing has ever happened, who has written to Spare Cotterill of Upper Clanging every month since they were both six, under the twinning, and has told him in every letter that she is nearly a princess and that Much Pottering has a volcano and a sea serpent. He told her that he was six foot and drove an airship. In the year 2000 they met, at her gate, and knew each other at once, and were both furious about it.</p>

## The gate

She waits at her gate for the post every day at about the time it comes, and has since she was six, so that the top bar of the Gotobeds' gate is worn smooth where somebody small leans on it. When the Forty-Eighth came up the lane she looked at him with great hope and asked whether he was the post, and when he said he was not, told him so: "You're not the post." Then she told him everything.

> "I'm waiting for a letter from Upper Clanging. From my pen pal. He's six foot. He drives an airship called the Two Hundred, and he's the foreman at the works. He's nine."

She did not see anything wrong with the last part. She has never once seen anything wrong with it, and I have come to agree with her: if you are going to be six foot and a foreman, nine is as good an age as any.

> "He's got a dragon now. It's called Ferrule. It's quite big. I've told him we've got a volcano. And a sea serpent, in the pond. Don't tell him."

## The twinning

In 1997 Mrs Agatha Throssell, chair of the parish council, chose Upper Clanging off the map for the twinning, as the most different place she could find, and arranged that one child of six from each village should write to the other. Effie was chosen for Much Pottering because she was the only child of six in it. She says that she was chosen because she was the best at writing, and that being the only one was a coincidence.

Her first letter, which her mother still has, is four words long and says HELLO. I AM EFFIE. The second says that she is nearly a princess. The volcano comes in at the fourth. By the tenth there is a sea serpent. The writing gets bigger and worse the further back you go in the box on the cabinet, and better and smaller towards the top, and the stories get bigger the other way.

<div class="marginalia">Her atlas has Upper Clanging circled in red. Her copy of *Letters, And How To Write Them* has every page turned down at the corner, which defeats the purpose, and she knows. Over her bed is a picture of a very tall chimney, drawn by somebody who has heard about chimneys and is excited. She has never seen a chimney taller than the Gotobeds'. She has drawn the one in the picture forty feet high, with smoke in three colours. - H.B.</div>

## The meeting

When Spare Cotterill came up the lane with the Forty-Eighth in the year 2000, she looked at him, and he looked at her, and neither of them said anything for some time.

*You're not six foot.*

*You're not a princess.*

She had never said she was a princess. She had said she was nearly a princess. It is different. It is nearly.

She showed him the volcano, which is Mr Dibble's bonfire, lit in November, which has never gone out; it is dormant, you can tell because it is smoking. She showed him the sea serpent, which is Old Tom, Mr Cobley's carp, thirty years unseen, and something very large and very slow turned over in the pond in front of all of them, and the duck went up in the air, and Effie said *I TOLD you*. She showed him the post, a man in a red coat, and he said he knew; he does theirs. Then he told her the boiler at home had never gone out, not since before Mam, and had ninety tubes, and that he brushed them, and she said that she was not a princess, and there was no volcano, and it was the first true thing either of them had said all morning, and they both noticed.

Then she asked if he would still write. He said that his mam said it did not have to be true. "Make the volcano go off," she said. They wrote one each, there and then, on their knees, and the courier walked the four steps from one to the other, and they read them at once, in front of each other, which you are not supposed to do.

## The well

On the windlass of the village's wishing well is a label in a child's capitals: I WISH FOR A VOLCANO. A PROPER ONE. E.G. Under it, on the same string, in different pencil, is A DRAGON. S.C. Nobody in Much Pottering knows when the second label was added. I believe it was added that morning, and that neither of them has ever told the other.

## Afterwards

She is eleven, and still waits at the gate for the post, and it still comes about now. The volcano has gone off since, by letter, twice, and buried Mr Dibble, who is fine. Spare is a big brother now, and writes to say that Washer is the size of a turbine and can lift the works, and Effie writes back that Much Pottering has had an earthquake, which is true, if you count the time the pig rolled over in its sleep against the post office. Her mother keeps every one of his letters in the box on the cabinet. Effie does not know her mother has told anybody that, and I have been asked not to say, and so I have not, here, in so many words.
