<p class="lede"><b>Mirabel</b> is the Last Oracle of <a href="../Places/Olde_Evermore">Olde Evermore</a>, the hidden village of the elves, which every party finds eventually by falling off the same ledge. She is four thousand one hundred and fourteen years old, and looks about eight, and a quarter. She has foreseen the fall of empires, and has to be in bed by half past seven.</p>

## The Oracle

She stands on a stepstool in the middle of the Forbidden Archive, with long silver hair and a silver circlet, and the look of somebody who has seen all of this before, because she has. When she prophesies she lifts her hands, and every candle in the Archive leans towards her, and she speaks in the Old Tongue, and it is flawless, and it is terrible, and Elder Lirien translates:

> "When the moon devours the sun, and the last bell falls silent, the one who was not chosen shall..."

> "...is that a biscuit?"

It always is. It was in the Forty-Eighth's pocket, and he had forgotten it was there, and she had not. The prophecy does not get finished, and nobody minds. She has been doing that one since the Second Age, and it is always a biscuit, and I have come to think that the only thing in the world more reliable than the Prophecy of Thistlewick is Mirabel's appetite, which has the advantage of being honest about it.

The Elders, every one of them visibly older than she looks and some thousands of years younger than she is, call her Grandmother, and are sent to fetch things from the top shelf, which is where the *Chronicle of the Second Age* is kept, so that she cannot reach it, because she reads the endings first. Elder Lirien translates for her when she prophesies, and when she asks for pudding, and the pudding is more urgent.

## The signing

She was at the signing of the Prophecy of Thistlewick, in the first year of the Third Age, a child of about two thousand, and she is the only eyewitness I have been able to find. "It was ever so long. Everybody had to sign it three times." So she drew a horse on it.

Elder Faelan has views about this, which are that it was in the margin of a sacred and binding instrument, Grandmother. She says that it is a good horse. It is. I have seen the Archive's fair copy, and the copyist copied everything, and it is there, very small, in wax crayon. I asked her nothing further about it, and she offered nothing further, and I record that we were both entirely satisfied with the arrangement.

## The secret

She told the Forty-Eighth a secret, a real one, not a prophecy: that Elder Lirien was going to have a baby. Across the Archive, Elder Lirien dropped a scroll, and at the foot of the ladder Elder Faelan stopped moving altogether. She had foreseen it, and told them at breakfast, before they knew. "I've known since the ninth of March." It is a girl, and she is going to be called Biscuit. Elder Faelan said that she is not going to be called Biscuit.

"She is, though."

## Bedtime

The Archive clock is tall and beautifully kept and says a quarter past seven, and the pendulum is tied to the side of the case with a hair ribbon, in a very neat bow. "It's nearly half past seven. I'm not tired. I have seen the fall of empires. I can see the clock. It says a quarter past."

Elder Lirien says that she has to be in bed by half past seven. They tried eight, once. She foresaw the fall of an empire in the middle of supper, out of sheer tiredness. It was only a small empire. Still. Her bed is in the corner of the Archive, turned down, with a stone hot-water bottle already in it, and on the pillow there is a note in the Old Tongue, flawless and terrible, which Elder Lirien translates without looking up: *Not tired.*

## Afterwards

She is four thousand one hundred and fourteen now, and says that she is eight and a half, and the Elders have agreed to mention the half. The clock still says a quarter past seven. The ribbon has been changed, once, for a blue one.

I wrote, for this article, to ask whether there was anything she would like to foresee for the readers of Volume Three. The reply came in the Old Tongue, in a large round hand, and Elder Lirien's translation was pinned to it. It began, *When the moon devours the sun,* and stopped, and under it, in Elder Lirien's hand: *She has asked whether you have any biscuits. I have told her that you are a long way off. She says you are not as far off as you think.* I am keeping a tin by the door.

Elowen's tea cosy, with the two little sleeves and the B on the front, is finished. I have not been told what it is being used for, and when I asked, both Elders went the colour of the carpet, and Mirabel said that it was a secret, a real one, and that she was not allowed to tell this one, because she had told the last one.
