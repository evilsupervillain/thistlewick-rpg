<p class="lede"><b>Lionel Pell</b> is the Registrar of the <a href="../Institutions/The_Adventurers_Guild">Adventurers' Guild (Provisional)</a> at Nether Sopping, and has been since it was founded, provisionally, in 1960. For eleven years he could register nobody, because Form A-1 had been mislaid, and he looked for it every day, everywhere in the hall, and it was under the doormat, six feet from his desk. He is aware of the tower. He is aware that the key was under the mat. He does not find the pattern amusing.</p>

## A registered man

He was born in 1940 in Nether Sopping and registered the same afternoon, by his father, who was the parish clerk and who did it, his son says, *in a good hand and before tea*, and who believed that a thing which had not been written down had not, in any sense that mattered, happened. Lionel Pell has believed the same all his life. He believes it more firmly, he says, than some men believe in a god, and with better evidence, because he has the forms.

He was a clerk in the harbour office from fifteen until 1960, when the Guild was founded on the front, provisionally, pending a review, and wanted a Registrar who knew one end of a form from the other. He took the post at twenty. He has never taken another.

## The three requirements

He recites them to everybody who comes in, whether or not they have asked, because a requirement not recited is a requirement nobody can be held to.

> "Form A-1. Three requirements. One: a character reference from a recognised authority. Two: a hat. Three: Form A-1."

> "The form requires a hat. It does not say why. It does not say which. Mrs Barrow across the way sells one for thirty, and no, we are not related."

They are not related. I have looked into it, at his request, because he wished it to be in an encyclopedia where it could be pointed to. The Pells of Nether Sopping and the Barrows of Wick & Barrow have no ancestor in common back to 1712, which is as far as the Nether Sopping register goes. He says that people assume it because of the hat. He says that he did not write the form.

## The mat

Form A-1 was mislaid in 1989. For eleven years nobody registered, because nobody could, and Mr Pell looked for it every day. "If you find it, you may fill it in. That is the whole of the process." In the year 2000 the Forty-Eighth lifted the mat by the door, which says WELCOME, and found it. Mr Pell looked at it for a long time. "Form A-1: found. Astonishing." And then, before anybody could speak:

> "...Do not say it. I am aware of the tower. I am aware that the key was under the mat. I do not find the pattern amusing."

Nobody said it. Everybody has been not saying it since.

## Form Z

In a drawer of his desk which most people never notice he kept, for forty years, a single sheet, yellow at the edges: Form Z, *Matters Not Anticipated*. In case. "Never once used. In forty years every matter has been anticipated. By somebody. On something." After the ending, the Forty-Eighth put a bounty card on his desk that had no authority, no number and a drawing of an octopus on the back, for <a href="Something_Unscheduled">something that had been met</a> at the bottom of the <a href="../Places/The_Stair">Stair</a> under <a href="../Places/Hithermost">Hithermost</a>. "There is no form for this," said Mr Pell. And then he said it again, more slowly, and opened the drawer.

He discharged it on Form Z, and stamped it, and the Forty-Eighth was no longer provisional: the first member of the Guild in forty years who was not. "I shall mention it to the review," said Mr Pell. "When it sits."

<div class="marginalia">I asked him how it had felt, after forty years, to use Form Z at last. He said that it had felt like filing. I asked whether it had felt like anything else. He was quiet for a while, and then he said that he had had a second one printed, at his own expense, and that it was in the drawer, and that it was a very great comfort to him to know that it was there, and that I was not to read anything into that, and I have not, in print. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

He is sixty-one, and the review has not sat. He has mentioned the Forty-Eighth to it, by letter, and the letter is in the filing, under the review, pending. The board in the hall has had a single card on it since 2000, NO VACANCIES, because a board with nothing on it looked, he said, as if the Guild had stopped expecting things. He has had the mat taken up, and the floor under it inspected, and nothing was found, and he has had the mat put back, because a hall should have a mat, and he has written on the back of it, in a good hand, before tea: NOTHING UNDER HERE. CHECKED.
