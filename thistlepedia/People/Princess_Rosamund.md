<p class="lede"><b>Princess Rosamund</b> of <a href="../Places/Fairholme">Fairholme</a> has been kidnapped eleven times, and rescued eleven times, and has a bag packed by the window at all times, eleven sensible travelling cloaks, a tiara for kidnappings, and a card on her table for whoever it is this time. On Thursdays she walks up the hill to Mrs Emberthwaite's, on her own two feet, with a cake tin, to play whist, and the Heroes' Guild has written to complain.</p>

## Already

She was born in 1978, the only child of the King and Queen. Her portrait in the chamber shows her at six, in a ball gown, holding a small packed bag. She says that she does not remember why; that her mother had the painter in, and that she had packed it herself that morning, for a picnic, and would not put it down. Everybody who looks at the portrait now says *already*, and she says that she knows, and that she has found it very difficult to explain to people that it was only a picnic, and has stopped trying.

Her mother died when she was five. She was brought up by her father and a series of governesses, and by the time of the first kidnapping, at eight, she had read every book in the palace library about princesses in towers, and had formed, she says, a very low opinion of the arrangements.

## The kidnappings

The first, in 1986, was a sorcerer. She was carried off from her room in the tower, in her nightdress, in the rain, with nothing, and was three days in a cold tower in the hills before Osric came for her, and took the half, and sat down at the council table, where he has been doing the arithmetic since, and she says that she spent all three days making a list. The list became the bag. The bag has been by the window ever since.

> "It's the bag by the window, if anybody asks. It is always the bag by the window."

In the bag: a book, a cardigan, sandwiches in greaseproof paper, made that morning, and a second round labelled OTHERS, for the kidnappers, who she says are generally very hungry and very badly organised. On the table beside it is the card:

<div class="stamp">FOR WHOEVER IT IS THIS TIME. No eels. No mushrooms. Nothing in aspic. Tea at four, and not stewed. I will not be carried unless there are stairs. I would like to be home by Thursday. Thank you so much for having me.</div>

Under her pillow is a rope ladder. She has never used it, because she thinks it would be unfair on the rescuers, who have generally come a long way.

She has been taken, in thirteen years, by five sorcerers (*everybody had a sorcerer*), a wyrm, two lots of bandits who were sorry about it, a rival kingdom by mistake, a witch who wanted company, and two men from beyond the marshes who left her on a dragon's doorstep; and once, by an eagle, which does not count. Each kidnapping has its cloak, labelled. Each rescuer has a chapter in her memoir, *Rescued Again*, eleven volumes, written about very kindly. The eagle has a cloak and no chapter, pending the council's decision.

<div class="marginalia">I asked her whether she was ever frightened. She said, the first time. She said that after that she had the bag, and the card, and that she found that most people who kidnap a princess have not thought it through, and are very relieved to be given a sandwich and told what to do, and that the frightening part, really, is the stairs. "Mind the seventh stair, it's a trap. The eighth is also a trap. The ninth is fine." She put the seventh in herself, at fourteen, for practice. - H.B.</div>

## Thursdays

The eleventh kidnapping, in 1999, was the one up the hill. Two men from beyond the marshes, who had been told there was a dragon on the hill and that dragons keep princesses, carried her up to the cave in the rain and left her at the door, and ran. Mrs Emberthwaite opened the door. She had not had anybody to talk to since Mr Emberthwaite. She made up the spare room. That evening, after a rubber of whist, which was a Thursday, the Princess came down the hill on her own, with a tin of cake, and Mr and Mrs Budge, who were only mucking out, walked her the rest of the way home, and were given the half, and did not know what to do with it, and put most of it back into the orchard.

> "I go up the hill on Thursdays. Mrs Emberthwaite does a lovely sponge, and she cheats at whist."

> "She said she hadn't had anybody to talk to since Mr Emberthwaite. So now I walk. It's quicker."

She has never stayed the night. The spare room is aired on Wednesdays anyway, and there is a hot-water bottle in the bed, and the card on the pillow says ROSAMUND - THURSDAYS. She walks home after the whist. She has not yet beaten Mrs Emberthwaite at it, and says she will, and that it will be the twelfth thing she has escaped from.

## Afterwards

She is twenty-four. There have been no kidnappings since 1999, because it is very difficult to kidnap a princess who is up a hill playing cards with a dragon, and the Heroes' Guild has written to the Palace to complain that she is *unreasonably unavailable*. She has replied, kindly, in writing, with a sandwich. The bag is still by the window. The twelfth volume of *Rescued Again* is in hand, and is about Thursdays, and has no rescuer in it at all, and she says it is the best of them.
