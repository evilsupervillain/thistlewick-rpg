<p class="lede"><b>Swale</b> was the Convenor of the review of <a href="Roland_Fairweather">Roland Fairweather</a>'s prophecy, which met in the snug of the Bell at <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a> from 1978 until the year 2000, a hundred and forty-two times, for eleven minutes, under her. She is a grey-haired woman in spectacles and a blue jacket, and she ran every one of those meetings to time and to the agenda, and thanked the asset for his continued patience at the end of each, and meant it, and I have never been able to decide whether that makes it better or worse.</p>

## The agenda

The meetings had five items, and they were always the same five. I give the hundred and forty-first, which the Forty-Eighth's party attended, because I am told by everybody who was at any of them that they were all the same, and because the minutes, which I have read, agree:

> "Mr Fairweather. Good. We're quorate. The hundred and forty-first meeting."

Item one was apologies. Fellow Grice reported that the sorcerer sent his. Item two was the minutes of the previous meeting, which Fellow Amory, who had taken them, agreed. Item three was readiness, which she took in order: the stone (<a href="Fellow_Blunt">Fellow Blunt</a>: "Not ready"), the sword ("In the stone"), the sorcerer (Fellow Grice: "Sends his apologies") and the lineage, at which Roland said "Present", and Fellow Amory wrote it down and underlined it. Item four was the continued availability of the asset. "Available," said Roland. "Noted, with thanks," said the Convenor. Item five was any other business. She asked for it every time, and waited, and there never was any.

> "Then thank you, Mr Fairweather, for your continued patience. Until the next door."

Then the four of them shook his hand and went out into the street together. When the asset was late, and he was often late, because he was always somewhere at somebody else's last door, she did not begin without him. "Mr Fairweather is expected. We can't start without the asset." She waited in the snug with her papers, and was not impatient, and when he came in she said "Good," as if he had been punctual.

## The Convenor

I have not been able to find out who appointed her, or to what. The minutes begin in 1978 with the review already constituted, with her at the head of it: *Present: Swale (Convenor), Grice, Amory, Blunt. In attendance: the asset.* She was forty-nine. She has told me that she was born in 1929, in Great Dunnage, and that she has convened a number of things in the town, the Bell's quiz among them, for thirty years, and that she was asked to convene the review because she was known to be a good convenor, and that she said yes because she is. That is the whole of what she would say about it, and I believe all of it, and none of it answers the question.

She is not, I think, a hard woman. Mrs Dewhirst, who has kept the Bell through all of it, says that the Convenor always paid for the snug herself, and for Mr Fairweather's half, and that in twenty-two years she never once let him see her do it. She also says that the Convenor never once let him see anything else, either, and that she does not know which of those she means.

## Item five

In the year 2000 a girl of sixteen, <a href="Maud_Penhallow">Maud Penhallow</a>, who had been the review's understudy since she was nine, asked Roland Fairweather whose prophecy it was, exactly. It is the only question anybody asked about the review in twenty-two years that was not on its agenda, and it was not put to the review, and it has not been answered, and this article is not going to answer it. What I can say is when it could have been asked. Every meeting ended with any other business, and the Convenor asked for it every time, and waited. Nobody, in a hundred and forty-one meetings, said anything.

After the Worm under Carn Drey was ended, the review met once more, to minute the prophecy discharged. It took four minutes. Under item three Fellow Blunt reported that the stone was ready, and Fellow Amory underlined it. Under item four, I am told, the Convenor did not ask about the asset's availability. She thanked him instead, for his patience, without the word continued. Under item five she asked for any other business, and waited, and there was none, and she closed the review.

<div class="marginalia">I wrote to the Convenor to ask who had commissioned the review, and on whose behalf the prophecy had been kept. Her reply came by return and was perfectly courteous. It said that the matter was not on the agenda. It said that any other business might be raised under item five, at the next meeting. Under that, in the same hand, a line lower, it said that there would not be a next meeting, and that she was sorry. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

The review has not met since 2000, and the snug at the Bell is still booked every quarter, in Mr Fairweather's name, the usual, by standing order. The Convenor has not cancelled it. She says that she has not been asked to, and that it is not on any agenda she convenes. The card on the corner table still says RESERVED, and Maud Penhallow sits under it, and has her pie there, and nobody has asked her to move.

The Convenor still convenes the Bell's quiz. It takes eleven minutes. There is always a round for any other business, and she always waits.
