<p class="lede"><b>Dame Winifred Cope</b> of the Order of the Errant is the Chapter's knight in the field at <a href="../Places/Little_Gore">Little Gore</a>, and has been since 1989, and there is a pin in her on the great map at Coldbarrow, just the one, and it is correct. She stands outside her own front door as if it were a post. Somebody has to be where the pin is, or what is the map for? Every quarter she writes NOTHING TO REPORT, and the Chapter writes back VERY GOOD, and she says it is the nicest correspondence she has ever had.</p>

## The field

She was born in 1958 at Saltinge, on the east coast, the daughter of the harbourmaster, and was, she says, the only child in the town who wanted to stay exactly where she was; her brothers went to sea, and she stood on the harbour wall and watched them go and liked it there. She went into the Order of the Errant at twenty and was knighted at twenty-six, and asked for a posting where a knight would be expected to stay, and the Chapter, after some searching, found her one.

> "Dame Winifred Cope, of the Order of the Errant. The Chapter's knight in the field at Little Gore. Eleven years. There's a pin in me at Coldbarrow. Just the one."

Her door has a card on it in the Order's lettering, KNIGHT IN THE FIELD. DO NOT DISTURB, and she stands four feet from it, and as far as the Chapter is concerned that is the field.

## Nothing to report

> "I write every quarter: NOTHING TO REPORT. They write back: VERY GOOD. It's the nicest correspondence I've ever had."

She has written it fifty-one times, in March 2002. Mordaunt, the Order's clerk, keeps her file, and says that it is the thinnest file in the Chapter House and the only one he has never once had to correct, and that he looks forward to her quarter day the way other men look forward to the post at Christmas.

<div class="marginalia">In the year 2000 Hob Grumnir came to Little Gore and saw that her helmet was one he had made, a long time ago, for somebody else, and asked whether he might have it back. She said, "I'm in it." He asked whether it had kept her safe. She said: "Not a dent in it. Nothing to report. That's your helmet." He has told me since that that was the whole of her reason, and that it was a very good one, and that a helmet with nothing to report is the best review a smith can have. - H.B.</div>

## Abel Thrush

For two years, from 1989 to 1991, the carrier Abel Thrush came through Little Gore every day on his round, and she stood at her post, and he went by, and in two years he never said a word to her. Tom Vane, who walked the round with him sometimes, says that Abel never stopped talking, to anybody, and that he cannot understand it. Dame Winifred says that she can. She says that he was the only man in the county who understood that a knight in the field is on duty, and does not want chatting to, and that he used to touch his hat to her every morning without a word, and that she has missed it every morning since he died.

## Afterwards

She is forty-four. Since 2000 a second Pemberton-Gore has come through Little Gore, as squire, the Fourth, with Sir Aldric, and found the way to her door first time, and saluted, and she says that it was very correct, and that she reported it, which was the first time in eleven years that she had anything to report, and that she wrote it under NOTHING TO REPORT, as a postscript, so as not to spoil the run.
