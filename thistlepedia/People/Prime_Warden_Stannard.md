<p class="lede"><b>Stannard</b> is the Prime Warden of <a href="../Institutions/The_Worshipful_Company_of_Smiths">the Worshipful Company of Smiths</a> of <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a>. She is a tall woman in black, with iron-grey hair pinned up and gold at her collar, and she stands at the Company's table as if it might try something. It never has. She presides over the Assize of Hammers, at which every smith in the land may submit one piece, and very few manage to, because it is a very good Schedule, and she would like it understood that that is the point.</p>

## Are you a smith?

She introduces herself in full, every time, and I have never heard her shorten it:

> "Stannard. Prime Warden of the Worshipful Company of Smiths of Great Dunnage. Are you a smith?"

There are two answers to this, and only one of them gets you anywhere. The Forty-Eighth said that he was a turnip farmer, and was told, "Then you may look. Do not touch. The Assize is in the spring. It is every three years." I said that I was a historian, and was told the same, and asked whether a history was a piece, and she considered it, as she considers everything, as though it might be submitted the next morning. "A history is not a piece," she said. "Half a history is not a piece either. Nobody has ever tried." I have not.

On later visits she does not ask again. "Look. Do not touch. The Schedule is on the wall."

She was born in Great Dunnage in 1936, and went into the Chain Works at fourteen, and made chain. At the Assize of 1958 she submitted one piece, which was a chain, and the Wardens sat for two days on whether a chain was one piece or several, under a clause she would not give me the number of, because, she said, I should look it up. It was ruled one piece, and it was given the Company's mark, and she was made a Warden in 1974 and Prime Warden in 1982, and she has not submitted anything since, because a Warden may not, and she approves of that too. The chain is on the wall of the Hall, behind the Wardens' table, where it holds up the Schedule.

## Beatrice

In the year 2000 <a href="Hob_Grumnir">Hob Grumnir</a> came into the Hall and stood in front of the Company's table with his cap in both hands, and said that he was a smith. "Then you may submit. One piece. Your own work, from the ore up, and your own property." He explained that that was where it came unstuck. He did not own anything he had made. Somebody wanted a thing, and he said take it.

> "And the invoicing?"
>
> "There's a shelf."

The Prime Warden looked at him, I am told, the way a toll-keeper looks at a bridge with nobody on it. "Then you have never entered." He said never once, and went round eleven people to get one thing back, and came back, some time later, with nothing, and every one of the eleven had had a better reason than his. Then he laid his hammer on the table. She asked whether it was his piece, and he said that she was not his to call a piece, and that he would like her looked at, proper, once.

She did not send him away. She gave him clause thirty-nine, which is that a piece is examined on receipt against clauses one and two, so that nobody waits three years to be told; and Warden Crabbe read those two clauses out, and <a href="Miss_Fettle">Miss Fettle</a> asked who had made it, and Hob said not him, and the Prime Warden ruled: "Clause two: the entrant's property. Satisfied. Clause one: the entrant's own work. Not satisfied. Disqualified." And then she wrote the Wardens' notes on a card, in her own hand, as is done for every piece examined, and at the bottom of the card, where there is a space for remarks, which in my reading of the Company's records nobody had used before, she wrote one: *In the Wardens' opinion, right about everything.*

I asked her whether the Schedule provides for remarks. She said that it provides a space, and that the space is the Schedule's, and that what goes in it is the Wardens', and that the decision of the Wardens is final, and so is the Schedule, and that she had found that there was no conflict.

<div class="marginalia">I asked the Prime Warden for permission to quote the Schedule in this history. She said that the Schedule is on the wall, and anybody may look at it, and that I need not ask to quote what anybody may look at. Then she asked me whether I proposed to quote all of it, and when I said only the clauses I had, she said that in that case I might like to come in the spring, when Warden Crabbe reads it out, and that she would keep me a chair. I have accepted. It is the only invitation I have ever had that was also a correction. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

Beatrice's card is pinned up beside the prize hinge, with the Company's mark on it. Since 2000 four smiths have come to the Hall with a piece that was not their own work, and asked to have it looked at, proper, once, in the hope of a remark. She has examined every one of them on receipt, under clause thirty-nine, and disqualified every one of them under clause one, and written no remarks at all. A remark, she says, is not a prize. It is an opinion, and the Wardens have only had the one.

In 2001 the Hall asked Hob Grumnir and <a href="Bryd_Ollerenshaw">Bryd Ollerenshaw</a> to judge BEST IN HALL together, and she will not say whether that was a compliment or a joke. I asked her again for this article. She said that it was in the Hall's gift and not the Company's, and that she was not a judge of jokes, and that the prize had been awarded, and I should not ask to whom. The next Assize is in the spring of 2003. The Schedule has not been amended.
