<p class="lede"><b>Ambrosine Quill</b> is the senior chronicler of the <a class="new" href="../Institutions/The_Quills">Quills</a>, the family that has written the official account of every Chosen One of <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a> for four generations, and the aunt of <a href="Piper_Quill">Piper Quill</a>. She is an extremely good writer. She wrote the official account of the Forty-Eighth, and finished it before he had finished, because the shape is known.</p>

## The house style

The Quills write in metre, and the house style is one line long: *never let the truth spoil the metre.* Ambrosine Quill has kept to it all her working life, as her own mother did before her, who wrote numbers forty to forty-three and had a wolf in forty-one, forty-two and forty-three - the same wolf. A good wolf. The Committee approves every account. It does not read them. Ambrosine Quill has known this for a long time, and has gone on writing them well anyway, which I think is a kind of courage, and she would think it a very odd thing to call it.

## The Lay of the Forty-Eighth

> THE LAY OF THE FORTY-EIGHTH. Being the Authorised Account of the Chosen One, Bram Thistle. By A. Quill, Chronicler to the Committee. APPROVED.

She started it the morning the Committee voted, and the Committee approved it on the Tuesday, and when the Forty-Eighth came into Prophecy Hall with Piper she had it squared off on the table in front of her, twelve cantos, and a pen she was no longer using. "He hasn't finished yet," Piper said. "The shape has, dear."

It is very good. I have read it. It is properly metred, and it begins with an orphan boy of humble birth, which Bram is not - he has a mother, who was making a pie when he last saw her - because nobody goes north with a mother; it doesn't scan. There is a wolf. Merribell dies in canto nine, of nothing in particular, because it scans; Merribell, reading it, said that she would have had a glass of water and sat down. He slays the Dark Lord at the top of a tower in a thunderstorm, in a rhyme on *tower* that was never going to be anything but *hour*, and comes home. Bram read all twelve cantos twice, looking for a turnip, and did not find one.

And it has a very good middle. Everybody has a beginning and an end, she says, and in between they walk; nobody does the middle. She did the middle. Piper, who had come to fight, read canto six and said nothing for a moment, and then said, "It is a very good middle."

## What came of it

What came of it is the subject of a disagreement among the Quills which I have been asked, by all of them, not to settle. I give both accounts, as I give both reckonings, and I do not say which is which.

In one, the Forty-Eighth told her it was very good, which it was, and she took her spectacles off and held them, and did not put them back on for some while, because in four generations nobody had said so before. The account stands, as she wrote it, with a single footnote at the foot of canto twelve, in small type: *None of this happened. It is very good. - P.Q.* She read the footnote twice and initialled it, as to the wording.

In the other, the two of them sat down and put what happened into metre, and it took the afternoon. She gave up the orphan for a mother in a kitchen, and Merribell for a glass of water, and fought for the wolf for an hour, and lost it to a goat which had sent its apologies to the meeting at which Bram was chosen, and which is in the minutes. "A goat is a sort of wolf. If you squint." She said the middle was worse, and Piper said the middle was true, and she said that it scanned, just, and signed it A. QUILL and handed Piper the pen, and it is the first account in four generations with two Quills on it. The Clerk stamped it AMENDED, which he had not had cause to do before, and looked pleased.

Both of these are, I am assured, the authorised version.

## Afterwards

She is still Chronicler to the Committee. Asked about the account, she says either that it is a very good middle and she reads it again most mornings, and the footnote; or that it has a goat in it now, and the goat is in the minutes, so she cannot object. I have heard her say each of these, on different days, and have decided that this is a matter for the Quills.

What is not in dispute is that she and her niece nod to one another now, across a room, as one professional to another, which is new. Ambrosine Quill has asked to read this encyclopedia before it is bound, and has read the article on her niece, and returned it with one remark in the margin, which was that it does not scan. I have taken that as approval.
