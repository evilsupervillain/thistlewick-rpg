<p class="lede"><b>Councillor Fenn</b> sits on the <a href="../Institutions/The_Prophecy_Committee">Prophecy Committee</a> of <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>, and has said at every meeting for about twenty years that clause twelve is the problem. He was right. Nobody listened. The Committee's minute book for the week of the ending records, as item five, that Councillor Fenn wished it minuted that he had been saying so. Minuted.</p>

## The position

Clause twelve reads THE DARK LORD SHALL RISE AGAIN. For two thousand years Thistlewick read it as a warning. Councillor Fenn read it as what it is. "That's not a warning," he said. "That's a subscription." He said it in those words, at the end of the table, under the window, to colleagues who were discussing whether the <a href="Grimspite_the_Inevitable">Dark Lord</a> *shall be slain* or *shall be defeated*, because *slain* is a commitment.

The Forty-Eighth heard him say it on the morning he was charged, and the narrator of that morning, whoever it is that narrates mornings in Thistlewick, has it that nobody was listening to him, and adds: *Remember him.* I have. It is one of the few instructions in this history that I have been given by the history itself.

He has also, over the years, put up a petition to rename the <a href="../Places/The_Obligatory_Tower">Obligatory Tower</a> the Optional Tower, which has one signature on it, his own. It is on the Hall's noticeboard still, and it is not struck, because the Committee has never got round to reading it.

## The first time anybody asked

In the year 2000 somebody in the Forty-Eighth's company asked him what he meant, exactly, about clause twelve. He said, "...Nobody has ever asked." Then: "Sit down. Sit DOWN. It goes back to the original wording, and the original wording goes back to a horse -"

He was away for some time. It was, I am told, the first time in twenty years that somebody was nodding.

I do not know what he said about the horse. The person who asked does not remember, having been, for reasons I accept, attending to something else, and the Councillor, asked by me, began at the original wording, and I took four pages of notes, and I am not going to print them, because I do not understand them, and because there is a horse in the margin of the Prophecy beside clause twelve that this encyclopedia has decided, on advice, to leave exactly where it is.

At some point during the explanation a key went off its hook on his belt, the one marked STRUCK, that opens the door behind the red curtains, and later in the afternoon it went back on again. He did not notice either. I understand that this was the point, and I have been asked by <a href="Piper_Quill">Miss Quill</a> to say that he was owed the listening, and got it, whatever else was going on, and that she would do it again.

## Afterwards

Clause twelve was struck, *nem. con.*, the week of the ending, with a note in its place: SEE BRAM. Councillor Fenn voted for it, and then asked for item five.

He is still on the Committee, and still at the end of the table, and people now ask him things, which he says takes some getting used to. He has a new position, which is that clause nineteen was also the problem, and since clause nineteen was struck as well, six months later, he has been right twice and minuted twice, and he says it is a great deal better for the digestion. The petition for the Optional Tower still has one signature. He says it is a matter of principle, now, and that he would not want it spoiled.
