<p class="lede"><b>Marjorie Gotobed</b> is the mother of <a href="Effie_Gotobed">Effie Gotobed</a> and the wife of a retired adventurer, and keeps the Gotobeds' cottage in <a href="../Places/Much_Pottering">Much Pottering</a>, where the kitchen is also the parlour and nothing happens at any of the times on the clock. Her daughter's pen pal has told her daughter that he drives an airship. Mrs Gotobed has never had the heart.</p>

## The cottage

She was born Marjorie Thorne in 1955, in the cottage she lives in now, which was her mother's, and her mother's mother's, and has never in its history had anything happen in it, which in Much Pottering is a family distinction. She grew up in it, and was a quiet girl, and read a great many books about ladies in towers, and liked the bits in the middle best, before the rescue, when nothing much was going on and the lady was sewing.

## The wrong door

In the spring of 1983 a man in very good boots came down the lane on a quest to rescue a lady, and knocked on the wrong door. It was hers. She opened it, and he told her, with a sword in one hand and a map in the other, that he had come to rescue her. She told him that she did not need rescuing, and that he had better come in, because it was raining. He came in. He has been rescuing her ever since, mostly from spiders, which he puts out of the window.

Her account of it is on a card tucked behind one of his swords on the bedroom wall, in her hand, and I give it whole, because I could not improve on it:

<div class="stamp">HE CAME THROUGH ON A QUEST TO RESCUE A LADY. HE KNOCKED ON THE WRONG DOOR. IT WAS MINE. HE HAS BEEN RESCUING ME EVER SINCE, MOSTLY FROM SPIDERS, WHICH HE PUTS OUT OF THE WINDOW.</div>

They were married in 1984, at the church, in the morning, and the parish council minuted it as *Nothing arising*, which Mrs Throssell said was the highest compliment the parish could pay a wedding.

## The heart

Effie writes to Spare Cotterill of Upper Clanging every month, and he writes back, and his letters are on the cabinet in a box, tied up by the year in string. Mrs Gotobed told the Forty-Eighth so in the kitchen, in a lowered voice, though Effie was at the gate:

> "Waiting for the post. Her pen pal writes from Upper Clanging. They've been at it since they were six. He says he drives an airship. I've never had the heart."

And then, when he came back: "She keeps every one of his, on the cabinet. Don't tell her I said."

She has never had the heart to tell her daughter that a boy of nine does not drive an airship. She has never had the heart, either, to tell her daughter that there is no volcano, or that Old Tom is a carp, or that nearly a princess is not a thing. I asked her whether that was wise. She said that the books she read as a girl were all about ladies being rescued, and that the bit she had liked best was the middle, before anybody came, when the lady was getting on with things and nobody had told her yet how it was going to turn out. She said she would like Effie to have as much of the middle as possible.

<div class="marginalia">On the best-plate shelf of her dish cabinet, kept with the best ones, is a plate with a crayon volcano on it. On the painting of a mountain by the fireplace somebody has added, in crayon, on the glass, a good deal of smoke coming out of the top. On a potted plant in the window is a little flag, VOLCANO (DORMANT). Mrs Gotobed has not removed any of them. She says that you do not clean a volcano off a mountain in Much Pottering; it is the most exciting thing that has ever happened in the house. - H.B.</div>

## The tea cosies

She keeps two. One is for an ordinary afternoon. The other goes on top of the first, for a really quiet one. I asked her how often she used the second. She said, most days. I asked her whether she had ever needed a third. She said that she had knitted one, once, in 1997, for the afternoon the parish council discussed the twinning, and that it had been put away since, because there was no point having a cosy for an afternoon that quiet if you were never going to have another one.

## Afterwards

After the meeting at the gate in the year 2000, she found out that the airship was real, and was driven by somebody else, and that the boy was nine and the size of nine, and had come all the way from Upper Clanging, with a Chosen One, to see her daughter, and had brushed ninety boiler tubes since he was six. She sat down. She put the second cosy on. She says that she has never been so relieved in her life to find out that something was true, and that she has kept the letters since with a better conscience.

She still has not had the heart to tell Effie that there is no volcano. She says that it seems a bit late now, because, by letter, it has gone off twice.
