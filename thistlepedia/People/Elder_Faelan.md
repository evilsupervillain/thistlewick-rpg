<p class="lede"><b>Elder Faelan</b> is one of the Elders of <a href="../Places/Olde_Evermore">Olde Evermore</a>, the husband of Elder Lirien, and the elf who fetches things down from the top shelf of the Forbidden Archive for <a href="Mirabel">Mirabel</a>, whom he calls Grandmother. He is also the only person in the world who will tell you, plainly and without being asked twice, how the years have actually gone.</p>

## The top shelf

He is to be found at the foot of the Archive's ladder, or on it. "Elder Faelan. Forgive me - I am fetching the Chronicle of the Second Age for Grandmother." Everybody calls her Grandmother: "She was old when my grandmother was a girl, and my grandmother was a girl for a long time." The *Chronicle* is on the top shelf so that she cannot reach it, because she reads the endings first. "The top shelf. It is always the top shelf."

He has views about the margin of the Prophecy of Thistlewick, which are that it is the margin of a sacred and binding instrument, and he has expressed them to Grandmother, who says it is a good horse. I have not asked him for more than that and he has not offered it.

## The sun

When the Forty-Eighth mentioned who he was, Elder Faelan was delighted. "Are you! The Forty-Seventh came off the ledge ten years ago. A lovely boy. Landed on his feet." And then, without any unkindness at all:

> "Your Committee will have told you it was a hundred years. It counts the way the Prophecy does. We count the way the sun does."

And when the Forty-Eighth said, as everybody from Thistlewick says, that the Age began with the Prophecy:

> "The Age began with a great many things. The Prophecy was the one with a clerk present."

These are the two most useful sentences anybody gave me for this volume. The whole of <a href="../History/Two_Reckonings">Two Reckonings</a> is a long note on them. I asked him, by letter, what the other things were, that the Age began with. He wrote back very kindly that it was a long time ago, and that he had been on the ladder at the time, and that I should come and ask Grandmother, who had been on the floor, and would tell me the ending first.

## Afterwards

In the year 2000 Grandmother told him, across the Archive, in front of the Forty-Eighth, that Elder Lirien was going to have a baby; she had told them at breakfast, before they knew. He stopped moving altogether at the foot of the ladder, and then went the colour of the carpet, and could not stop smiling, which made the colour worse. He said that she was not going to be called Biscuit.

He is still at the foot of the ladder. He has made no further statement on the subject of the name. Asked for one by me, he went the colour of the carpet, and fetched something from the top shelf that I had not asked for, and gave it to me, and it was a biscuit tin, and it was empty, and I have decided not to read anything into that, which is a decision I make a good deal in Olde Evermore.
