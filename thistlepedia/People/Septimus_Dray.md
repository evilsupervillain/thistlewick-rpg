<p class="lede"><b>Septimus Dray</b> of <a href="../Places/Great_Dunnage">Great Dunnage</a> plays the serpent, an instrument of the bassoon family which has, in his hands and I am told in everybody's, given up. He entered the Contest at the Sign of the Bell three times in twenty-six years. He came second twice, of two entries, and third in the year 2000, of four, which he regards as his best result, because it was the first time he had beaten anybody.</p>

## The serpent

The serpent belonged to the parish church of Great Dunnage, from when the church had a band. In 1946 the parish bought an organ, and the band was stood down, and the serpent was put in the vestry cupboard. Mr Dray was sixteen and in the choir. He asked the vicar what was to become of it. The vicar said that nothing was, which Mr Dray thought was a shame for it, so he took it home, so that it would have somebody. He has played it every evening since, for fifty-six years, in the back kitchen, with the door shut. His neighbours have never complained. They have, he says, moved, but none of them complained.

It is a long black wooden pipe bent back and forth on itself, like a drain that has been folded to fit in a cupboard, and it sounds like one. Asked about this he says: "It's a serpent. It's meant to sound like that. Mostly."

## The Contest

The Contest at the Sign of the Bell is always tonight; in most years it simply has not had the entries. Mr Dray entered in 1974 and came second, of two. He entered again in 1988 and came second, of two. Asked how he had done, the evening the Forty-Eighth's company came in, he said:

> "Dray. Instrumental (Other Wind). Second place, twice. There were two entries, both times."

In the year 2000 there were four. He went first. He played for eleven minutes, and it was very like listening to a drain that has had bad news, and Mr Ambler gave it a nine, as he gives everything a nine, and Miss Quayle gave it a two, as she gives everything a two, and Mrs Harbottle gave it a six. Seventeen. Then he sat down, and when Piper Quill took the stage he was, with Miss Moon and the back table, part of the floor, which had views, and expressed them. It is the custom of the Bell. I am told that his contribution was to play under the quiet bits. Somewhere in her fourth verse he ran out of breath and sat down, to a smattering, and heard the rest of it, and the next morning he came round to the Bell before breakfast to tell her it had been very good, and that he had not meant any of the serpent personally.

He came third. He was pleased with this all week.

<div class="marginalia">I have heard the serpent. Mr Dray was kind enough to play it to me in his back kitchen, with the door shut, for the length of a cup of tea, and then for the length of a second cup, because I had drunk the first one quickly. I did not know what to say afterwards, and said that it was a remarkable instrument. He said that it was, and that most people didn't say so, and gave me a third cup. I have not been able to improve on "remarkable", and I have tried. - H.B.</div>

## Afterwards

He is seventy-one. He has entered every Contest since, now that there are Contests worth entering, Instrumental (Other Wind), the category still having nobody else in it. In 2001 he came fourth, of nine, which he says is the most people he has ever beaten in one evening. He is working on a shorter piece, of nine minutes, at the panel's suggestion. Mrs Harbottle has told him privately that the serpent is a six, and that a six is a very respectable number, and he has had it painted on the case.
