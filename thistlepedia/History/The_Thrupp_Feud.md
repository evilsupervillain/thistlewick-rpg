<p class="lede">The <b>Thrupp Feud</b> was a silence of thirty years between two sisters who keep inns forty miles apart, Dorcas Thrupp at <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a> and Prudence Thrupp at <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a>, conducted entirely by parcel. Neither of them will call it a feud, and neither of them can remember what it was about. It was ended in the year 2000 by the Forty-Eighth, who carried a jar south and something heavier north and was not told what was in either.</p>

## The stopping

Dorcas and Prudence Thrupp were both born at the Gilded Turnip, in 1948 and 1951, and were both behind its bar by the age of ten, and were by every account the best of friends and a terror to their father's customers. In 1970 they stopped speaking. There was no quarrel that anybody saw. There was no door slammed.

> "Thirty years we've not spoken. That's not a falling-out. We just stopped one day and neither of us started again. So we send each other things instead."

The next year the Slain Wyvern at Nether Sopping wanted a landlady, and Dorcas went south to take it, and Thistlewick did not mention it, Thistlewick having never mentioned that there was a town on the south coast at all. Prudence took the Turnip over from their father in 1976. They were forty miles apart, and stayed so, and kept the silence up on both ends, which, I have been told by a good many people who have tried, is harder than it sounds.

## Thirty years of parcels

They did not write. They sent things: by carrier, by tinkers, by the occasional hero going south and the more occasional one coming back, by anybody who would take a parcel and not ask. Nobody in either inn knows what went, because nobody was ever told, and nobody asked twice. The carriers on the coast road say that a Thrupp parcel was always very well tied, and that you could tell which way it was going by the knots, and that it was always a little heavier than the last one going the other way.

<div class="marginalia">I asked both sisters what had begun it, separately, a year apart. Dorcas said that she would not say. Prudence said that she would not say either. I am now fairly sure, from the way that each of them said it, that neither can remember, and that each of them believes the other can, and would rather die than ask. - H.B.</div>

## The jar

In the year 2000 the Forty-Eighth came through the Gilded Turnip on his way out of the village, properly out, roads and everything, and Prudence looked at him for slightly too long and gave him a jar.

> "There's a town on the south coast. Nether Sopping. Follow the road out of the gate and turn left at the sea. There's a woman there runs a tavern. Dorcas Thrupp. My sister. Give her this. Don't shake it. Don't ask what's in it. Tell her I said nothing. She'll know what I mean by it."

It was warm on one side. Dorcas knew the hand on the label at once. She held it up to the light, went an interesting colour, and said, "Thirty years. THIRTY. And she sends me THIS." It went on the highest shelf behind the bar, where it can be seen from every table in the room, and it is there still, and sealed.

## The reply

There was then a sound from the back room of the Wyvern like somebody doing carpentry to a hamper. What came out had been under construction for some time, against exactly this day, and it was heavier than the jar was. Considerably heavier.

> "Don't open it. Don't shake it. Don't let it near a fire. And tell her I send my love, because I do, and she knows I do, and that's her lot."

It went north. Prudence weighed it in both hands, put it on the bar, and did not open it for long enough that the whole room noticed. Then she opened it. Whatever was in there was worse than what had gone south, and deliberately worse.

> "...The cow."

She was laughing. The regulars of the Gilded Turnip say that they had not heard her laugh properly, from the bottom, in thirty years.

## The locket

Then she went upstairs and came down with the locket: two little portraits on ivory, one of each sister, facing resolutely away from each other, made at the Michaelmas fair in 1962, when Dorcas was fourteen and Prudence eleven, and kept in a drawer since.

> "It's hers and mine and it's been in a drawer since we were girls and I'm sick of the sight of it. That was HER idea, that was. I'll write to her. Don't tell her that. I'll write to her at the weekend."

Dorcas says that the facing away was Prudence's idea. Neither of them has looked for the man who painted it.

## The terms

Prudence wrote at the weekend: four pages, two of them about the Forty-Eighth, and one of those complimentary. Dorcas wrote back. It was the first letter either of them had written to the other in thirty years, and they have written every month since. Prudence's go south by the carrier on the last Friday of the month and Dorcas's come north on the first Thursday, so that each has the other's letter for a week before she must answer it, which Prudence says is the right amount of time.

They have not met. Each has written to say that the other must come to her: Dorcas because she is the elder, Prudence because Dorcas was the one who moved. Each has had a reply saying exactly the same thing the other way round. Dorcas says that they are getting somewhere.
