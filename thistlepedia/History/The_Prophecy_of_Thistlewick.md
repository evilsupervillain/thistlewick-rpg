<p class="lede">The <b>Prophecy of Thistlewick</b> is a document of very great length, kept by the <a class="new" href="../Institutions/The_Prophecy_Committee">Prophecy Committee</a> in <a href="../Places/Prophecy_Hall">Prophecy Hall</a>, which for very nearly two thousand years told the village of <a href="../Places/Thistlewick">Thistlewick</a> that every hundred years the Dark Lord would rise in the <a href="../Places/The_Obligatory_Tower">Obligatory Tower</a> and that somebody had better go. It was right every time. That should have been the first clue.</p>

It was never a prophecy. It is an employment contract, with numbered clauses, and the post it filled was the Dark Lord's. That was not understood until the year 2000, and then only because a turnip farmer did the one thing nobody in Thistlewick had ever done with it, which was to read it.

## The signing

The Prophecy was signed in the first year of the Third Age, and Thistlewick has always held that the Age began with it. The elves of <a href="../Places/Olde_Evermore">Olde Evermore</a>, who were there, say the Age began with a great many things and that the Prophecy was the one with a clerk present. I have not been able to get either party to say more, and I record both.

What is agreed is that the signing was ever so long. I have that from an eyewitness: <a href="../People/Mirabel">Mirabel</a>, the oracle of Olde Evermore, who attended as a child of about two thousand and was bored. Everybody had to sign it three times. So she drew a horse on it.

The Forbidden Archive at Olde Evermore keeps a fair copy made at the signing by a copyist who copied *everything*, and I can confirm that "everything" includes the margins. The horse is in the margin beside clause twelve. It is a good horse. I have nothing further to say about it, and when I asked the copyist whether *he* had anything further to say about it, he went on copying.

Nobody has told me where the original is, or who held the pen for the other side.

## What it says

The Prophecy has never been printed entire, and I have not attempted it here; Volume Two is large enough. These are the clauses I have been able to establish, from the copy, from the people they were done to, and in one case from a leaflet.

### Clause two: a hundred years

*"A hundred years" is a defined term. It means "such period as the Committee shall find convenient."*

This is the most important sentence in the document, and for two thousand years nobody noticed it. It was found by Miss <a href="../People/Pargeter">Pargeter</a> of <a href="../Places/Frostwick">Frostwick</a>, who keeps the winter there under a contract of her own and reads contracts the way other people read weather. She went all the way through the Forty-Eighth's copy, which nobody had ever done, and turned back to clause two. The consequences are in [Two Reckonings](Two_Reckonings), which is a separate article because it needed one.

### Clause four

I do not know what clause four says. It appears on the back of the Committee's leaflet, *So You've Been Chosen*, which is one page long and says CONGRATULATIONS!, and then, in very much smaller print, CLAUSE FOUR APPLIES. I have asked every Chosen One I could reach what clause four was. All of them said that it applied.

### The companions clause

Elder <a href="../People/Elder_Wispel">Wispel</a> gives the charge to every Chosen One, and includes this: *take companions, up to three.* "That is the clause," he says, "not my preference." I have not been able to find its number. The Tower has a cupboard on its fourth floor with ten hooks in it, each with a small brass label: CHOSEN ONE, HEALER, SMITH, MAGE, SPECIALIST, KNIGHT, MUSICIAN, and two added later in a cheaper brass, RIVAL and CATALOGUER. The tenth reads GUEST, and is always empty. So the clause is at least well provided for.

### Clause seven: on foot

The Chosen One goes on foot. <a href="../People/Grimspite_the_Inevitable">Grimspite</a> himself explained why, when he was asked: "Because a thing that flies over and drops something is not a story. It is a Tuesday."

The town of <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a> has spent two hundred years building ships that fly to the Tower, and kept the ones that did not in the <a href="../Places/The_Long_Field">Long Field</a>. Grimspite was of the view that Miss <a href="../People/Ott_Hoyle">Hoyle</a> would never get one off the ground in sight of his tower, and that it had nothing whatever to do with her spars. Her two hundredth attempt landed beside the Tower that same year, with a Chosen One aboard. As far as I know nobody has mentioned this to him, and I do not intend to be the first, in an encyclopedia or out of one.

### Clause eleven: fit to travel

*A Chosen One is certified fit before departure.* It had been done forty-seven times. The Forty-Eighth's certificate was blank, because the Committee chose him while he was asleep, and nobody had examined a man who was asleep. <a href="../People/Merribell">Merribell</a> found the blank on a pad of works fitness forms in Upper Clanging and did it properly, on the road, with stamped scales borrowed from the fish stall at Nether Sopping, the light of a lit lighthouse, and a witness. He is certified fit to travel. He had, by then, travelled.

### Clause twelve: the renewal

<div class="stamp">THE DARK LORD SHALL RISE AGAIN.</div>

For two thousand years Thistlewick read this as a warning. It is not a warning. As Councillor <a href="../People/Councillor_Fenn">Fenn</a> of the Committee told his colleagues for years, to no effect: "That's not a warning. That's a subscription." Grimspite put it more shortly, and he had more right to: "It isn't a threat. It's a renewal notice."

Every time a Chosen One beat the Dark Lord, clause twelve renewed him, "before you reach the stairs". It renewed him forty-seven times. The sentence is carved forty-seven times into the wall behind his throne, and on the Obelisk in the Tower there is a list of forty-seven dates and names, each ending in the word RENEWED, with room left at the bottom for a forty-eighth. In the copy issued to the Forty-Eighth, clause twelve has been underlined by somebody worried.

The Committee struck it in 2000, *nem. con.*, and in its place wrote a note: SEE BRAM. Nobody has needed to yet.

### Clause fourteen: the first meeting

*The first meeting shall not go well.* I have this from <a href="../People/Merrow_Halgate">Merrow Halgate</a>, whose party of twenty - the best-equipped that ever went north - fought the Dark Lord at the bridge and lost, as the clause required, and as was written down in advance. He has made his peace with losing. He has not made his peace with the paperwork.

### Clause nineteen: the Resettlement Clause

*A returning Chosen One shall receive a pension, a cottage, and such distance from the village as is mutually agreeable.*

It was added later, somewhere about the Ninth, when Thistlewick found that a hero who comes home is awkward: he knows what is at the top of the Tower, and he asks at meetings why the Committee is already drafting the next one. <a href="../People/Halbert_Quy">Halbert Quy</a>, the Forty-Fifth, asked exactly that - "if it works every hundred years, why is there always a next one?" - and the cottage was ready by the Friday. The mutually agreeable distance turned out to be forty miles, which is how I come to be writing this in <a href="../Places/Nether_Sopping">Nether Sopping</a>, among most of the people in it. "They're not cruel," Mr Quy told the Forty-Eighth. "They're embarrassed."

It was applied to the Forty-Eighth the morning after he got home, by letter, on the mat: his pension, one quarter, in advance, with no mention of the next one, and the key to a cottage at Nether Sopping. *We trust Friday will suit.*

Six months later, after a very long meeting, the Committee struck clause nineteen as well. Forty miles south, a man in a garden read the notice twice and then went indoors.

<div class="marginalia">The meeting was eleven hours. I have read the minutes. They are, for the most part, about the biscuits.</div>

### Clause thirty-one: mechanism

<div class="stamp">THE DARK LORD SHALL NOT BE CONCLUDED BY MECHANISM.</div>

This is why ITEM 1, which had stood unticked in the <a class="new" href="../Institutions/The_Hoyle_Works">Hoyle Works</a>' stores book since 1802, did nothing whatever when it was finally set down in front of the throne, except make smoke and knock Grimspite's bookmark on to the floor. "Ordnance is not provided for," he said. "Nothing is provided for. That is what a renewal notice is."

## How it ended

The Forty-Eighth beat Grimspite, as forty-seven had before him. Clause twelve unrolled - the Prophecy itself, the width of the room, moving like something with an opinion - to renew him, and then, instead of renewing him, it turned to the party. It had found a party of the correct size, at the correct place, on the correct date.

> THE CHOSEN ONE SHALL BE CHOSEN. A NEW DARK LORD SHALL BE APPOINTED. THE ROLE IS VACANT. CONGRATULATIONS.

"Yes," said Grimspite. "That's how I got the job."

It was Grimspite who told the Forty-Eighth what to do. Not to kill it: to read it, out loud, all of it, because a contract that has been read in full has never once survived the experience. He read it. It came apart in the middle of a sentence. It did not burn or explode. It simply stopped being binding, which for a contract is the same as dying. "Four thousand eight hundred years," said Grimspite, "and it was an admin problem."

The Committee keeps it still, rolled, in Prophecy Hall, with two clauses struck. It is not binding on anybody. The Committee consults it anyway, at every meeting, out of habit, and minutes that it has been consulted.

<div class="disputed">

**Why the role was vacant this time.** Grimspite said clause twelve had renewed him forty-seven times before anybody reached the stairs. On the forty-eighth it declared the post vacant instead. Nobody has told me why, and I have three theories from three tables in the Wyvern, which I give without preference: that it was because he said *thank you*, which in forty-seven fights he had never said; that it was because the Forty-Eighth was still in the room, listening, which in forty-seven fights none of them had been; and that the clause had always said so and nobody had read that far down. Grimspite, asked by letter, replied that it was a very good question and that he had a kettle on.

</div>
