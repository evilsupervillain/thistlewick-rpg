<p class="lede">The <b>Flight of the Two Hundred</b> was the day in the year 2000 when the two hundredth airship of the Hoyle Works at <a href="../Places/Upper_Clanging">Upper Clanging</a> went up, and stayed up, and came down where it was meant to, beside the Obligatory Tower, two squares from the door. It is the only one of the two hundred that did. The works had been trying since about 1800. The attempt log gives the cause as under review, and will not say anything else until the review has found something.</p>

## Two hundred years

The Hoyle Works was begun by Miss Hoyle's great-grandmother to stop the Dark Lord, and has built an airship to do it, on and off, for two hundred years, under four generations of Hoyles. A hundred and ninety-seven of them came home to the Long Field, and two did not; and the town has turned the ornaments in its front rooms round to face the works, which is what it thinks of the works. Isaac Sowerby, who did sixty years on the rivets, has seen a hundred and ninety-nine go up and the same number come down, never in a hurry about it, mind.

> "It is the coming down that wants the work. She will get to it."

## What she wanted

The Two Hundred was finished, all but two things, when the Forty-Eighth walked into the drawing office in the year 2000. She wanted forty bolts of oilskin and a spar that would hold. The oilskin came from Mrs Barrow at Nether Sopping, who would not have forty, and was made to find forty, and Miss Hoyle counted all forty out loud in front of the Forty-Eighth, and did not hurry any of it, and said that nobody had ever brought her forty of anything. The spar came from Ollerenshaw's at the bottom of the hill, and was made in an afternoon, by two smiths, without a word, after which the two smiths went for a drink and the town lost its mind. It held. The tube plate for her boiler was made by four hands as well.

She was handed over to the Forty-Eighth with one instruction:

> "Do not set her down on anything with a roof on it. You would be astonished how often that has been the finding."

## The day

I have spoken to a good many people in Upper Clanging about the day she went up, and all of them were in the street, and none of them can agree what time it was. It was a working day, and nobody worked. The works' hooter was sounded once, which it is not supposed to be except for the shifts. She went up from the yard behind the drawing office, slowly, the way the others had gone up, and then she kept going, which the others had not, and the whole of the street cheered, except for Mr Sowerby, who was watching for the coming down.

She was seen over the Long Field, and over the stack where the Hundred and Ninety-Ninth is still on her crag, and over the Gloamwood. Then she was not seen, from Upper Clanging, and the town went to the Safety Valve to wait.

## The landing

She came down on the grass beside the Tower, ticking as she cooled, two squares from the door, on nothing with a roof on it. The company got off, and walked the two squares, because the Prophecy says in clause seven that the Chosen One shall come unto the Tower upon his own two feet, and somebody at the works had copied that clause onto the flyleaf of a ledger, in a hand a hundred and sixty years dead, with four generations' worth of notes under it in four inks, the last of them one word in pencil: *quite.*

The log reads:

<div class="stamp">199. LOST IN SIGHT OF THE TOWER. CAUSE: UNDER REVIEW.<br>
200. REACHED THE TOWER. LANDED ADJACENT. CAUSE: UNDER REVIEW.</div>

Two hundred years, and the one thing at the works that Miss Hoyle will not write down is the reason it worked. She does not write down anything she has not checked.

## The night

The Safety Valve's best china had stood on the dresser under a card, FOR WHEN SHE FLIES, for as long as anybody could remember, and had been dusted every week and never used. That night it was used, for the whole of the house. Miss Hoyle came down the hill, and drank one cup of tea out of it, standing up, and said that it would do, and went back up the hill. The china has been dusted since and put back, with a new card in front of the old one: FOR WHEN SHE FLIES AGAIN.

When word came up the valley that she had come down, beside the Tower, in her own time, Mr Sowerby said that it was very well done, in the voice other men keep for a rivet, and went back to his bench.

<div class="marginalia">At the top of the Tower, Grimspite the Inevitable was asked two questions on Miss Hoyle's behalf, and gave it as his view, in passing, that she would never get one off the ground in sight of his Tower. Nobody has told him that she did, and nobody has told her that he said so, and I have been asked by everyone concerned to leave it there, and I am leaving it there, in a book which I am aware they both may read. - H.B.</div>

## Since

The Two Hundred was put to the use of the Forty-Eighth and his company, and has since flown to a number of places that nobody can walk to. The Hoyle Works laid the keel of the Two Hundred and First in 2001. She is to land on the Tower. Not adjacent. On. She is on the slip. She is behind.
