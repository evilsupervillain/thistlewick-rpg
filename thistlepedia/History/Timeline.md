# Timeline

<p class="hatnote">All years are years of the Third Age, counted the way the sun does. Where the Prophecy counts otherwise, see <a href="Two_Reckonings">Two Reckonings</a>.</p>

<p class="lede">This is every date I have been able to pin, in order. A date I have from a headstone, a ledger or a plaque is given plainly. A date I have from somebody's memory is given as "about". Where I have nothing, I have left a gap, which in a timeline is more honest than it looks.</p>

<div class="marginalia">Several of these dates have been corrected by post, from the top of the Obligatory Tower, in a very beautiful hand. I have accepted all of the corrections. I have not been told the date of the correspondent's birth, and I have not printed one.</div>

## Before the Third Age

| When | What |
|---|---|
| The First Age | Olde Evermore is where it is. Thalion begins judging every arrival off the ledge, out of six. |
| The Second Age | Mirabel is born, about two thousand one hundred and thirteen years before the Prophecy. Elder Lirien and Elder Faelan marry. Calder begins fishing for the one fish in the pond. |
| Before the year 1 | The Dark Lord's engagement is held at the castle on the cloud, the Former Premises, so that no hero could ever reach it. None ever does. |

What began the Third Age is not known to me. It was not the Prophecy.

## The first nineteen centuries

| Year | What |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The Prophecy of Thistlewick is signed**, three times by everybody. A horse is drawn in the margin. The engagement moves to the Obligatory Tower, which has a door at the bottom, and the first hero to arrive gets the job. The caretaker of the Former Premises is retained. |
| 100s-900s | The first nine Chosen Ones, a real century apart. |
| About 900 | About the Ninth: Thistlewick finds a returning hero awkward, and writes clause nineteen. |
| 900s-1930s | The Tenth to the Fortieth, closer and closer together, always in a round year. |
| 1400 | The Collegium Arcanum is founded, by the Founder, in one afternoon. |
| 1500 | The First Dean's portrait is hung, pending the Hanging Committee. |
| 1702 | Weeping Bottom is founded by Mother Ursula Pym, for the sea air. Her Rule of Life is four lines long, and one of them is about socks. |
| 1749 | The Sky Anvil falls into Meech's top field. His great-great-grandfather makes it a gatepost. |
| 1750 | Pilgrims build a shrine round the gatepost. |
| 1788 | Great Dunnage seals its Counting House, "when the key was lost". J. Pocklington, Clerk to the Corporation, leaves behind the Door one chair and a note: WE SPENT IT. |
| 1794 | Sump: wasps in the well. The Water Committee is wound up. *Cursed 1794.* |
| 1795 | Sump: a general sense that things used to be better. The parish drafts Standing Orders for the Lifting of the Curse, and does not use them. |
| About 1800 | Nether Sopping begins throwing things down the Pit. |
| 1802 | The Hoyle Works begins its stores book. ITEM 1 is written into it. Bond No. 40, one cabbage, very large, "for the show", goes into the Bonded Warehouse at Great Dunnage. |
| 1804 | The Hoyle Works takes its first apprentice. |
| 1811 | Dame Honoria Pemberton-Gore's portrait is painted. |
| 1840 | Rollo, a dog, is painted. |
| 1841 | The clock at Upper Clanging goes four minutes fast. |
| 1860 | Attempt Eighty-Four comes down in the Long Field. |
| 1890 | Mabel Thistle, Bram's great-grandmother, is born. She grows a turnip as big as a chair. |

## The twentieth century

| Year | What |
|---|---|
| About 1900 | Attempt 112 comes down on her rock. |
| 1902 | Elspeth Moore is born. She will win the Thistlewick turnip show forty-one years running. The forty-second was fixed. |
| 1908 | A crate of ship's biscuit is stencilled for the store at Saint Bother's. |
| 1909 | The shipping stops. The Lighthouse of Saint Bother is kept lit for the ones coming home. |
| 1919 | <a href="The_Cold_Winter">The Cold Winter</a> at Upper Clanging: the foundry is banked down for six weeks in January and February, for want of coke. Edna Hargreaves, later Mrs Tunnicliffe, is born in June. In the autumn, the Class of 'Nineteen, two hundred and forty of them. Beatrice Grumnir is born. |
| About 1930 | The Thirty-Ninth sets out. Mr Ottershaw of Fenmarch Hall begins the animals. |
| 1931 | Mrs Pardew's gran makes her last jar of gooseberry jam at Sump, and labels it BETTER. |
| 1940 | **The Forty-First, Halloran Vance**, comes home from the marshes with nothing, and makes a wyvern out of a goose and wire in two days. Upper Clanging, which sets its watches by its clock, begins to be four minutes early for everything. |
| 1940s-1950s | The Forty-Second and the Forty-Third. |
| 1941 | Abel Thrush begins carrying the post between Little Gore, Fenmarch and Sump, every day, for fifty years. |
| 1949 | Corvin Ash's grandfather makes the black sword and cuts its name into his doorstep at Ashcombe, with MIND THE STEP under it for visitors. |
| 1959 | Wick, of Wick & Barrow, outfitters, of Nether Sopping, goes north, in the spring. His peg is still on the wall. |
| 1960 | **The Forty-Fourth, Ambrose Fitch.** Beatrice Grumnir makes a sword at the Thistlewick forge and sells it to a man going south. Perce Stagg breaks Frostwick's winter, and it thaws for <a href="The_Eleven_Days">eleven days</a>. The Adventurers' Guild is founded, provisionally. The Gloamwood Society is founded, to prove the Thing In The Woods is not there. |
| 1961 | Frostwick's first Winter Festival, on the twelfth. Perce Stagg cuts the ribbon, and marries Ida Frome the next day. |
| 1969 | Gideon Markup prices the Sword of Ultimate Ending. |
| 1970 | **The Forty-Fifth, Halbert Quy**, declines the tea. Dorcas and Prudence Thrupp stop speaking, and the <a href="The_Thrupp_Feud">Thrupp Feud</a> begins, by parcel. Tobias Frayne's first revival of eleven. Sir Aldric, sixteen, is due to keep his vigil, and keeps it in the wrong chapel. Mr Tinder, the parish's last fire person, retires, and the Cinder Fen is not burnt again for thirty years. Mr Cobley begins fishing for Old Tom. |
| 1971 | Edith Pellow sees the Thing In The Woods, and counts nine heads. |
| 1972 | K and M marry, and carve it into the driftwood on the beach at Nether Sopping. STILL is cut under it later. |
| 1974 | Walt Ashbury begins at the Eight Hours, Restwell. |
| 1977 | Roland Fairweather, aged eleven, is page to Dame Florence Ickworth against the Wyrm of Ickworth. |
| 1978 | Roland Fairweather is told that his prophecy is nearly ready. |
| 1979 | Mabel Thistle dies. |
| 1980 | **The Forty-Sixth** sits down in the Gloamwood. |
| 1981 | <a href="../People/Edwin_Tarrant">The Hermit</a> goes up the hill to find out whether he has any wisdom, and stays. |
| 1986 | Sir Hamo Tench comes to Weeping Bottom for the sea air. |
| 1987 | Bryd Ollerenshaw wins BEST IN HALL at the Smiths' Hall in Great Dunnage. Hob Grumnir does not enter, having a cold. The Committee begins meaning to water the plant in Prophecy Hall. |
| 1989 | A year of beginnings and endings. Corvin Ash, nineteen, sits down in the corner of the Slain Wyvern, and Ashcombe floods in his absence, on <a href="The_Day_of_the_Water">the Day of the Water</a>. Nix, fifteen, puts her gran's coat into bond until it fits, and starts the ledger. Attempt 199 is lost in sight of the Tower. Zephyrine Vance's <a href="The_Incident_at_the_Collegium">incident at the Collegium</a>, with the Dean, a duck and an unsanctioned quantity of fire. Beatrice Grumnir dies. |
| 1990 | **The Forty-Seventh**, the Old Man's boy, comes off the ledge at Olde Evermore and lands on his feet. |
| 1991 | Sir Aldric goes out errant, with an invitation to deliver to Little Gore. Abel Thrush dies, in the spring. |
| 1992 | Sir Aldric talks to the Wrong Dragon about Horace until dark. |
| 1993 | Maud Penhallow, nine, becomes Roland Fairweather's understudy. |
| 1994 | Elspeth Moore dies. <a href="../People/Perpetua_Small">Perpetua Small</a> wakes on the beach at Nether Sopping, with no memory and a headache. Sir Aldric reaches Thistlewick and cannot find the north gate. That winter his horse walks into a yard at Great Dunnage on her own, saddled. |
| 1996 | Mr Pilbeam rescues Princess Rosamund from the eagle. Merribell leaves the Sisterhouse at Weeping Bottom, because it has begun holding symposia. |
| 1997 | Much Pottering twins with Upper Clanging. Effie Gotobed and Spare Cotterill, both six, become pen pals. |
| About 1999 | Ambrose Fitch dies, in his cottage at Nether Sopping. The masons come within a fortnight. |

## The year 2000

| When | What |
|---|---|
| The last of the winter | **The Forty-Eighth, Bram Thistle**, is chosen in his sleep. [The Obligatory Quest](The_Obligatory_Quest), from the bed to the throne room. The Prophecy is read in full and stops being binding. Grimspite retires. |
| The same winter | On the way, and because of it: <a href="The_Flight_of_the_Two_Hundred">the Two Hundred flies</a>, and lands adjacent. A jar goes south and a reply comes north, and <a href="The_Thrupp_Feud">the Thrupp sisters</a> write to each other. A duck is walked four miles, and the Collegium Arcanum is quorate for the first time since <a href="The_Incident_at_the_Collegium">the incident</a>. Corvin Ash speaks at the Flood Dinner. |
| The next morning | There are still turnips. The letter on the mat: clause nineteen, a quarter's pension, a key. *We trust Friday will suit.* Clause twelve is struck, and SEE BRAM written in its place. |
| Spring | Washer, the tenth Cotterill, is born at Upper Clanging at six in the morning, on the hooter. Clevis Cotterill's book pays twenty to one. |
| That year | Bram walks to Nether Sopping and tells Halbert Quy everything Grimspite said, including the part about the tea. My history is completed, and the last page filled. |
| Autumn | Clause nineteen is struck, after a meeting of eleven hours. |
| Winter | The forty-eighth frame on the Wall of the Forty-Seven still reads PORTRAIT TO FOLLOW. SITTER AT THE COAST. |

## Since

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 2001 | Merribell addresses the Healers' Table, for eleven minutes, on water. It is the shortest address in the Table's history, and is minuted as the most useful. |
| 2001 | The Gloamwood Society's annual walk, three hundred and twelve strong. Nothing is found. The Society minutes it with satisfaction. |
| 2001 | Ott Hoyle lays the keel of the Two Hundred and First. |
| 2001 | Corvin Ash is asked back to the Flood Dinner at Ashcombe, and goes, and is early. |
| 9 June 2001 | Sir Aldric finds the north gate of Thistlewick, unaided. He has not found it since. |
| 2001 | Hob Grumnir and Bryd Ollerenshaw judge the Smiths' Hall prize together, and disagree for nine hours. |
| Harvest 2001 | Bram's first full crop since the Quest, and the best in the parish. The Committee asks whether it was prophesied. It was not. |
| 21 March 2002 | The first day of spring, which Spare Cotterill has confirmed by letter. I sign this volume. |
