<p class="lede">There are <b>two reckonings</b> of time in the world, and the history of the Obligatory Quest is written in both of them at once, usually in the same sentence. The <i>calendar</i> is the elves' Third Age, in which this is the year 2002. The <i>contract</i> is the Prophecy's, in which a hundred years is whatever the Committee says it is. By the one, the Obligatory Tower is about two thousand years old. By the other it is four thousand eight hundred. Both are correct, and it took a woman in Frostwick with her gloves on to find out why.</p>

## The calendar

The years in this book, and in most books, are years of the **Third Age**, which is the elves' count. <a href="../Places/Olde_Evermore">Olde Evermore</a> counts in Ages, and has been where it is since the First; <a class="new" href="../People/Mirabel">Mirabel</a> has been making her prophecy about biscuits since the Second. The Hoyle Works' stores book was begun in 1802, Weeping Bottom was founded in 1702, the Collegium Arcanum in 1400, and the Forty-Eighth set out in 2000. Those are all the same kind of year, a year of the sun, and they add up.

Thistlewick signed the Prophecy in the first year of the Third Age, and has said ever since that the Age began with it. It did not. What did begin it, I do not know, and Elder <a class="new" href="../People/Elder_Faelan">Faelan</a> would only tell me that the Age began with a great many things and the Prophecy was the one with a clerk present. I have not been able to find out what the others were. I suspect that nobody has ever asked, and I suspect that is the reason.

## The contract

The Prophecy counts in years of its own. Its clause two defines a hundred years as *such period as the Committee shall find convenient*, and every figure that comes from the Prophecy's side of things - from the Tower, from the Dark Lord, from the people who are paid to look after him - is a figure of that kind. These are some of them:

| What | Says | Means |
|---|---|---|
| The Dark Lord rises | Every hundred years | Whenever the Committee found it convenient. Latterly, every ten |
| The plaque in the Tower | Est. approx. 4,800 years ago, UNDER THE SAME MANAGEMENT | Forty-eight Committee centuries, give or take the first few |
| Grimspite's tenure | Four thousand eight hundred years, this job | The same |
| The Contingency Cache, fourth floor | RESTOCKED EVERY CENTURY | Every Chosen One. Nobody has ever opened it. It is six feet from the stairs |
| The wayshrine's visitors' book | The last entry, "Feeling good about this one!", is dated a hundred years ago | The Forty-Seventh's, 1990 |
| The Former Premises | Two thousand years of Dark Lords, and nineteen thousand two hundred quarterly reports | Contract years again: four a year, for four thousand eight hundred |

The calendar and the contract ran together at first, while the Committee still found a hundred years convenient, for the first nine Chosen Ones or so. Then it began finding shorter periods convenient, and then shorter still, and always in a round year. By the end the Forty-First went in 1940 and the last five went ten years apart, which is how the Forty-Fifth, a man who set out *three centuries* before the Forty-Eighth, is in his seventies and potting things on his kitchen table forty miles from here.

## Who notices

It depends, very largely, on how far you live from the Committee. I have made something of a study of it, from a table in the Slain Wyvern, which is forty miles away and is where the people who noticed were sent.

**In Thistlewick nobody notices.** The village holds both reckonings at once, comfortably, like a man with a pie in each hand. The Old Man's boy was the Forty-Seventh. A regular in the Gilded Turnip will tell you his cousin was the Thirty-Ninth. They went a hundred years apart. Nobody in the village is over a hundred. I have put all of this to nobody in Thistlewick, because I like it there and should like to go back.

**In Nether Sopping, everybody noticed, which is why they are in Nether Sopping.** <a href="../People/Halbert_Quy">Halbert Quy</a> asked at a meeting why, if it worked every hundred years, there was always a next one, and the cottage was ready by the Friday. Down here we talk in plain years, sixty and forty and thirty, and we do not make a point of it. We do not have to.

**Upper Clanging believes the text.** The Hoyle Works read "every hundred years" two hundred years ago and made an engineering programme of it, and has not counted since, because the document had done the counting for it. Noticing grows with distance, I find, but it is not guaranteed by it, and engineers who take a document literally are their own case.

**In Frostwick, far to the north, there is one person who reads contracts**, Miss <a href="../People/Pargeter">Pargeter</a>, who keeps the winter there under a contract of her own - nine years, renewed on time. She read the Prophecy through, all the way, and found clause two, and read it out. She does not do the arithmetic for you, and does not see why she should.

**The elves simply tell you.** Elder Faelan told the Forty-Eighth that the Forty-Seventh came off the ledge ten years ago, a lovely boy, and that the Committee will have told him it was a hundred. "It counts the way the Prophecy does. We count the way the sun does."

**And the Prophecy's own people do not notice either**, because to them there is nothing to notice. The Tower is four thousand eight hundred years old. They have the paperwork.

<div class="marginalia">I wrote to Grimspite, after the Quest, to ask whether he had ever thought it strange that his Tower should be four thousand eight hundred years old in a world where the oldest dated thing in the Collegium is six hundred. He wrote back, in a very beautiful hand, that the Tower was four thousand eight hundred years old, that he had been there for all of it and would know, and that he had the paperwork, if I should like to see it. He has also corrected three of my dates in Volume Two, all of them correctly. I asked him, in the same letter, how old he was. He replied that he had held the post for four thousand eight hundred years, and that how old he had been when he took it was his own affair, and that he hoped I was keeping well.</div>

## Reading the two together

The rule I have used in this book is the one the world uses without meaning to. **A date is a calendar year** unless it comes from the Prophecy, the Tower or the Former Premises, and "N years ago" means two thousand less N, counted from the Quest. **A span the Prophecy gives is in contract years**, and should not be added to anything. Where the two disagree I have printed both. Where they agree, which is mostly in the first nine centuries, I have been grateful.
