# The Southern Half

A design for the optional half of *The Obligatory Quest*: everything you can
walk to that the Prophecy does not require you to.

The main quest is a straight line north - village, world map, wood, tower - and
it is finished and it works. This expansion adds a *south*: a second town, four
more places you can walk into, seven side-quests, three more companions, and
about two dozen people with something to say. None of it is on the way to the
Dark Lord. That is the point. The road north is the plot; the road south is the
world the plot is happening in.

Read this first. `CLAUDE.md` is the reference; this is the intent.

---

## 1. The lore this hangs on

The existing joke is that the Prophecy is a *contract*, and Grimspite the
Inevitable is four thousand eight hundred years into a renewable engagement he
cannot get out of. Everything below is built on one new question, which the
finished game never asks:

> Forty-seven Chosen Ones went north and Thistlewick says it worked forty-seven
> times. Where are they?

The answer is **Nether Sopping**.

### The Resettlement Clause

Prophecy of Thistlewick, clause nineteen. A Chosen One who returns is granted a
pension, a cottage and "such distance from the village as is mutually
agreeable". Thistlewick discovered, somewhere around number nine, that a
returning hero is an *awkward* thing to have about the place: he knows what is
up the tower, he says so at meetings, and he asks why the Committee is already
drafting the next prophecy. So the village pensions its heroes south, to the
coast, at a generous rate, and does not write to them.

Which means the entire adventuring economy of the world has silently pooled in
one wet fishing town on the south coast: the retired, their surviving party
members, the ones who went north for someone else's prophecy, the ones who never
went at all, and the trade that grew up to sell things to all of them.

Thistlewick believes it is the centre of the story. Nether Sopping is where the
story's *cast* actually lives, and it is forty minutes' walk away, and nobody
from Thistlewick has ever been.

### What this adds to the ending

Three concrete payoffs, all optional, all in the finale:

* **Halbert Quy**, Chosen One #45, retired, gardening, tells Bram the one thing
  nobody has ever done: *ask Grimspite why he doesn't stop*. That is already the
  first choice the player gets in the throne room. If the player has spoken to
  Quy, Grimspite notices where the question came from, and the answer is
  different, and better.
* **The memorial** on the Sopping harbour front lists every name that went
  north, not just the Chosen Ones. Thistlewick's Wall of the Forty-Seven has
  forty-seven portraits. The memorial has a hundred and eighty-one names. Nobody
  in Thistlewick has counted the difference.
* **The tally**: the ending already reads back how many clichés Bram walked
  into. It will now also read back whether the song got written, whether the
  bench got delivered, and whether the guest character was, as always,
  unavailable for the final dungeon.

### The running gag of the south

Thistlewick's comedy is *rural bureaucracy*. Nether Sopping's is **the
adventuring industry**: a guild that is provisional, a bounty board, an outfitter
who sells you a hat because a form requires a hat, a tourist attraction with an
admission fee, and a tavern where every cliché in the genre is somebody's
retirement anecdote and they are all still slightly annoyed about it.

## 2. The places

### Map 13 - Nether Sopping (town)

South coast, at the end of a coast road running east from Thistlewick. Sea along
the south and east, a shingle beach, a stone harbour with a pier, and a town
that is mostly one street of tall wet buildings. Displayed name: *Nether
Sopping*. The town sign reads **NETHER SOPPING - 'THE SEA IS RIGHT THERE'**.

On the map: the harbour memorial, a ferryman whose tide is never right, two
children playing Chosen One (the loser has to be the Dark Lord and nobody wants
to be the Dark Lord), a fish stall, a man asleep in a boat who has been
adventuring for thirty years and is having a day off, and four doors.

### Map 14 - The Slain Wyvern (tavern)

The reason for all of this. A long room, a big fire, a stuffed wyvern over the
mantel, and eleven people who have all been on a quest.

The wyvern is a goose. Everybody knows the wyvern is a goose. Chosen One #41
needed to bring something back, and he was a good man, and Dorcas will say "it
is a wyvern" in a tone that ends the conversation. If **Wren Halloway** is in
the party she identifies it as a goose, correctly, at length, and is asked to
leave. *(This is the only bit of the game that is meaner to a party member for
being right.)*

Who is in there - the six **tales**, which are the lore payload, each a
different genre cliché told as a retirement anecdote:

| who | face | the tale | the trope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ysolde Marrow** | People1 7 | healer to the Forty-Fourth. Walked a duke's son from the coast to the tower "for the exposure". He had four hit points and walked into everything. Nine weeks. | the escort mission |
| **Hulda Bole** | People2 1 | four hundred and six errands in eleven years. Twelve bear pelts, then a *different* sort of bear, and then the man had moved. | the fetch-quest chain |
| **Nabb and Tolly** | People4 7 / People2 5 | they were #46's party. He sat down in the Gloamwood and said the woods were enough. They said "fair". They were tired. They still write to him; he does not write back; the letters are collected by a man on a log. | the party that quits |
| **Merrow Halgate** | People3 6 | fought Grimspite at the bridge and lost. They were *meant* to lose - it was written down in advance. He has never forgiven whoever wrote it. Also: Grimspite apologised. | the unwinnable scripted fight |
| **Councillor Dree** | People3 5 | the kindly old advisor who was accused of secretly being the Dark Lord for eleven weeks by his own party. He was not. He was just kindly and old and an advisor. | the twist villain |
| **Perpetua Small** | People3 3 | remembers nothing before six years ago. Is wearing a tiara. Everyone in the tavern knows. Nobody has told her, because the way she works it out is going to be *tremendous*. | amnesia + secret royalty |

Also in the room, not tale-tellers: **Dorcas Thrupp** (landlady, beds 25cr,
recipient of the Feud), **Hosea Bellwether** (scribbler, buying the tales),
**Two Very Ordinary Travellers** (People3 0 and 1, in crowns, insisting they are
peasants), **Sops** the potboy, and the three recruitables.

### Map 15 - The Adventurers' Guild (Provisional)

One room, one counter, one **Registrar Pell**, forty years provisional. The
bounty board is here. Registration is Form A-1, and Form A-1 has been lost, and
it is under the mat, which is a callback the tower has already made and which
Pell finds no funnier the second time.

Pell also holds **Form C-12(S)**: the southern half of the roster amendment, so
Corvin, Wren and Roland can be dismissed here the way the Thistlewick clerk
dismisses the original six. (Six is the practical maximum for one Show Choices
list, and splitting the form across two towns is funnier than a submenu.)

### Map 16 - Wick & Barrow, Outfitters

Mrs Barrow. There is no Wick. The sign is worth more than Wick was.

Sells the southern goods: ship's biscuit, chowder, Sopping Pale, oilskins, lamp
oil, a crossbow, a recurve bow, a black sword called Grudge, **a hat** (30cr,
required by a form), and **a bench, flat-packed** (400cr) which nobody has ever
bought and which is the last thing the expansion needs.

### Map 17 - Number Forty-Five's Cottage

**Halbert Quy**, Chosen One #45, aged seventy-one, in a garden. Roses. He hates
turnips with a clarity that has taken decades. He explains the Resettlement
Clause, the pension, and the letters Thistlewick does not send - and then, on the
way out, says the thing that changes the finale.

### Map 18 - The Lighthouse of Saint Bother

A world-map landmark east of Sopping on a spit of rock. **Ferrety Bother**, who
keeps a light for a sea nobody sails, because his great-grandmother kept it, and
her reason was better: the light is what the ones who come back walk home by.
Forty-seven times the light has been lit for somebody. It has been lit for
nobody rather more often than that.

Quest: he is out of oil.

### Map 19 - The Bottomless Pit

A world-map cave mouth in the western hills, with a hut, a rope and a turnstile.
**Fenwick Splint** charges 2cr admission. The pit is four feet deep. It is where
Nether Sopping has thrown everything for two hundred years, and something has
moved into it, and the something is a badger, and the badger is enormous and has
been eating the lost property.

At the bottom: two centuries of the town's mislaid belongings, a genuinely good
accessory, a great many odd socks, and a sign reading **BOTTOMLESS (APPROX.)**.

### Map 20 - The Barrow of the Forty-Fourth

A sealed mound in the eastern hills, which is a Scheduled Monument and therefore
requires a Guild Card to enter, which is the single most petty gate in the game.

**Ambrose Fitch**, Chosen One #44, came home, was pensioned south, and died old
and ignored. Forty years later Thistlewick - which had not written to him once -
built him a heroic barrow with grave goods, a hound and a curse on it, *because
that is what you do with a hero*. He is still in it, standing in the middle of a
circle somebody drew for him. He will fight you, because that is what is done.

He is the hardest fight in the game after the two at the top of the tower, and
he is entirely optional, and he stops the moment it is over and sits down on the
floor of his own burial chamber, which the shape of the room does not allow for.

Beat him and he stops, and thanks you, and asks for a bench. He would like to
sit down. He would like somewhere for people to sit.

**The Bench** is the last side-quest in the game: buy the flat-packed bench from
Mrs Barrow, put it on the barrow, and go and tell Ysolde Marrow - who was his
healer, and who is the reason this whole town exists in the design - that it is
done.

### World-map curiosities (no new maps)

* **The Standing Stones of Uncertain Purpose** - four stones. The plaque lists
  six competing theories and notes that the sixth was proposed by a man who was
  standing right there when they were put up, in his lifetime, and is being
  ignored on principle.
* **The Hermit of the Middle Distance** - lives alone, looks meaningfully at the
  horizon, gives cryptic advice. The advice is a shopping list. He would like
  someone to go to the shops.
* **The Crab of Unusual Size** - bounty, on the south beach.
* **Meredith Crooke, Bandit Chief** - bounty, at a camp in the west. Runs the
  six-week banditry apprenticeship the trainee bandits keep mentioning. Has a
  syllabus.
* A southern signpost, and a memorial bench that is *not* the bench.

## 3. The three new companions

All three are in the tavern. All three respect the party limit of four, and say
something different if you are full.

### Corvin Ash - "Doomed Rival" (Actor1 face 4)

Sits in the dark, apart from everyone, hood up, one enormous scar, speaks
entirely in ominous fragments about a destiny he has been waiting for since he
was nineteen. Nobody has ever asked him to join anything. When you do, he says
yes immediately, and then tries to make that sound reluctant.

His tragic backstory: his village was destroyed. By flooding. In his absence.
Everyone was fine. They moved eight miles inland and are doing well. He has
never been able to get anyone to understand why this still ruins him.

**Mechanically**: a glass cannon that gets stronger the worse things are going.
Dark element, high ATK and AGI, terrible DEF. `Old Wound` damages him to fill
his TP; `Prophesied Rival` scales on his own missing HP; `Brood` buffs his attack
and lowers his defence and restores nothing. The first party member who is
actively bad for you.

### Wren Halloway - "Cataloguer" (Actor2 face 6)

Green cloak, crossbow, seventeen specimen jars, and a monograph in progress. She
does not fight monsters, she *documents* them; the fighting is a regrettable
step in the methodology. Taxonomy is a moral position. She will tell you what
the wyvern is.

**Mechanically**: multi-hit ranged physical, status infliction (poison, slow),
armour-shredding debuffs, and the party ability that doubles item drops - she
picks things up because they are *specimens*.

### Roland Fairweather - "Guest Star" (Actor2 face 2)

Magnificent. Glowing. Extremely good at everything. Joins at level nine when the
party is around six, has a heal *and* a nuke *and* a party buff, and is
obviously the protagonist of a much more expensive game.

He is contractually unavailable for the final dungeon.

Everybody in the tavern warns you. The Guild has his file. He warns you himself,
twice, with genuine regret. And then at the door of the Obligatory Tower he gets
the look of a man remembering an appointment, gives you his sword - which is
better than anything else in the game at that point - and goes, and the party is
one smaller for the last act.

Taking him is a real decision, and it should be: he makes the middle of the game
much easier and the end of it slightly harder, and he is funny, and he is
genuinely fond of you, and he still leaves.

The two delivery legs of quest 1 are exactly what was asked for: something from
your village that has to go to theirs, and something of theirs that has to come
back. The two sisters have not spoken in thirty years and communicate solely by
sending each other increasingly indefensible gifts. They love each other very
much. Bram is the postal service and gets shouted at twice.

## 6. Changes to what already exists

* **World map (Map 8)**: the continent grows a south-east bulge, a coast road
  east from Thistlewick to Nether Sopping, a shingle beach with its own encounter
  region, and eight new landmarks and events.
* **The tower door (Map 8, event 4)**: Roland's departure happens here, before
  the choice to go in, so a player who has just lost a party member can walk back
  and get another one.
* **The Contingency Cache (Map 11)**: two more hooks, in a different hand, and a
  ninth hook that is empty and labelled GUEST.
* **The Gilded Turnip (Map 4)**: the innkeeper is now named Prudence Thrupp and
  has a sister and a jar.
* **Prophecy Hall (Map 3)**: Elder Wispel writes character references; the clerk
  explains that southern personnel are a different form.
* **The Gloamwood (Map 9)**: the man on the log is #46, and now collects post.
* **The finale (Map 12)**: Grimspite reacts to the question if Quy sent it, and
  the ending reads back the song, the bench and the guest who left.
* **`story.py`**: `roster_amendment()` takes the list it amends, so the same
  machinery serves both clerks.

## 7. How it gets verified

Same as the rest of the game: screenshots for maps, `balance.py` for the new
fights, `validate.py` after every build, and scenarios for anything with state.
New scenarios:

| scenario | what it proves |
| --- | --- |
| `south_road` | the coast road reaches Nether Sopping and the town door works |
| `tavern` | all six tales count, and each one counts as a cliché |
| `tavern_history` | Hosea asks for six and pays out at six; the wyvern is a goose |
| `recruit_south` | Corvin and Wren join; a full party is turned down |
| `guest_leaves` | Roland joins, and is gone by the time the tower opens |
| `feud` | the jar goes south, the reply comes north, the locket arrives |
| `guild` | reference plus hat plus the mat equals a Guild Card |
| `barrow` | the card opens the mound, the Forty-Fourth is beaten, the bench is delivered and Ysolde answers |
| `reachable_sopping` | the tavern, the guild, the shop and the cottage, walked to with the arrow keys |

The last one matters most: everything here is optional, which means every single
event in it is one the player has to be able to *find*, and an unreachable
optional event is one nobody will ever report as a bug.

Three things that came out of actually running these, and are worth not
rediscovering:

* **Props on a street are a wall.** `validate.py` floods the map and only
  complains when an event has no reachable neighbour at all, so a barrel in the
  middle of the high street passes it - the doors are still reachable, by
  detour. `reachable_sopping` walks the streets in a straight line, which is
  what caught the four things sitting in them.
* **Wait after a `goto`.** A scenario that transfers and then presses a key
  immediately loses the key press to the fade-in. Every `goto` in a walking
  scenario wants a `{"frames": 30}` behind it.
* **New terrain reaches further than you meant it to.** The beach is painted by
  scanning for the lowest land rows rather than by hand, which is what makes it
  follow the coast - and it found the two rows outside Thistlewick's gate,
  where the old game's first scenario asserts you are standing in the gentle
  half of the world. The sand looks right there and stayed; the encounter
  region now starts two tiles east, once the coast road is clear of the
  village. The old `gate` scenario is the only thing that noticed, which is the
  argument for re-running all of them after an expansion, not just the new ones.
