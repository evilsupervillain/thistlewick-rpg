# How the Prophecy works

Pieced together from the game's own text and design docs. A reference, not a
design document: it describes what the game currently says and changes nothing.

## What the Prophecy is

It's a contract, not a prediction. The Prophecy of Thistlewick is a scroll full
of numbered clauses. It was signed in Year 1 of the Third Age, "three times by
everybody". It sets up a standing engagement: there is a Dark Lord in the
Obligatory Tower, and Thistlewick sends a Chosen One to deal with him. The
clauses you meet in play:

- **Clause two**: "a hundred years" means *such period as the Committee shall
  find convenient*. Pargeter reads this out in Frostwick, the only place it's
  said.
- **Clause seven**: the Chosen One goes on foot. Grimspite: "a thing that flies
  over and drops something is not a story. It is a Tuesday."
- **Clause twelve**: "THE DARK LORD SHALL RISE AGAIN." Grimspite: "It isn't a
  threat. It's a renewal notice."
- **Clause nineteen**, the Resettlement Clause: a returning hero gets a pension,
  a cottage and "such distance from the village as is mutually agreeable".
- **Clause thirty-one**: "THE DARK LORD SHALL NOT BE CONCLUDED BY MECHANISM."
  This is why ITEM 1, the bomb, does nothing.

## Before it existed

There's a prehistory at the Former Premises, the castle on the floating
continent. For "two thousand years of Dark Lords" the venue was up there "so
that no hero could ever reach it", and none ever did. After complaints about the
climb, the engagement moved into a tower with "a door at the bottom". That move
happened when the Prophecy was signed, 4,800 contract years ago. The first hero
ever to arrive got the job, and that was Grimspite. Hollowell, the caretaker,
was retained and has kept the old place ready "in case it's re-let".

## One cycle

1. **The Committee picks a moment.** "Every hundred years" is in contract years,
   so in practice it's whenever the Committee finds it convenient, and lately
   always a round year. The Chosen Ones went out in 1940, 1960, 1970, 1980, 1990
   and 2000, so the recent gap is ten years. That's why #45 is still alive in
   his seventies.
2. **Someone is chosen.** Bram was picked by the Prophecy Committee while he was
   asleep. Elder Wispel gives the charge: up to three companions ("that is the
   clause, not my preference"), go north, kill the Dark Lord, come home.
3. **The hero climbs the tower and fights Grimspite.** Every time he says "so,
   you have come at last" ("I meant it the first time"). Quy: "We all did the
   sword. Not one of us ever asked the man why he doesn't stop."
4. **Clause twelve renews him.** Once he's down, the scroll unrolls and puts him
   back. Grimspite: "It has renewed me forty-seven times. It will do it before
   you reach the stairs." Each renewal is a dated line on the Obelisk in the
   tower that ends in RENEWED, and there's room for a forty-eighth. The sentence
   is carved forty-seven times on the wall behind the throne.
5. **The hero goes home and Thistlewick says it worked.** Wispel: "It has worked
   forty-seven times." Asked what happened the other times, he says the same
   thing again. The village never notices that if it worked, there shouldn't be
   a next one. In the timeline doc's words, that gap is "the joke".

## What happens to the heroes afterwards

This is the south's whole premise (`EXPANSION.md` §1). Around Chosen One #9,
Thistlewick found that a returning hero is awkward. He knows what is up the
tower, says so at meetings, and asks why the Committee is already drafting the
next prophecy. So it wrote clause nineteen. Heroes are pensioned off about forty
miles south to Nether Sopping and never written to.

- Halbert Quy (#45) asked "if it works every hundred years, why is there always
  a next one?" and "the cottage was ready by the Friday."
- Ambrose Fitch (#44) spent forty years in his pension cottage, ignored, and
  died around 1999.
- #46 stopped in the Gloamwood and never went up the tower.
- #47 was the Old Man's boy.
- Sopping's memorial lists 181 names: everyone who went north, not just the 47
  Chosen Ones.

## How Bram's cycle ends differently

If the player has met Quy, Bram does the one thing nobody has done: he asks
Grimspite why he doesn't stop. The answer is clause twelve.

After the fight, Grimspite says "...Thank you." The Prophecy unrolls, renews
nothing, and turns to the party: "A NEW DARK LORD SHALL BE APPOINTED. THE ROLE
IS VACANT. CONGRATULATIONS." Grimspite: "That's how I got the job." He then
tells Bram not to kill it but to read it, all of it, out loud, because "a
contract that has been read in full has never once survived the experience."
That reading is the last boss fight. The Prophecy "simply stops being binding,
which for a contract is the same as dying." Grimspite: "Four thousand eight
hundred years and it was an admin problem."

What follows:

- Grimspite signs Bram a Receipt ("in case anyone asks whose fault this was").
- His parting line: "There isn't a forty-nine."
- Thistlewick strikes clause twelve and writes "SEE BRAM" in its place.
- If Bram met Quy, the village strikes clause nineteen six months later, and
  Bram walks south to tell Quy that the tea was just tea.
- The post-game picks up the next morning: Grimspite in his chair with a kettle,
  the pension letter, and Fitch's cottage.

## Two gaps

- **Why the role is vacant this time.** By Grimspite's account, clause twelve
  has always renewed him "before you reach the stairs". This time the Prophecy
  declares the job vacant instead. Bram's party is "of the correct size, at the
  correct place, on the correct date", and that's also how Grimspite got the
  job. Nothing says why the earlier 47 weren't offered it, or what decides
  renewal versus appointment. It could be his "Thank you", or because this hero
  is still in the room. The game leaves it unsaid, which may be on purpose.
- **The count of 47.** #46 never reached the tower, yet Grimspite says he has
  been renewed and has said "you have come at last" forty-seven times. Quy says
  "forty-seven of us… we all did the sword", and Grimspite's own line is
  "Forty-seven of you". Ott counts forty-six, which `AUDIT.md` treats as
  deliberate, but the throne-room lines may be off by one. I haven't checked
  whether there's a ruling on this.
